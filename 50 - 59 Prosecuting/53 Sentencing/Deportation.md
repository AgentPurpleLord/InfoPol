

Deportation *is relevant* when sentencing, but *only* if it will *make the burden of imprisonment more onerous* or *may result in the offender losing the opportunity to settle permanently in Australia.* [Konamala v The Queen [2016] VSCA 48 - BarNet Jade](https://jade.io/article/459082)

The court *should not* consider the *possibility* of deportation as a mitigatory factor unless it will *actually be a hardship* for the offender. [DPP (Cth) v Peng [2014] VSCA 128 - BarNet Jade](https://jade.io/article/336914)

Deportation should be given limited weight in cases where the offending is serious. [Fichtner v The Queen [2019] VSCA 297 - BarNet Jade](https://jade.io/article/694816)

There must also be *evidence* of the *likely risk of deportation* and the *impact of that risk*. [Guden](https://jade.io/article/196988) [[25]–[27]](https://jade.io/article/196988/section/3553). See also [Hatzis v The Queen](https://jade.io/article/789900) [2021] VSCA 43, [[28]](https://jade.io/article/789900/section/140558)

A reduction will be permissible and appropriate where the prospect of deportation (and its impact) are certain. Ibid [[28]](https://jade.io/article/789900/section/140558); [R v Kwon](https://jade.io/article/137813) [2004] NSWCCA 456.

There must be evidence or a prosecution concession, otherwise, the court need not consider the possibility of deportation.

Where the is unusual circumstances demonstrating a connection to Australia (e.g. lengthy period of residency before commission of offence; offenders family has made financial sacrifices to send offender to Australia), the court should consider these matters. [Valayamkandathil](https://jade.io/article/202460) v The Queen [[2010] VSCA 260](https://jade.io/article/202460), [[28]](https://jade.io/article/202460/section/140558); [Ankur v The Queen](https://jade.io/article/804929) [2021] VSCA 110, [[117]](https://jade.io/article/804929/section/2218). See also [Matamata v The Queen](https://jade.io/article/834511) [2021] VSCA 253.

The mandatory visa cancellation provisions for child sex offences (ss 501(3A)(a)(ii) and ss 501(6)(e)(i)) *do not* remove the prospect of deportation as a proper matter for consideration at sentence.
