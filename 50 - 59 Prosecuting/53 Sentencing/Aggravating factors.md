# Overview
In sentencing, there will always be *aggravating factors* and *mitigating factors* which will assist the court in determining the most appropriate sentence.
Aggravating factors could be *anything*, but some of them include:
- Planning and scale of offending (e.g. premeditation)
- Intent and knowledge of the accused in relation to the offending
- Role and status (e.g. co-offenders, ring leader, etc.)
- Unprovoked attacks or violence
- Hate crimes
- Impact on and circumstances of the victim (e.g. vulnerability)
- Harm (direct injury, loss or damage)
- The use of weapons
- Benefit gained by the accused at a loss of a victim (e.g. trafficking or financial crimes)
- Circumstances of family violence

>In the present case, the appellant had the right to remain silent. Had he done so, his silence could not have been regarded as an aggravating circumstance of the offence. Rather than remain silent, however, through his lies the appellant sought actively to mislead the police, and to deflect their attention from him. The appellant did not merely fail to admit his offending. Instead, he told deliberate lies that went beyond a simple denial of responsibility, those untruths, as we have said, being calculated to divert attention away from his criminal conduct.
