# Purpose
The Sex Offenders Registration Act provides it's purpose as Section 1, which is summarised as:

> Require sexual offenders to keep police informed of their whereabouts and other personal details for a period of time.

And the above is done for the following reasons:
- to reduce the likelihood that they will re-offender.
- to facilitate the investigation and prosecuiton of any offences they commit.
- to prevent RSO's from working in child-related employment.


# Applications to place a person onto the Sex Offenders Register

- The application occurs **after** sentencing.
- The court cannot consider the punitive aspect of them being placed onto the SOR in sentencing.


> **WHO IS A REGISTERABLE OFFENDER?**
>
> [Section 6 SORA](https://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/vic/consol_act/sora2004292/s6.html) is the legislation.
>
> Any person who has been sentenced for a registerable offence.
>
> [Class 1 offences are found in Schedule 1](https://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/vic/consol_act/sora2004292/sch1.html) - serious sex offences against child - penetrative.
>
> [Class 2 offences Schedule 2](https://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/vic/consol_act/sora2004292/sch2.html) - sexual offence against a child (non penetrative)
>
> Class 3 or 4 offence or any offence; an offence that results in the making of a sex offender registration order.

| ---------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| Adult Offender         | Any offence except not a Class 1 or 2 offence |
| Juvenile Offender      | Any offence                            


## Class 3 & 4 Offences
Class 3 offences are *discretionary*
- Person who is a serious sexual offender; penetrative offences against an adult. 

Class 4 offences are *discretionary*
- Person who is a serious sexual offender; Person who commits a sexual offences against an adult (non-penetrative offences against an adult).

A **serious sexual offender** is a person who has been sentenced for 2 or more offences listed in a Schedule in the Act s. 8(3) SORA.

# Common Offences Prosecuted

The most common offence you will see on a brief is **failing to comply with reporting obligations**.

These can only be **non-authorised** by an Inspector or above.


## Proof of failing to comply with reporting obligations

[Section 73 of the SORA](https://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/vic/consol_act/sora2004292/s73.html) e.g. a certificate that without evidence to the contrary, is considered proof.
