# Adults - Discretionary Registration

Adult discretionary applications occur when the accused has been sentenced to **any offence** other than a Class 1 or Class 2 offence.

The application **must be made within 45 days** of sentence.


The court must be satisfied beyond reasonable doubt that the accused **poses a [risk to the sexual safety of one or more persons of the community](<Risk to the sexual safety of others.md>)**, see s 11(3) of SORA.


# Procedural Matters

- VP Form 1142
- Verbal application made to the court.
- Requires submissions by the prosecutor to satisfy s 11(3) SORA
- May lead/call evidence to support application. 
	- Psych reports; or
	- Information from ROI; or
	- Witness statement, something said at the time, etc.


# Bowden v The Queen and Sayer v The Queen

- Bowden v The Queen and Sayer v The Queen are the relevant discretionary points of analysis:
	- Whether to make a registration order is a two-stage process.
	- First; is the court satisfied beyond a reasonable doubt that the person poses a risk to sexual safety as defined in the act.
		- The risk must be real.
		- Evaluation of the risk is when the offender is *released* not at the time of sentence.
	- Second; if satisfied BORG on risk, whether order should be made in all relevant circumstances.
		- Balancing the identified risk, having regard to the purpose of the Act, with restrictions imposed on offenders *right to enjoy freedom* and *autonomy of action*.
		- Consider the magnitude and nature of risk, degree of likelihood of the risk eventuating, gravity of harm, to be balanced against serious consequences for the offender.

# Juveniles - Discretionary Application
Found guilty of any offence; and
Prosecution must make an application; and
Court has discretion for all offences committed by juveniles; and
All reporting periods are half that of adult offenders with a maximum of 7 and a half years.
