- Case preparation is necessary to be an effective and persuasive prosecutor. 
- Convincing people that your argument is the best one.
- Mastering the brief of evidence.
- Knowledge of the law.
- Understanding the rules of evidence.

# How To Analyse A Brief
1. Assess Charges
2. Assess Exhibits & Witnesses (Statements)
3. Read Summary of Facts
4. Consider Correspondence b/w Parties & Disclosure
5. Create Chronology of Events
6. Draft Closing Address
7. Consider any submissions of law
8. Craft Examination of Witnesses
9. Consider Cross-Examination & Defence Arguments
10. Draft Opening Address
11. Review Document Management

# Step 1 - Charge
- Assess the charge-sheet, read the particulars.
- Charge will tell you what needs to be proved.
- If charge is deficient or left unamended, Prosecution of case jeopardised. 
- Look up the provision for the offence, cross-check all elements are included in the body of the charge.
- Check judicial college notes and all elements are appropriate.
- Research any law that may assist in proving the case.
- Ensure the correct offence provision and correct date is alleged.
- Elements of the offence must be at forefront of the prosecution mind when analysing the brief.
- Remember the duplicity rule, multiple acts within one charge is wrong. They should be individual charges.

# Step 2 - Exhibits & Statements
- Read the exhibits and the statements / witnesses.
- You must assess the prospects of reasonable conviction.
- After reading the charge, we know what MUST be proven (each element).
- Cases are proved by witnesses, not by the prosecutor. Witnesses offer facts, a Prosecutor is responsible for eliciting those facts from the witnesses.
- Analysing these will highlight deficiencies in the case. You can then advise the informant on the way forward.
- When analysing a witness statement:
	- Not only evidence that may be missing, may be material which is vague or unsatisfactory (could be a line of attack against PROS)

## Step 3 - Read the Summary of Facts
- SOAF is not evidence, not admissible under the Evidence Act. It can be helpful, but it can be misleading.
- E.g. may say that there are three punches, but statement may only say that there is one.
- If you follow the order, you will identify these issues early.
- SOAF may taint your view of the evidence, strength of the case, before you conduct a proper analysis.

## Step 4 - Consider Disclosure & Correspondence
- This means whatever defence has already stated to prosecutions or informant thus far and their take on the case.
- An example of being distracted by correspondence:
	- You have sex matter, pick up brief, email from defence saying comp unreliable, cannot make out element 3, etc.
	- May say that comp consented to the touching.
- Reading this before assessing the evidence, it may taint your mind.
- Do not be wholly guided by what your opponent is suggesting before considering the brief.
- Best considered after own independent, comprehensive assessment.

## Step 5 - Chronology of Events
- Briefs with lots of material helps a lot.
- Greatly assists with committing the brief to memory.
	- This is important, on your feet before the Magi, you can respond to any questions from the bench at a moments notice.
- Identifies the strengths and weaknesses of the case.
	- e.g. prosecuting sex assault, comp on brief says that male sex assaulted in front of revs. CCTV of footage that shows accused leaving at 4:30am. No footage of sex assault. Witness who is best friends with comp. Says she witnesses sex assault. 
	- Breaking this down into a timeline will crystallising what gaps there are in the case
	- Try and identify inconsistencies and issues, important to keep at the front of your mind.
- This is the best point to formulate a case theory. A succinct document you can refer to.

## Step 6 - Closing Address
- Most important step in preparing a contested hearing.
- Highlights precisely what evidence you want to illicit from each witness. What is admissible what is not, etc.
- Precise wording changes as evidence comes out.
- Important to pre-prepare one before you start.
- Lights up what each witness wants.
- Drafting this helps to figure out what evidence-in-chief questions you will have.
- How is difficult, no one way. Encourage everyone to have own style.
- Suggested way:
	- Write out the elements.
	- What evidence PROS has, after evidence if given in proof of each element.
	- Write down key points in a heading form, as to why offences proven beyond reasonable doubt.
	- Under each heading, note what evidence is relevant, why it shows court must be compelled to finding charge beyond reasonable doubt.
	- Step back, reflect on key points. Reflect on why persuasive. 
- Note down what your opponent will bring up, and why that doesn't counteract the evidence.
- Most easily understood closing, is the most persuasive.
- [Leave under s.73A of CPA for closing statement.](http://www8.austlii.edu.au/cgi-bin/viewdoc/au/legis/vic/consol_act/cpa2009188/s73.html) -> leave must be sought for Prosecutor to make closing address to sum up the evidence.

## Step 7 - Submissions on the Law
- May be submissions on law that you must prepare, depending on admissibility of evidence / rules of evidence.
- Prosecution may need to draft tendency notice, etc.

# Step 8 - Examination of Witnesses
- Is it a FV?
- Is it a child comp?
- Responsibility on prosecutor to consider circumstances which evidence may be adduced, through VARE / BWC, etc. Responsibility must be on Prosecutor to give evidence pursuant to CPA for remote witness facility.
- You have remarkable privilege to ask personal questions in public which witnesses must answer. Be mindful of this. For us its work, but for a witness it is an experience they will never forget in their life. Potential to have devastating impact.
- Witness must be at ease with the process.
- **Golden Rule:** Elicit from each witness, only what you require for your closing statement. 
	- Hours of pointless examination will annoy magistrate, prolong proceeding, leave witness feeling uncomfortable, etc.
- Remember Section 37 (Leading Questions)
- Group questions into topics in a chronological way. Write out questions, note down expected answer. 
	- Questions short. 
	- Seek one fact at a time. 
	- Only ask questions you know the answer to.
- Listen to their answers during the examination in chief. Defence and Magistrates' will notice repeated questions. 
- Cross-check EIC with closing statement. Ensure covered off on all matters with all witnesses.
- Structure questions with Who, What, Where, When, Why, How
- Take witness through evidence chronologically. Telling a story chronologically, the Magistrate is more likely to comprehend where things are going. It is inherently more persuasive.
- Remember Section 32 (attempting to revive memory)
	- If statement made at the time, and the witness forgets something important, you can rely upon this section if statement made at the time

# Step 9 - Consider Defence Case + Cross Examination
- Only elicit what you need from cross-examination, nothing more/less. Don't do it for the sake of it. Must be structured.
- Questions must highlight to Magistrate you are heading, what direction you are taking.
- Cross-examination is usually used to show a witness is being inaccurate.
- Use simple English.
- Comply with Browne v. Dunn rule.

# Step 10 - Opening Statement by Prosecution
- How case put by prosecution; and
- Identify issues in dispute; and
- Witnesses called and evidence they will give.
- Good way to signal you have mastered brief and know what's going to happen. Try and feel trusted by the Bench to find your argument persuasive.
- Section 65 provides power to grant leave to let parties deliver an opening. Strongly encourage prosecutors to do so.

# Document Management
- Paperwork in order at all times. Incredibly important. 
- Ought to be able to readily access critical material in a brief.
- May be a question put from the bench, you ought to be able to respond quickly.
- Nothing worse then Magistrate asking question and Advocate having to fumble through material. 
- Brief be tabbed, material in proper order.
- Prepare a separate folder, Prosecution Materials
	- Chronology at the front
	- closing
	- opening
	- examination in chief
	- cross examination
	- elements of offence
	- cases to refer to during course of hearing

# Conference Witnesses
- Meet with witnesses before giving evidence, explain your role and put them at ease before giving evidence.
- More positively engaged with the process.
- Always have the informant with you. They can take notes and raise disclosure issues.
- Less likely to make Prosecutor a witness or allegations of coaching.
- Ask whether they ever given evidence before.
- Show them court room, show them evidence from, etc. Tour.
- Tell witness who you are what you do, what your role is.
