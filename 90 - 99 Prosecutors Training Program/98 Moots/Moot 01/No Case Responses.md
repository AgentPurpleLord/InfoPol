# If No Evidence At All
- May v O'Sullivan (1955) 92 CLR 654.
- Not ought to be convicted, whether he *could* be lawfully convicted.
- Evidence from informant satisfies charge elements; certainly the accused could be convicted on this.

# If Evidence Weak
- Doney v R (1990) 171 CLR 207
- Even if there is weak evidence it should be left open to the jury for its decision.
- Evidence is not tenuous, evidence satisfies charge elements, accused has a case to answer.

# If Competing Inferences / reasonable doubt
It is a question for the jury; Attorney-General's Reference No 1 of 1983 2 VR 410; jury may assess reasonable doubt, does not mean accused does not have a case to answer.
