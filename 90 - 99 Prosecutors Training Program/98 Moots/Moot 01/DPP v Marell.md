## Proceeds of crime
- DPP v Marell [2005] VSC 430
_dealing in property reasonably suspected to be proceeds of crime — whether prosecution must prove beyond reasonable doubt that property was proceeds of crime_
Was the prosecution required to prove beyond reasonable doubt that the property was the proceeds of crime?
The appellant submitted that the magistrate misdirected himself in holding that to prove a charge of dealing in property reasonably suspected of being proceeds of crime contrary to the [Crimes Act 1958 (Vic), s 195](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U5&docFamilyGuid=Ib3e001f8035011e99495db3043f758b0&pubNum=1100190&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&docVersion=Law+in+Force&ppcid=0fa77a12de4d4f2b8f067fe4fcd9be31&contextData=\(sc.DocLink\)), the prosecution must prove beyond reasonable doubt that the goods were ‘proceeds of crime’ as defined in s 193.

Held: (allowing the appeal)
- (1) [Section 195](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U7&docFamilyGuid=Ib3e001f8035011e99495db3043f758b0&pubNum=1100190&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&docVersion=Law+in+Force&ppcid=0fa77a12de4d4f2b8f067fe4fcd9be31&contextData=\(sc.DocLink\)) does not impose on the prosecution any burden of establishing beyond reasonable doubt that the relevant property is the proceeds of crime as defined in s 193(1).
- (2) In holding that [s 195](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U7&docFamilyGuid=Ib3e001f8035011e99495db3043f758b0&pubNum=1100190&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&docVersion=Law+in+Force&ppcid=0fa77a12de4d4f2b8f067fe4fcd9be31&contextData=\(sc.DocLink\)) imposed that requirement, and in dismissing charges upon that basis, the magistrate fell into an error of law in the present case.

Para 24: It was not disputed that the language of s.195 of the Crimes Act did not require the prosecution to prove that the property in question was the proceeds of crime.  Rather, the prosecution was required to prove only that there were reasonable grounds to suspect that the property was the proceeds of crime.  



Paragraph 38: I conclude, on the basis of the plain meaning of the terminology used, the coherent relationship inter se of [ss 193](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U7&docFamilyGuid=Ib3e001fc035011e99495db3043f758b0&pubNum=1100190&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&docVersion=Law+in+Force&ppcid=0fa77a12de4d4f2b8f067fe4fcd9be31&contextData=\(sc.DocLink\)), [194](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U7&docFamilyGuid=Ib3e00202035011e99495db3043f758b0&pubNum=1100190&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&docVersion=Law+in+Force&ppcid=0fa77a12de4d4f2b8f067fe4fcd9be31&contextData=\(sc.DocLink\)) and [195 of the Crimes Act](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U7&docFamilyGuid=Ib3e001f8035011e99495db3043f758b0&pubNum=1100190&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&docVersion=Law+in+Force&ppcid=0fa77a12de4d4f2b8f067fe4fcd9be31&contextData=\(sc.DocLink\)), the statements in the relevant Second Reading Speech and Explanatory Memorandum and the established construction of previous, broadly equivalent statutory provisions, that [s 195 of the Crimes Act](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U7&docFamilyGuid=Ib3e001f8035011e99495db3043f758b0&pubNum=1100190&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&docVersion=Law+in+Force&ppcid=0fa77a12de4d4f2b8f067fe4fcd9be31&contextData=\(sc.DocLink\)) does not impose on the prosecution any burden of establishing beyond reasonable doubt that the relevant property is the proceeds of crime as defined in [s 193(1)](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U7&docFamilyGuid=Ib3e001fc035011e99495db3043f758b0&pubNum=1100190&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&docVersion=Law+in+Force&ppcid=0fa77a12de4d4f2b8f067fe4fcd9be31&contextData=\(sc.DocLink\))

44: It is apparent from the transcript of the hearing below that his Honour relied, in that context, on the evidence of Ms Witty, a manager of a clothing store, who identified the jeans by their barcodes as items stolen from her store in the course of a burglary in August. The theft was verified by reference to a stock take.

53: I am not satisfied that the learned magistrate's dismissal of charge 6 was predicated on an erroneous imposition on the prosecution of the onus of negating the defence. It is, in any event, unnecessary to decide that question. While it appears that on the better view, [s 195](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U7&docFamilyGuid=Ib3e001f8035011e99495db3043f758b0&pubNum=1100190&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&docVersion=Law+in+Force&ppcid=0fa77a12de4d4f2b8f067fe4fcd9be31&contextData=\(sc.DocLink\)) imposes on the prosecution the onus of excluding the defence of honest and reasonable mistake of fact, in the present case, an alleged error of law on that basis is not a ground of appeal.

Explanatory Memorandum for the Crimes (Money Laundering) Bill
Dealing with property suspected of being proceeds of crime New [section 195](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U7&docFamilyGuid=Ib3e001f8035011e99495db3043f758b0&pubNum=1100190&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&docVersion=Law+in+Force&ppcid=0fa77a12de4d4f2b8f067fe4fcd9be31&contextData=\(sc.DocLink\)) creates a summary offence for dealing with property if there are reasonable grounds to suspect that it is proceeds of crime. This replaces the existing offence in [section 123 of the Confiscation Act 1997](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U5&docFamilyGuid=I3a351962035611e99495db3043f758b0&pubNum=1100190&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&docVersion=Law+in+Force&ppcid=0fa77a12de4d4f2b8f067fe4fcd9be31&contextData=\(sc.DocLink\)). The offence is a strict liability offence. Accordingly, it is intended that the common law defence of honest and reasonable mistake of fact should apply.”
