### Introduction

The case involves three matters (Fox, Bant, and Nunn) that raise similar issues regarding the adequacy of charges for summary offences under the CPA. The applicants argued that the charges were invalid due to insufficient particulars and that they could not be amended after the limitation period had expired.

### Legislative Framework
The court examined the relevant provisions of the CPA, particularly:
- **Section 6**: Commencement of criminal proceedings by filing a charge-sheet.
- **Schedule 1, Clause 1**: Requirements for charges to state the offence and provide necessary particulars.
- **Section 8**: Power to amend charge-sheets.

### Key Issues and Reasoning
### Validity of Charge-Sheets (Paragraphs 12-15)
The court noted that under **Section 9(1)**, a charge-sheet is not invalid merely because it fails to comply with Schedule 1. This provision aims to preserve the jurisdiction of the court despite minor defects in the charge-sheet.

### Power to Amend Charge-Sheets (Paragraphs 16-31)
The court emphasized the broad power of amendment under **Section 8**:
- **Section 8(1)** allows amendments at any time unless it causes injustice to the accused.
- **Section 8(3)** and **8(4)** impose limitations on amendments after the expiry of the limitation period, ensuring that amendments do not amount to charging a new offence and that the original charge sufficiently disclosed the nature of the offence.

### Specific Cases
1. **Fox v Director of Public Prosecutions (Vic)**
    - **Factual Background**: Fox was charged with speeding, but the charge misdescribed the location of the offence.
    - **Court's Reasoning**: The court held that the charge could be amended to correct the location without causing injustice or charging a new offence. The original charge sufficiently disclosed the nature of the offence (Paragraphs 121-144).

### Conclusion
The court concluded that the charges in all three cases could be amended under Section 8 of the CPA. The amendments were necessary to correct defects and ensure that the charges provided reasonable information about the nature of the offences without causing injustice to the accused or circumventing the limitation period.

### Key Quotes
- "A charge-sheet that contains a defect — even one that could, at common law, have led to invalidity of a charge — is not a nullity" (Paragraph 30).
- "The power to amend a charge-sheet must permit the amendment of a charge contained on the charge-sheet to cure a defect that might at common law have rendered the charge invalid" (Paragraph 23).
