Hi all,

Welcome to the course #117 SCC/CME moots.

Attached you’ll find the brief that is the subject of the assessment tomorrow. Do yourself a favour and don’t analyse it during your time away from the course.

The images and supporting docs are A.I. generated and they support the complainant’s version of events. There is some artistic license to them so don’t analyse them too much, just take it on face value that they support the prosecution case.

Below is the email from the defence practitioner to you and sets out the salient points they wish to agitate.

Also, turn your minds to charge elements and what evidence you have; what you need and what you should have.

Don’t make a decision in a vacuum. If you don’t have a specific piece of evidence that would exonerate the accused at SCC, then it should be a plea to the substantive charge(s) or off to CME.

Remember the criteria for withdrawal of charges; no reasonable prospect of conviction, or not in the public interest.

Come to a bottom line. This is what defence is after so they can get back to their client.

Tomorrow morning you’ll have a bit of time in the morning to form a view of the brief. The please reply to this email with your counter offer (if you disagree with defence).

We’ll the conduct the in person SCC assessment and (provided we disagree) fill out a Form 11A to progress the matter to CME. If you can pre-populate the form with some of the information (names, informant, etc) it’ll speed things up.

At the conclusion of the SCC you’ll be asked what material if any you would ask the informant to request. Material will then be provided to you for the anticipated CME moot.

You’ll then have some time to prepare for the CME while we rotate through the SCCs and then the CMEs.

The CME moot will be recorded and usual court rules and etiquette apply (make your appearance, stand when required etc).

You may be asked how the prosecution intends to prove (insert element of a charge here). You’ll then be expected to point to some evidence and articulate how it supports the prosecution position.

If your matter does not resolve at the CME you’ll then be asked to complete a Form12 with the practitioner.  The Form12 forms part of the assessment so that should give a clue as to where the matter is ultimately headed.

Charge 2: Commit Indictable Offence Whilst On Bail
- Can have discussion with my supervisor and discuss potential WD of this charge; offence has since been repealed.
- Hot issues 2024; may rely upon rebuttal that:
	- Section 14 of the Interpretation of Legislation Act 1984 clearly states that the repeal of an  offence does not operate retrospectively unless the contrary intention expressly appears,  which it does not.
	- It cannot be an abuse of process to prosecute for an offence that Parliament has determined  continues to apply with respect to conduct that occurred prior to its repeal.
	- Insofar as the prosecution has exercised its discretion to charge and continue the prosecution, it falls within the prosecutorial discretion and is generally unreviewable. This legal basis for discretion provides a solid foundation for decision-making. As it cannot be reviewed on non-Charter grounds, it is also unreviewable on Charter grounds (see Section 39 of the Charter).
