## Consistency and Fairness
In [_Commissioner for Railways (NSW) v Agalianos_ (1955) 92 CLR 390](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U2&serNum=1955040181&pubNum=0003586&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&ppcid=75e0a459fdc44cf2a2e46cf55b9544ff&contextData=(sc.Category)) Dixon CJ said (at 397):
> The context, the general purpose of a provision and its consistency and fairness are surer guides to its meaning than the logic with which it is constructed.

## Don't make a fortress out of the dictionary
In [_Cabell v Markham_ 148 F 2d 737](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?findType=Y&serNum=1945113873&pubNum=0000350&originatingDoc=I01553ac40b3011ea83b0ac48dfa2554a&refType=RP&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&ppcid=75e0a459fdc44cf2a2e46cf55b9544ff&contextData=(sc.Category)) (2nd Circ, 1945) Learned Hand J said (at 739):
> Of course it is true that the words used, even in their literal sense, are the primary, and most ordinarily the most reliable, source of interpreting the meaning of any writing: be it a statute, a contract, or anything else. But it is one of the surest indexes of a mature and developed jurisprudence not to make a fortress out of the dictionary; but to remember that statutes always have some purpose or object to accomplish, whose sympathetic and imaginative discovery is the surest guide to their meaning

## Avoid unjust or irrational reasoning
In [_Public Transport Commission (NSW) v Murray More (NSW) Pty Ltd_ (1975) 132 CLR 336](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U2&serNum=1975144802&pubNum=0003586&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&ppcid=75e0a459fdc44cf2a2e46cf55b9544ff&contextData=(sc.Category)) Gibbs J said (at 350):
> [W]here two meanings are open … it is proper to adopt the meaning that will avoid consequences that appear irrational and unjust.

## Avoiding Absurd Results of Interpretation
In [_DPP v Serratore_ (1995) 38 NSWLR 137; 81 A Crim R 363; 132 ALR 461](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U2&serNum=1995388118&pubNum=0005138&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&ppcid=75e0a459fdc44cf2a2e46cf55b9544ff&contextData=(sc.Category)) (CCA) Kirby P said (at 146; 470; 373):

> The avoidance of absurd, incongruous and highly inconvenient results in legislation is a function of the courts, derived from the presumption that parliament would not intend such results; cf _Bermingham v Corrective Services Commission of New South Wales_ [(1988) 15 NSWLR 292](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U2&serNum=1988281626&pubNum=0005138&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&ppcid=75e0a459fdc44cf2a2e46cf55b9544ff&contextData=(sc.Category)) ([CA](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U5&docFamilyGuid=I504f8337ee8111e8af46ddc380776190&pubNum=1100190&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&docVersion=Law+in+Force&ppcid=75e0a459fdc44cf2a2e46cf55b9544ff&contextData=(sc.Category))) at 299. The modern approach to the construction of legislation is to endeavour to avoid such outcomes. In [_Kingston v Keprose Pty Ltd_ (1987) 11 NSWLR 404](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U2&serNum=2000722712&pubNum=0005138&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&ppcid=75e0a459fdc44cf2a2e46cf55b9544ff&contextData=(sc.Category)) ([CA](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U5&docFamilyGuid=I504f8337ee8111e8af46ddc380776190&pubNum=1100190&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&docVersion=Law+in+Force&ppcid=75e0a459fdc44cf2a2e46cf55b9544ff&contextData=(sc.Category))) at 424, McHugh JA, in an often quoted explanation of the purposive approach to construction of statutes, cited Lord Diplock's injunction:
> 
> > if … the courts can identify the target of parliamentary legislation their proper function is to see that it is hit: not merely to record that it has been missed: “The Courts as Legislators”, _The Lawyer and Justice_ (Sweet & Maxwell) (1978) p 274.
