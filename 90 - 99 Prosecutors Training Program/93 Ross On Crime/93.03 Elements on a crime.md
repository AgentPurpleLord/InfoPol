# Causation
## Refusing Medical Attention
The tort law principle that people must take their victims as they find them may also be relevant here. In [_R v Blaue_ [1975] 1 WLR 1411; [1975] 3 All ER 446](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?findType=Y&serNum=1975027048&pubNum=0004891&originatingDoc=If87b39b60b3c11ea83b0ac48dfa2554a&refType=IC&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&ppcid=5d0a6be857a747c7aa5247258bb2c2be&contextData=(sc.Default))[199](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Document/If87b39b60b3c11ea83b0ac48dfa2554a/View/FullText.html?originationContext=typeAhead&transitionType=Default&contextData=(sc.Default)&comp=wlau#co_footnote_199) the victim who had been stabbed by the accused was a Jehovah's Witness and refused to have a blood transfusion which would probably have saved her life. The Court of Appeal held that the victim's refusal to have a transfusion did not break the chain of causation:[200](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Document/If87b39b60b3c11ea83b0ac48dfa2554a/View/FullText.html?originationContext=typeAhead&transitionType=Default&contextData=(sc.Default)&comp=wlau#co_footnote_200)

> It has long been the policy of the law that those who use violence on other people must take their victims as they find them. This in our judgment means the whole man, not just the physical man. It does not lie in the mouth of the assailant to say that his victim's religious beliefs which inhibited him from accepting certain kinds of treatment were unreasonable. The question for decision is what caused her death. The answer is the stab wound. The fact that the victim refused to stop this end coming about did not break the causal connection between the act and death.
