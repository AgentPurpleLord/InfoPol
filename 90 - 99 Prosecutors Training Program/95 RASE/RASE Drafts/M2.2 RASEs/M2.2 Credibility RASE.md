# Question
You are prosecuting a contested hearing in relation to charges of drug trafficking. It is now time for you to cross-examine the accused, Jasmyn ST JOHN. Before you begin your cross-examination, you recall that defence cross-examined one of the prosecution witnesses, Nelda WESTBROOK, about a prior conviction for theft and she admitted it. The cross examination of WESTBROOK’s prior conviction did not relate to the drug trafficking charge that is subject of the contested hearing.
When you cross-examine the accused, are you able to question the accused about her prior convictions for theft and other dishonesty offences?
Prepare a submission for the court (IRAC format preferred).

# Issue
Can we cross-examine/question the accused about her prior convictions?

# Rule
101A Credibility evidence
Credibility evidence, in relation to a witness or other person, is evidence relevant to the credibility of the witness or person that—
(a) is relevant only because it affects the assessment of the credibility of the witness or person; or
(b) is relevant—
(i) because it affects the assessment of the credibility of the witness or person; and
(ii) for some other purpose for which it is not admissible, or cannot be used, because of a provision of Parts 3.2 to 3.6.

102 The credibility rule
Credibility evidence about a witness is not admissible.

103 Exception—cross-examination as to credibility
(1) The credibility rule does not apply to evidence adduced in cross-examination of a witness if the evidence could substantially affect the assessment of the credibility of the witness.

104 Further protections—cross-examination as to credibility
(1) This section applies only to credibility evidence in a criminal proceeding and so applies in addition to section 103.
(2) An accused must not be cross-examined about a matter that is relevant to the assessment of the credibility of the accused, unless the court gives leave.
(3) Despite subsection (2), leave is not required for cross-examination by the prosecutor about whether the accused—
(a) is biased or has a motive to be untruthful; or
(b) is, or was, unable to be aware of or recall matters to which his or her evidence relates; or
(c) has made a prior inconsistent statement.
(4) Leave must not be given for cross-examination by the prosecutor under subsection (2) unless evidence adduced by the accused has been admitted that—
(a) tends to prove that a witness called by the prosecutor has a tendency to be untruthful; and
(b) is relevant solely or mainly to the witness's credibility.
(5) A reference in subsection (4) to evidence does not include a reference to evidence of conduct in relation to—
(a) the events in relation to which the accused is being prosecuted; or
(b) the investigation of the offence for which the accused is being prosecuted.
(6) Leave is not to be given for cross-examination by another accused unless—
(a) the evidence that the accused to be cross-examined has given includes evidence adverse to the accused seeking leave to cross-examine; and
(b) that evidence has been admitted.


# Application
In this case, I am seeking to adduce the accused's prior convictions. This is considered to be credibility evidence (s. 101A) and it is only relevant to the accused's credibility, as it does not relate to the drug trafficking charge (s. 101A(a)).

Credibility evidence is not usually admissible (s. 102), but in this case, there is an exception to this. The exception has been brought on by the accused questioning WESTBROOK's own credibility by introducing her prior convictions during cross-examination. Your Honour was satisfied that WESTBROOK's priors could substantially affect the assessment of her credibility. The accused's own priors could also substantially affect the assessment of the accused's credibility (s. 103((1)).

In considering this substantial affect, Your Honour must have regard (s. 103(2)) that the accused's priors are for dishonesty offences. This tends to prove that the accused has made previous false representations (s. 103(2)(a)).  

There are further protections granted to the accused in these circumstances under s. 104 EA (as this matter is a criminal proceeding). 

Prosecution are seeking leave of the court to be able to cross-examine the accused (s. 104(2)), about her previous convictions. By the accused questioning WESTBROOK's credibility (s. 104(4)), they have adduce evidence tending to prove that WESTBROOK's credibility is questionable (s. 104(4)(a)). WESTBROOK's prior convictions had no relevance beyond her credibility (s. 104(4)(b)).



# Conclusion
Upon leave being granted by the court, yes, this evidence can be brought out during cross-examination of the accused.
