You are prosecuting a contested hearing where the accused, a teenage girl, is charged with : 
1. Intentionally Causing Injury 
2. Recklessly Causing Injury 
3. Unlawful Assault. 

It is alleged that she approached a classmate, V, after school in a lane way and slashed her arm twice with a kitchen knife. V received two lacerations to her forearm approximately 5 cm in length. V made a statement to police detailing the assault. V identifies the accused and states that the accused yelled just prior to slashing her arm: 
“You better stop talking about me. I haven’t been using drugs and ditching school. If you don’t stop next time I will cut you deeper!” 
One week later the accused was speaking with a friend, F. F mentions the assault on V and the accused states: “Well if you tell lies about people you get what you deserve” 
F asks the accused: “What has she been saying? 
The accused replies: “Spreading lies about people using drugs and ditching school.” 
During the running of the contested hearing, you call F to give evidence of the conversation with the accused. Defence object stating the entire conversation is hearsay. The Magistrate asks you how they can hear the evidence from F. Prepare a submission in response (IRAC format preferred). NB: When you are in court if the defence object to hearsay evidence being adduced, you must consider whether the evidence is hearsay first. If you determine that it is then you must consider if there is an exception that would allow the evidence to be admitted. For the purpose of this submission you have conceded that the evidence is hearsay.

# Issue
How can the Magistrate hear the evidence from F?

# Rule
81 Hearsay and opinion rules—exception for admissions and related representations
(1) The hearsay rule and the opinion rule do not apply to evidence of an admission.
(2) The hearsay rule and the opinion rule do not apply to evidence of a previous representation—
(a) that was made in relation to an admission at the time the admission was made, or shortly before or after that time; and
(b) to which it is reasonably necessary to refer in order to understand the admission.

82 Exclusion of evidence of admissions that is not first-hand
Section 81 does not prevent the application of the hearsay rule to evidence of an admission unless—
(a) it is given by a person who saw, heard or otherwise perceived the admission being made; or
(b) it is a document in which the admission is made.

Evidence Act Definitions
"*admission*" means a previous representation that is—
(a) made by a person who is or becomes a party to a proceeding (including an accused in a criminal proceeding); and
(b) adverse to the person's interest in the outcome of the proceeding

"previous representation" means:
1. An express or implied representation (oral/writing)
2. A representation which can be inferred from someone's conduct.
3. Representation that is not to be seen or heard by others (e.g. journal)
4. Representation that is not to be communicated (e.g. unsent letter)

**Note:** A representation otherwise made in the course of giving evidence in the proceeding in which evidence of the representation is sought to  be adduced. 

"representation" includes—
(a) an express or implied representation (whether oral or in writing); or
(b) a representation to be inferred from conduct; or
(c) a representation not intended by its maker to be communicated to or seen by another person; or
(d) a representation that for any reason is not communicated;

Section 88 Evidence Act 2008
For the purpose of determining whether evidence of an admission is admissible, the court is to find that a particular person made the admission if it is reasonably open to find that he or she made the admission.

# Application
It is conceded that F's evidence is hearsay evidence.

s. 81(1): F's evidence of the accused stating,  "Well if you tell lies about people you get what you deserve", is considered an admissions and therefore an exception exists.

Evidence Act - Admission: The accused has made an admission to F, as the accused is a party in this proceeding and her words (being a previous representation) adversely affected her interests in the outcome of it. An inference can be drawn from her wording that the reasons for her assaulting the victim, was due to the victim having previous lied.

Evidence Act - Previous Representation: In this case, the evidence of these admissions are previous representations, as they were made outside of this proceeding (accused spoke to F).

Evidence Act - Representation: In this case, the accused's words to F are representations. They are express or implied representations which were given orally (spoken). 

s. 81(2): F's evidence in relation to the accused stating "Spreading lies about people using drugs and ditching school," has an alternative exception. It is evidence of a previous representation, it was made shortly after F's admission (a) and it provides clarifying context which is reasonably necessary to understand F's admissions (b). 

s. 82(a): F is giving evidence of a conversation that she had herself with the accused. It would be considered first-hand hearsay of an admissions made by the accused.

s. 88: F is a friend of the accused. It is reasonably open to the court to find that the accused is the person that made this admission, as that is F's evidence currently.

# Conclusion
The evidence should be admitted, as an exception exists to both statements made by the accused, as they are admissions or a previous representation giving the necessary context to the admission shortly after it was made.
