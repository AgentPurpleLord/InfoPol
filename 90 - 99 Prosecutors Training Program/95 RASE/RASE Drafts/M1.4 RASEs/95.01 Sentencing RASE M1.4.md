## Issue
What is a 'within range' sentence for the accused in this matter, taking into account all of the relevant sentencing principles?

## Rule
<u>Summary Offences Act 1966 - Section 23 - Common Assault</u>
Any person who unlawfully assaults or beats another person shall be guilty of an offence.
**Penalty: 15 penalty units or imprisonment for three months.**

<u>Crimes Act 1958 - Section 195H - Affray</u>
(1)     A person who uses or threatens unlawful violence and whose conduct would cause a person of reasonable firmness present at the scene to be terrified commits an offence and is liable to—
	(a)     **level 6 imprisonment (5 years maximum); or**
	(b)     imprisonment for 7 years if, at the time of committing the offence, the person is wearing a face covering used primarily—
		(i)     to conceal the person's identity; or
		(ii)     to protect the person from the effects of a crowd-controlling substance.
(2)     A person is guilty of an offence under subsection (1) only if the person intends to use or threaten violence or is reckless as to whether the person's conduct involves the use of violence or threatens violence.
(3)     For the purposes of subsection (1), a threat of unlawful violence must involve more than words alone.
(4)     An offence under subsection (1) may be committed—
	(a)     in private as well as public places; and
	(b)     whether or not a person of reasonable firmness is present at the scene.
(5)     For the purpose of subsection (1), if 2 or more persons use or threaten unlawful violence—
	(a)     it is the conduct of them taken together that must be considered; and
	(b)     it is immaterial whether or not those persons use or threaten unlawful violence simultaneously.


Significant differences in the antecedents of co-offenders justifies disparity in the sentences: [_R v Cox_ (1996) 66 SASR 152; 85 A Crim R 178 (CCA)](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U2&serNum=1996431670&pubNum=0004840&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&ppcid=62e65e3b48bc4295a7d68c21888e9e7f&contextData=(sc.DocLink)).

## Application
"Your Honour, Prosecutions is guided by Sections 5, 6 and 6AAA of the Sentencing Act 1991 in these submissions. In particular, Section 5(1)(b), (c) and (d) are highly relevant to this matter. Others in the community need to be deterred from alcohol-related violence. This offender has a reasonable prospect of rehabilitation., but this must be weighed against the need for denunciation of his behaviour on the day."
"The maximum penalty for Unlawful Assault is 15 penalty units or 3 months imprisonment. The maximum penalty for Affray is 5 years imprisonment (level 6)."
"Mr. WILLIAMS co-offender was charged and pled guilty to Recklessly Cause Serious Injury. He was sentenced to 3 months imprisonment and a 12 month CCO. He has one prior conviction for unlawful assault. Prosecutions highlights the case of R v Cox (1996) 66 SASR 152, which can assist the court in justifying disparity between co-offenders sentences when there are significant differences in the backgrounds between them."
"Prosecutions have reviewed the sentencing outcomes for similar charges to this matter. Between 1 July 2020 and 30 June 2023, the outcomes for the relevant charges were:
- Unlawful Assault; 26.7% imprisonment, 18.9% community corrections order, 21.3% fine, 31.3% adjourned undertaking, 1.5% discharge/dismissal.
- Affray; 28.7% imprisonment, 29.1% CCO, 23.5% fine, 18.1% adjourned undertaking, discharge/dismissal 0.3%
"
"Prosecutions concedes the following points:
- Mr. WILLIAMS has made an early plea of guilty.
- Mr. WILLIAMS is of generally good character, providing 3 reference letters and having no prior convictions.
"
"Prosecutions weigh these concessions against the random and vicious attack conducted by Mr. WILLIAMS co-offender. Mr. WILLIAMS was caught up in his sense of loyalty and appears to believe he was defending his friend. But the victim's words are important: 'Hey, stop that, leave him alone'. Whilst there was no significant physical injury to the victim, the psychological impact from being prevented from assisting your friend from suffering an assault cannot be quantified."

## Conclusion
"Prosecutions believe a community corrections order is within range for this matter. This sentence would address Section 5(1) of the Sentencing Act 1991 in the following ways:
- (a) it would punish the offender in an extent and manner that is just in the circumstances; and
- (b) it would deter both this offender (specific deterrence) and others (general deterrence) from repeating the same types of alcohol driven crimes; and
- (c) it would establish an opportunity for the offender to be rehabilitated, of which he has good prospects given his lack of previous history; and
- (d) it would adequately denounce this type of behaviour"

"In coming to this conclusion, Prosecutions has considered Section 6 of the Sentencing Act 1991, being the lack of this offenders criminal history, a generally good reputation and his gainful employment being an on-going contribution to the community."

"This is also considering his early plea of guilty pursuant to Section 6AAA of the Sentencing Act 1991."
