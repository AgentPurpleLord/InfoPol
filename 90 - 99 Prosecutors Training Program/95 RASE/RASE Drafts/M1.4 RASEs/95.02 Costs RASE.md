**Costs RASE – M1.4**
**Exercise One**
You are the prosecutor in a mention hearing where the accused has been charged with one count of dealing with property suspected to be proceeds of crime, pursuant to s 195 of the Crimes Act 1958. The matter has been listed in court on two previous occasions, first mention and a case conference. The brief is included in the mention list and this is the first involvement you have had with it.
There is an authorised withdrawal report attached stating that the charge is to be withdrawn on the basis that the accused provided a receipt from the local TAB valid explaining his possession of the property the day before this hearing. When the matter is called you address the court and state that the charge is to be withdrawn. Defence agree and then submit that costs should be awarded to the accused on the basis that he has sought legal advice and attended court three times when the charge should not have been laid in the first instance. The magistrate does not ask you if you wish to reply and appears to be ready to award costs against the informant personally.
You stand to make a submission in respect of costs.
Learners are required to prepare a written submission (IRAC format preferred).

# Issue
Should costs be awarded to the accused / defence?

# Rule
## The Offence
Crimes Act 1958 Section 195 - Dealing with property suspected of being proceeds of crime
A person who deals with property if there are reasonable grounds to suspect that the property is proceeds of crime is guilty of a summary offence and liable to level 7 imprisonment (2 years maximum).

DPP v Marell [2005] VSC 430
At 36: "The previous provisions equivalent to s.195 of the Crimes Act were s.123 of the Confiscation Act 1997 and s.25 of the Summary Offences Act.  Although expressed in different terms, each of those sections dealt with property reasonably suspected to be stolen or the proceeds of crime.  Neither provision imposed any requirement that the prosecution prove that the relevant property was the proceeds of crime."
At 38: ""I conclude, on the basis of the plain meaning of the terminology used, the coherent relationship inter se of ss.193, 194 and 195 of the Crimes Act, the statements in the relevant Second Reading Speech and Explanatory Memorandum and the established construction of previous, broadly equivalent statutory provisions, that s.195 of the Crimes Act does not impose on the prosecution any burden of establishing beyond reasonable doubt that the relevant property is the proceeds of crime as defined in s.193(1)."
At 39: "In holding that s.195 of the Crimes Act imposed that requirement, and in dismissing charges upon that basis, the learned magistrate, in my opinion, fell into an error of law. "

## Costs
Criminal Procedure Act 2009 Section 400 - Right to be heard
    (1)     Subject to subsection (2), the court must not make an order awarding costs against a person without giving that person a reasonable opportunity to be heard.
    (2)     Subsection (1) does not apply to an order for costs made on the hearing and determination of a charge in the absence of the accused in accordance with Division 10 of Part 3.3.

Criminal Procedure Act 2009 Section 401 - Costs in Magistrates' Court
(1)     Unless otherwise expressly provided by this or any other Act or by the rules of court, the costs of, and incidental to, all criminal proceedings in the Magistrates' Court are in the discretion of the court and the court has full power to determine by whom, to whom and to what extent the costs are to be paid.
(2)         In exercising its discretion under subsection (1) in a criminal proceeding, the Magistrates' Court may take into account any unreasonable act or omission by, or on behalf of, a party to the proceeding that the court is satisfied resulted in prolonging the proceeding.
(3)     If the Magistrates' Court strikes out a charge under section 14(3), the court may award costs against the informant.
(4)     This section and section 410 apply to a purported proceeding in the Magistrates' Court which is beyond the jurisdiction of the court as if the purported proceeding were within jurisdiction.
(5)     If the Magistrates' Court determines to award costs against an informant who is a police officer, the order must be made against the Chief Commissioner of Police.

Latoudis v Casey [1990] HCA 59
"It was inequitable that the defendant should be expected to bear the financial burden of exculpating himself or herself, though the circumstances of a particular case may be such as to make it just and reasonable to refuse an order for costs or to make a qualified order for costs."
"In exercising its discretion to award or refuse costs, a court should look at the matter primarily from the perspective of the defendant."
"If a defendant has been given an opportunity of explaining his or her version of events before a charge is laid and declines t o take up that opportunity, it may be just and reasonable to refuse costs."
"Speaking generally, before a court deprives a successful defendant in summary proceedings of his or her costs, it will be necessary for the informant to establish that the defendant unreasonable induced the informant to think that a charge could be successfully brought against the defendant or that the conduct of the defendant occasioned unnecessary expense in the institution or conduct of the proceedings."

Alexander v Renney (unreported, VSC, Batt J, 21 August 1995)
Hung Phi Do v Bowers (unreported, VSC, OBryan J, 10 October 1996

# Application
"Your Honour, Section 400 of the Criminal Procedure Act provides that the Court must not award costs against a party without allowing them to first be heard."
"My friend has sought to apply for costs in relation to this matter. I would first turn to a history of the proceedings in my submissions. The accused was charged pursuant to Section 195 of the Crimes Act 1958, dealing with property suspected of being proceeds of crime."
"This offence and the nature of it was discussed in DPP v Marell [2005] VSC 430, with the Supreme Court concluding that the offence does not impose a burden of beyond reasonable doubt and that it is a strict liability offence. These types of offences do not require police to establish mens rea prior to laying a charge."
"The informant established his suspicion and laid the charge. The matter went to a first mention and was adjourned. The matter was summary case conferenced. It is now before Your Honour for its third hearing."
"One day prior to today's hearing, the accused provided the prosecution with a TAB receipt, evidence effectively nullifying the charge and providing proof of his ownership of the items that police suspected of being proceeds of crime."
"In outlining both the offence and the timeline of these proceedings Your Honour, my submission is that the accused has prolonged these proceedings. The accused had opportunities at arrest and interview, charge and summons, service of the preliminary brief, first mention and summary case conference to present this TAB receipt to Prosecution. In doing so, the matter would have immediately been withdrawn, as it is being withdrawn today, the first opportunity of the discovery of this evidence."
"This is further substantiated by Latoudis v Casey [1990] HCA 59, which provides:
- A court should view the case primarily from the defendant's point of view; and
- Consideration be given to whether the informant can establish that the defendant induced the informant to believe a charge could be successfully brought against the defendant; and
- If a defendant was given a previous opportunity to explain his or her version of events, it may be just and reasonable to refuse costs."
"Lastly Your Honour, if costs were to be awarded, pursuant to Section 401(5) of the Criminal Procedure Act 2009, the costs order must be made against the Chief Commissioner of Police."

# Conclusion
"Prosecutions submission is that no costs should be awarded to the accused, due to him omitting a reasonable explanation for a strict liability offence. Prosecutions has withdrawn the charge at the first available opportunity upon this evidence coming to light and has not prolonged these proceedings."
