Two primary *modes* of prosecution;
1. A police officer to prosecute; and
2. A lawyer to prosecute.

# Duties of Prosecutor
- Duty to act fairly
	- Important to be impartial and not to be emotionally invested.
	- Judicial officers will get to know you, your reputation can be at stake. 
	- Avoid inflammatory language and let evidence do the speaking.
	- Your job is to run a slideshow, to present a case.
	- Do the best you can with the evidence you have to get a conviction, but not at all costs.
	- Never mislead any party, the court or your opponent. 
	- Own your mistakes; honesty is best policy. 
	- The court doesn't care about your opinions as an individual.
	- The court wants to hear about your research; the law, etc.
	- Best submissions are those based 100% in the law, calmly delivered. 
	- You must be independent of the parties; you must disclose conflict of interests.
	- You may have worked with a previous person before, or supervised by them in the past, you must disclose them; e.g. close friend or supervisor. Head that off at the pass and be upfront.
- Duty to disclose
	- On going duty to disclose; duty to the court and administration to justice. 
	- Obligation on the Prosecutor, not the informant, to ensure Accused has all material to be fully aware of the charge.
	- If you become aware of something from the Informant, minimum is to immediately disclose, whatever it is, even if it is just the existence of this. 
	- E.g. cannot take statements and then intentionally leave those statements out of the brief if its counteracting another statement. 
	- Public interest; e.g. how VicPol goes about retaining evidence, or might be related to policies or procedures in relation to safety.
	- AB v CD [2018] HCA 58
		- Lawyer X case.
		- Justice Gananian, dealing in Supreme Court with VicPol application to prevent DPP from disclosing various clients of Lawyer X, as she was an Informant.
		- VicPol sought to prohibit by injunction in Supreme Court.
		- VicPol 'had obligation to keep promises to informer and disclosing information of identity would put her safety in jeopardy'
		- Justice refused the injunction.
		- Court of Appeal upheld this. 
		- VicPol appealed to HCA.
		- High Court very critical of Victoria Police
- Duty to call witnesses
	- Prosecutors responsibility alone to decide what witnesses to call; it is your presentation, your case. 
- Legal Profession Uniform Conduct (Barristers) Rules 2015
- Withdrawal of Charges
	- Reasonable Prospect of Conviction; and
		- All the evidence; and
		- Any further evidence that has yet to be obtained and supplied.
		- Only once we have all material / any further evidence, can we determine a withdrawal / prospect of conviction. 
	- Public interest
		- You only apply this step if there is a reasonable prospect of conviction.
