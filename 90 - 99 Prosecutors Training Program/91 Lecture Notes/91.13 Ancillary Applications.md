# Interpleader Summons
- VP in possession of property, dispute occurs over ownership. 
- E.g. multiple items from a burglary recovered, make media request for owner to identify themselves, then two people claim ownership.
- Section 58 of Victoria Police Act 2013
	- When MOPF has taken possession of goods; and
	- Doubtful whether a person claiming such goods is entitled to them; or
	- if two or more persons are claiming them and it is unknown who is entitled to them
	- Member may make application to Magistrates' Court to make decision about who the owner is.
- Order can be granted to have such goods delivered accordingly.
- It can be heard ex-parte.
- **No action may be brought against the member in relation to the application brought before the court**
- Practicalities:
	- MOPF served VP752 on all parties with some claim to property.
	- The form is effectively a summons.
	- Matter gets allocated to a Prosecutor. All parties (including informant) attend on the day.
	- Prosecutors role is to lead evidence from the informant how they came into possession and anything they know about the claimants to the property.
	- You can cross-examine any of the parties, they are not prosecution witnesses.

# App to be deemed not to be a prohibited person
- App brought by a prohibited person, relating to Firearms Act 1996.
- Quite a wide term as to who can be a prohibited person.
	- Most common is that any final orders under FV Act or PS Act means prohibited person.
- Person that can make an application:
	- Only a prohibited person by virtue of
	- s 3(1)(c)(i) & (ib) relate to final intervention orders that **do not** include conditions cancelling or revoking a licence. IB relates to PSIO with similar. 
	- If any order includes a provision cancelling / suspending, that person cannot make an application. They must serve the order + 5 years. Even if the order is withdrawn, if it contained that caluse, they still cannot apply under s. 189 of Firearms Act. 
- Practicalities:
	- Applicant serves notice on CCP
	- CCP notifies the relevant Divisional Firearms Officer
	- DFO contacts the AFM to determine their attitude.
	- File sent to Prosecutions.
	- If matter is uncontested, it will be dealt with first hearing.
	- If any contested witnesses, including AFM, the matter will be called on another date.
	- If AFM hasn't been contacted, ask for it to be adjourned to obtain this.
	- If the applicant is a police member, must consider conflict of interest. 
	- There should be a report from the DFO stating whether we oppose or not, along with interview with AFM / attitude towards the application.

# Drugs - Finding of Fact and Forfeiture
- DPCSA s. 83
	- App by MOPF
	- Must prove substance is drug of dependence
	- Or that an instrument, device or substance has been or could be used for cultivation, manufacture, sale or use of DOD.
	- We can prove via analysis, VPFSC provides a certificate. 
- (2) where there is a finding of fact, conclusive evidence as to which the finding of fact relates. 
- Allows destruction of drug paraphernalia / drugs. 
- Application made by police member for a magistrate to make finding of fact, including what it is and how much it weighs. 
- We might use this procedure prior to the conclusion of a matter, because of clan lab hazardous materials, or a significant amount of cannabis plants, etc. Informant would make app for finding of fact so that it can become conclusive proof and then items destroyed.


# Part 6B Notice Appeals
- Old Section 51 appeals are now captured by Part 6B. 
- Primarily relates to drink driving offences.
- Offences captured by Part 6B:
	- Driving under influence; and
	- XPCD; and
	- XPCA; and
	- related refusal offences; and
	- Excessive speed, over 45 or more. 
- ! Do not apply to interstate and international licence holders.
- Applicant is required to show s. 85T:
	- Division 1 (alcohol / drugs) or Division 2 (speeding): **exceptional circumstances** as to why notice should be cancelled; you can refer back to the bail act for this. 
	- Division 3 (Crimes Act offences), they are not an **unacceptable risk to road safety**.
- Hearing of the application
	- Onus is on the accused; and
	- Applicant must give evidence; and
	- ! Previous provision under s. 51 - court must hear any relevant evidence tendered by the applicant or CCP (no longer applies). 
	- Division 3 notices cannot have the accused cross-examined as to the offences alleged, but you **can** cross examine them on the risk to road safety.


# Licence Eligibility Orders
- 28 days notice is required.
- Must provide notice of s 58E from VicRoads, indicating what program they must complete.
	- ! App should be struck out if not provided.
- PROS given opportunity to cross-examine applicant if there is a need.
- Without limiting discretion, court must regard:
	- applicant's conduct with regards to alcohol & drugs during period of disqualification
	- applicant's physical & mental condition at the time of the hearing of the application
	- the effect of making the order on the safety of the applicant or public.
	- If they were referred to a medical / therapeutic service and the applicants level of attendance.
	- Must be on balance on probabilities, no limit to what they can consider. 
- Prior to the hearing, you should have priors (traffic/crime) and any outstanding penalty notices. 

# Application For Direction (Alcohol Interlock Condition Removal)
- VicRoads can refuse to remove AIC if data on interlock indicates:
	- Person attempted to start MV, failed due to detection of alcohol; or
	- Person failed to provide breath sample containing no registerable alcohol. 
- Applicant can then appeal this decision from VicRoads.
- The court can only direct VicRoads to treat applicant as not having been responsible for the failed attempt, per s. 50AAAC(6).
- The court cannot order the removal of the interlock. 

## Alcohol Interlock Condition Exemption
- VicRoads, on app may exempt a person being relicensed from requiring an AIC.
- If a s. 50AAAE exemption is available and it is refused by VicRoads, an appeal can be made to Magistrates' Court for a direction


# Arrest Warrants
- When the court may issue a warrant to arrest:
	- Accused fails to appear on summons to a summary offence (s. 80(1)(b) CPA 2009)
	- Accused FTA to summons for indictable offence (s. 81 CPA 2009)
	- Accused FTA when bailed to attend for any offence (s. 330(1) & (4) CPA 2009)
- If witness fails to appear, s. 194 Evidence Act can be used for a warrant if they have been served with summons/subpoena and conduct money.
- When should we apply?
	- Every time they fail to appear on bail; and
	- Every time an accused fails to appear on indictable offence;
	- ? Sometimes when offence is summary, you can use judgement regarding ex-parte.
