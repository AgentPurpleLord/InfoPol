- An item produced for inspection by the court. 
- Must be relevant, re: section 55 Evidence Act 2008.
- **Document** very important, defined in evidence act dictionary.
	- Section 47 - Document in question and copy of the document in question.
		- Allows copies of documents as well as originals.
		- Brings the act up to date with modern technology. 
	- Section 48 - Proof of content of document by tendering
		- Adduce evidence of admission by another by another party to the content of the document
			- E.g. if the accused signs a CCTV still it can become an exhibit
		- tendering a copy of the document
		- DVD tape etc by tendering transcript of the words
		- Computer document - via Word, pages or other word processor, by tendering document produced by that word processor
	- Section 50 - Proof of voluminous documents
		- Volume or complexity, may on application, adduce evidence in form of summary.
		- E.g. large amount of CCTV capturing offending, such as 50,000 still images depicting the offending.
			- App could be made to use summary, asked to stitch the stills into movie file, application was granted.
- Authenticity
	- Means proving an exhibit's origin
	- CSO takes a photograph, they must prove that it was taken and explain its relevance. E.g. the person creating an exhibit.
	- The witness must **identify** and **verify** the video before you can **tender**, e.g. informant conducts ROI.
		- "Your Honour may the witness be shown this item."
		- Item is handed to witness.
		- "Senior, what is this item?"
		- They respond with whatever the item is.
		- "Senior, did you view the recording after the interview?"
		- "Senior, what can you say about its accuracy?"
		- "Senior, did you have the ROI transcribed?"
		- "Senior, have you compared the ROI to the transcript? What can you say about its accuracy?"
		- Once all of these checked off, you can then say:
		- "Your honour, I now tender this record of interview."
- Continuity
	- The prosecution must prove the exhibit hasn't been tampered with, altered or in any way changed.
		- Seized drugs haven't been altered or replaced.
		- Digital recordings have not been edited.
		- Blood samples have been correctly stored, moved and analysed.
- Marking of Exhibits
	- Exhibits marked in order of production.
	- Prosecution is done alphabetically, Defence done numerically.
- Production for inspection
	- Common for witnesses to view or see exhibit.
	- Informant may have seized glass bottle used in glassing assault.
	- Witness to assault; give evidence that same bottle used in assault.
	- Forensic scientist might give evidence re: DNA on the bottle.
	- FPX expert might give evidence of FPX on the bottle.
	- "Your honour, may this Exhibit be marked for identification."
	- Court then applies a letter to the exhibit.
		- **The exhibit is not yet tendered. Do not forget to tender them.**
		- Mark off via the exhibit list to know what is tendered, what is identified, etc. 
- **SHOW** "Your Honour, I ask this item be shown to the witness OR may the witness see Exhibit B?"
- **IDENTIFY** "Please tell the court what I have just handed to you."
- **VERIFY** "Where did you first see this item?"
- **TENDER** "Your Honour, I tender this as an exhibit."
