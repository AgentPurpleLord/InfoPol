- Section 1 sets out the purpose of the act
	- Maximise safety for children/adults who have experienced FV
	- prevent/reduce FV greatest possible
	- hold perps accountable
- Section 5 provides thorough definition re: what is family violence.
- Police initiate application in 1 of 3 ways:
	- app and summons (with or without interim); or
	- app and warrant; or
	- FVSN
- App and summons
	- Registrar may issue summons upon receipt of app required attendance by RESP
	- If interim sought, court can rely on affidavit / oral evidence re: s. 55
- App and warrant
	- Better to seek interim order and discharge bail than to extend bail, as interim orders are made to last until final orders are made.
- FVSN
	- s. 53(1)(c) creates assumption that interim order will be made unless court is satisfied that it is not required.
	- This is effectively a **reverse onus** on the RESP to show why an interim order not required.

- IVO's can be finalised in one of four ways:
	- By consent without admission to the allegations (s78); or
	- Unopposed; or
	- At a contested hearing once the evidence of the parties is heard; or
	- Struck out (either due to police withdrawing the application or the magistrate making an assessment about the merit of the application)
	- Final order cannot be made without being satisfied as to service. Consider alternative / substituted service. 
- Final Orders
	- Two limb test, RESP committed family violence and is likely to do so again.

# Contested Hearings
If the app is opposed by the RESP -> must proceed to contested hearing as soon as practicable (s.59 FVPA).
Hearing can relate to more than one app (s. 63).
- Court can inform itself as it sees fit, despite rules of evidence, meaning otherwise inadmissible evidence may be heard.
- RESP cannot cross-examine protected witnesses unless via legal rep s.70 fvpa.
- Where an adult AFM is on the application, police cannot seek more limited conditions on a final order without that AFM's consent (s. 75).
- Court must consider any children before making a final decision (s.73I).
- Court must make a final order for a child if court makes final order for an affected family member (s 77)

- The court can make a **separate** order for a child on its own initiative even where a FIVO is not made for an AFM.
- **Duration** of a final order is a matter for the court (s 97/99), however it must not exceed 12 months in the absence of exceptional circumstances where RESP is a child.
