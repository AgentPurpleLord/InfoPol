- Role of the prosecutor
- Fully and fairly set out circumstances, including impact on victims.
- Where dispute on factual matters, call evidence to support prosecutors claims.
- Prosecutor advocates for proportionate and appropriate sanctions, not jail for every offender.

# Making a Submission
- Responding to the defence submission.
- Incorporate, where appropriate, parts of the defence submission into the prosecutions.
	- E.g. when was the psych report created, was that before previous court dates, is it dated? is it a lot of self-reporting by the accused? has it been reconfirmed by others?
	- Could turn a psych report on its head, defence might rely upon psych report to reduce moral culpability; may in turn mean there needs to be increase in protection of the community. 
	- Letters of reference might be provided by defence too.
		- Have the people mentioned the offending at all? If they haven't, do they actually know what the accused has done?
		- E.g. this is out of character, I'm surprised by this, etc. It is possibly objectionable or something you can say little wait should be put onto it.
		- Look at language of the letter, has it been prepared by the actual author?
- IRAC your submission, it gives you direction and ensures you address the relevant areas of the Sentencing Act 1991.

# Purpose of Sentencing
- Punish offender; just in all the circumstances
- Deter offender or others from committing offences
- Rehabilitation
- Denunciation
- Protection of the community
- A combo of any of these purposes

- Important you identify:
	- Why the sentence Prosecutors suggest will achieve an appropriate purpose?
- Section 5(2) is a good place to argue what purpose the sentence we are suggesting is. 
- Current sentencing practices; easiest way is to use Sentencing Advisory Stats. SAC Stats are fallible because don't discuss aggravating features taken into account. Limited use. 

- *Nature or gravity of the offence*
	- In the case of a theft, things like a theft from an employee or employer (breach of trust) makes it more serious.
	- Assaults; age or vulnerability of the victim, etc.
- *Impact on the Victim*
	- Tender photographs of injuries or damage, even at sentencing phase, you can still tender them.
- *Early plea of guilty*
	- Was the accused remorseful and was their early plea indicative of that?
	- Or were they caught red-handed. If they were, point that out, it carries weight. 
- *Previous Character*
	- re: character references
		- "Your Honour, there is no mention of what the current offending is, so it is not known whether the author of this letter is aware of why he's before the court today."
	- Highlight relevant priors, particularly in family violence.
- *Victim Impact Statement*
	- VIS made via stat dec or oral evidence.
	- Defence can review, they may object to admissibility, but tender full copy and let court decide.
	- Can attach photographs, drawings, medical reports, etc.
	- Court may call victim or others to give evidence.
	- Person can elect to have it read aloud. 

# Community Corrections Orders
- Conditions that can be included:
	- Unpaid community work cannot exceed 600 hours, or if it is the sole condition, 300 hours.
	- Treatment and rehabilitation
	- Supervision
	- Non-Association conditions
	- Residence restrictions or exclusion
	- Place or area exclusion (must not be inconsistent with an intervention order condition)
	- Curfew
	- Alcohol exclusion condition 
	- Judicial monitoring

# Deferral of Sentence
- Magistrates' pursuant to 83A of the Sentencing Act 1991 can defer a sentence for up to 12 months.
- In doing so, they may be released on bail and may order pre-sentence report.
- On return, court take into account behaviour from the deferral and the pre-sentence report or other relevant thing.

# Case Law and Other Concepts
Section 60 & 61 CPA 2009
- Commonly occurs at summary case conferences and contest mention stage.
- Magistrate is not bound by it and it should not be referred to in future hearings if resolution not reached at the time.

# Diversion
-  *Prosecution* should only object to diversion being granted when **exceptional circumstances exist**.
- Diversion covered by s. 59(1)(a) of CPA 2009 and s 356B of CYFA 2005
- Prosecutor considers the same criteria as the informant and sub-officer. 
	- For *firearms* offences, diversion must not be offered without consulting with Inspector or above.
	- For *family violence* offences, diversion must not be offered without OIC approval. 
	- For *sex offences*, diversion must not be offered without SSOPU OIC approval.
	- For *Victoria Police* employee's, the AC of PSC must agree to diversion.

# Sentencing Terminology
- *Parity*; like offences should be treated alike, but relevant differences between offenders can justify different outcomes (Anthony v The Queen [2016] VSCA 22[12])
- *Quantum*; the total damage/theft value in something. 
- *Cumulative*; being added onto one another.
- *Concurrent*; to run along side each other. 

# Serious Offenders
- Part 2A of Sentencing Act
	- Serious Sexual Offender; and
	- Serious Violent Offender (Make Threats To Kill is within this boundary)
		- Person must first have been a serious violent offender prior to this.
		- Normally requires conviction and sentence (term of imprisonment) in order to categorise person as this.

# Slip Rule
- Section 104A of Sentencing Act; allows Magistrate to correct clerical mistakes, slips or omissions, etc.
- Has a 14 day time-limit; e.g. incorrect licence loss, you can use this to fix.
- Section 104B; reopen proceedings to correct penalties imposed. 

# R v Verdins (2007) 16 VR 269
- Impaired mental functioning, whether temporary or permanent is relevant to sentencing in at least the following six ways:
	- Reduction of moral culpability of the offending conduct (denunciation is less or reduced);
	- Impacts the kind of sentence imposed and the conditions to be imposed;
	- General deterrence may be eliminated or reduced (i.e. is the accused an appropriate vehicle for this purpose of sentencing);
	- Specific deterrence may be moderated or eliminated;
	- Existence of mental health condition at the time of the sentence, may mean a sentence will be more burdensome on the accused compared to a person with normal mental health;
	- If imprisonment would have an adverse effect on the persons mental health, this will be a mitigating factor.

# Boulton v The Queen (2014) 46 VR 308
- Guideline judgment conducted pursuant to the provisions of the sentencing act 1991.
- Discusses CCO's.
- Boulton is not a get out of jail free card, re Hutchinson v The Queen [2015] VSCA 115
- Distinguish your case from Boulton
	- One count each of armed robbery and reckless cause injury
	- Sentence to 3 months imprisonment with an 8 year CCO for armed robbery and 265 days imprisonment (reckoned as served) with a 5 year CCO for RCI (CCO concurrent.)
	- On appeal, 3 months imprisonment with 3 year CCO for armed robbery and 2 1/2 year CCO fro RCI (CCO concurrently)
	- *Boulton had limited prior convictions & mental health issues reducing moral culpability*.
- Distinguish your case from Clements
	- Armed Robbery + Attempted Armed Robbery
	- Sentenced to 10 year CCO and $3000 fine for armed robbery + 10 year CCO and $1000 fine for attempted armed robbery.
	- Appeal: 6 year CCO together with the fines.
	- *Clements, rehabilitation would be achieved within the range of CCO maximums. Rehabilitation was a key consideration, age at time of offending too, no prior convictions either*.
- Distinguish your case from Fitzgerald.
	- 2x Common Assault - Sentenced on each charge to 5 year CCO, 540 days of PSD (did not constitute a sentence)
	- Appeal - sentence to 540 days imprisonment, time reckoned as already served.
	- *Fitzgerald did have prior relevant history but so old it was not relevant, substantial mental health issues, spent 540 days in PSD because he did not want to make bail application*.

# Bugmy Principles
Bugmy v The Queen (2013) 249 CLR 571
- Whilst in the context of Aboriginality, the principle in Bugmy would apply broadly, i.e. to any deprived background. 
- Bugmy confirms that there is no separate approach to sentencing of Aboriginal persons to non-Aboriginal persons. 
- If accused comes from deprived background, relevant in determining moral culpability.
- Remote aboriginal communities there is reduced exposure to European ways, may impact on imprisonment.
- Effect of deprived background does not dissipate over time, nor does it dissipate over the course of an accused's criminal history.


# Pasinis v The Queen [2014] VSCA 97
- **General deterrence** is of fundamental importance in cases of domestic violence... The key to **protection** lies in **deterring** the violent conduct by sending an unequivocal message to **would-be perpetrators** of domestic violence that if they offend, they will be sentenced to a lengthy period of imprisonment so that they are no longer in a position to inflict harm [57]

# R v Renzella [1997] 2 VR 88
- Where pre-sentence detention has not been declared by a court pursuant to s 18 of the Sentencing Act 1991, that pre-sentence detention can be taken into account by a future sentencing court.
- Days spent in a residential rehab facility or immigration detention can also be taken into account as Renzella time, less weight is attributable.

# Winch v The Queen (2010) 27 VR 658
- Glassing cases should, in our view, be treated as being in the same category as other RCSI offences which involve the use of a dangerous weapon [54]
- Glassing cases have a number of recurrent features. The typical glassing... occurs in or near licensed premises. It is usually an act of alcohol fuelled aggression, in disproportionate response to an actual or perceived slight. The typical offender is young and of generally good character, and is full of remorse after the event [32]
