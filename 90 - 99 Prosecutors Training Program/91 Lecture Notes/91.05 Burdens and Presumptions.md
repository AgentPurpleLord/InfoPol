- ~ **Burden of Proof:** Obligation to prove something by either party.
- The **legal burden** is proving the guilty of the accused.
	- To discharge the burden, the prosecution must prove the guilt of the accused **beyond reasonable doubt**.
- If the accused has the legal burden, they must discharge that burden on the **balance of probabilities**.
	- An example of this is *mental impairment*, they would be required to prove a mental impairment on the balance of probability.
- If a **no case to answer** submission is made, prosecution must satisfy the evidential burden for each element of the offence. *May v O'Sullivan (1955) 92 CLR 652*

# Affirmative Defences
- Duress (section 322O Crimes Act 1958)
- Honest and Reasonable Mistake of Fact
- Intoxication (322T CA)
- Sudden and Extraordinary Emergency (322R CA)
- Sane Automatism
- Self Defence (section 322K CA)

An example of where the accused might bear the burden is in a majority of defences; e.g. possessing a controlled weapon. If the accused has a *lawful excuse* they must prove that on the balance of probabilities. 

# Voir Dire
- Used to determine admissibility of evidence before it is admitted.
- s. 142 Evidence Act 2008 covers this.
- Something such as the discussion of the admissibility of an interview would be discussed.

# Bail Application
- Burden for *exceptional circumstances* or *show compelling reasons* is on the accused.
- *Unacceptable risk* is on the prosecution.

# Presumptions of Fact
- Are not conclusive, trier of fact need not draw the presumed conclusion.
- E.g. doctrine of recent possession, this is an inference that may be made.

# Presumptions of Law
- Are *conclusive* matters.
- Irrebuttable presumptions and rebuttable presumptions.
