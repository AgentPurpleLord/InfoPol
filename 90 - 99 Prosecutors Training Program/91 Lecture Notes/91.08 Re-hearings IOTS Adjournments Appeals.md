# Re-Hearings
- Section 88 of CPA 2009
	- If sentence imposed by MC in criminal proceeding (summary only) ex-parte, that person or informant on persons behalf may apply to MC for an order that the charge be reheard.
	- Requires the non-appearance of the accused
	- If the accused did not appear **but had counsel** appear on their behalf, they cannot re-apply for re-hearing. Refer to s. 328 for definition of appearance.
- Section 89 of CPA 2009
	- Accused must state the reason he wants the re-hearing.
- Section 90 of CPA 2009
	- Accused must serve notice within 7 days after filing of the notice. 
- s. 392 CPA 2009
	- Accused can serve notice on the prosecutor, not the informant explicitly
- Must consider two factors; discretion to grant (s92) and are they entilted to re-hearing (s94)
	- **Carelessness** (Van Ryswyck v Hicks (1974) 8 SASR 376); misread date on summons, failed to appear on the correct date, was convicted after ex-parte hearing.
	- **Delay by applicant**; knew of the hearing but delayed re-hearing application, waited 11 years (Garner v Gallienne [1985] VSC 226) before his re-hearing application.
		- Should examine why respondent failed to appear, delay in making application, availability of witnesses, etc.
		- Re-hearing was denied.
	- **Prejudice to any party**
		- Can witnesses be located? Documents and exhibits available?
	- **Personal circumstances of applicant changes**
		- May not want conviction because of new travel plan.
- If the applicants drivers licence was suspended, cancelled or varied by the court **that order remains** unless
	- Separate app for stay on licence susp/cancellation under s91(2); or
	- if a re-hearing app alone is lodged then licence susp/cancellation continues
	- Once app is granted then all original sentence orders are set aside.
	- If refused, original sentence and orders stand. 
- s. 94(2) rehearing in certain cases
	- If court does ex-parte, sends notification, accused within 28 days makes application for re-hearing, it must be heard.

# IOTS
- Indictable offence required by law to be heard by a judge or jury.
- Magistrates' Court can summarily hear/determine specified common law and indictable offences.
- s. 28 CPA 2009 outlines IOTS offences.
	- Listed in Sch. 2
	- Level 5 or 6 offence
	- Punishable by a term not exceeding 10 years or fine not exceeding 1200 penalty units
	- no other Act or instrument says otherwise.
- s. 29 CPA 2009
	- court considers it appropriate
	- accused consents
- Common law offences
	- Affray, common assault, criminal defamation, false imprisonment, misconduct in public office.
- Uplifting example
	- Detective lists matter in MC court accidentally.
	- s.29(2) CPA 2009, application can be made/opposed for matter to be heard in summary jurisdiction. 
	- Accused will always consent to IOTS, it benefits them, not as much power as county court. 
		- e.g. two years single offence, five years multiple offences.
	- Complicated financial arugments or tendency evidence can be good grounds to go up to county court.
	- Multiple co-accused's as well.

# Adjournments
- Section 331 CPA 2009
	- Court can adjourn matter to any time and place, for any purpose on any terms that the court considers appropriate.
- Section 20 CPA 2009
	- If the accused is not on bail or in custody the registrar can adjourn the proceeding; or
	- they can adjourn if on bail and EXTEND bail, but they cannot vary or revoke bail.
- Be aware of improper reasons for adjournment:
	- Magistrate shopping; as in defence adjourning the matter to get a more favourable outcome; or
	- Attempt to avoid operation of legislative provision (e.g. mandatory licence loss); or
	- Contravention of suspended sentences; getting affairs in order. 

# Appeals
- S. 254 CPA; accused can appeal to County court on conviction and sentence or sentence alone. 
- S. 257 CPA; DPP can appeal to County Court against sentence if it is in public interest.
- s. 272 CPA; DPP can appeal on behalf of informant to Supreme Court on question of law. 
	- Erroneous exercise of discretion; or
	- Erroneous finding of fact; or
	- Magistrate did not give reason for decision; or
	- Wrongful admission of evidence; or
	- Imposition of an illegal sentence
