Section 401 of CPA 2009 covers costs.
- Court has discretion to award costs, who pays them and how they pay them.
- Court must account for anything done by accused person.
- If Magistrates' Court strikes out charge under 14(3), court may award costs against informant.
- Section 410 apply to Magistrates' Court.
- Costs must be awarded against chief comm.
- Magistrates' Court Act 1989, s. 131 mirrors s 401 CPA.
	- s. 131A allows matter to go to Costs Court.

# When can costs be awarded
- Adjournment without merit:
	- Informant forgotten to subpoena witnesses to a contested hearing; or
	- Informant asked to provide further statements which was in their capability to obtain but did not do so; or
	- Forensic evidence, drug/fpx/DNA, informant made no effort to have that done (drug analysis sitting in station).
- Non-compliance with pre-court disclosure requirements:
	- If a witness was at an event, but they refused to provide a statement, that isn't non-compliance. Only if we haven't bothered, or have it but haven't disclosed is it an issue.
- Withdrawal of Charges
	- Happens constantly at MC.
	- Normally, when negotiating with defence, we request for them not to make application for costs if we withdraw to avoid this situation. 
	- Can be situations where Defence gave good reason to withdraw but we haven't, so they won't agree.
- Dismissal
- Intervention Order Without Merit
	- IVO where wrongful intervention by informant, e.g. family friend / relative, sought to take out IVO to subvert need to go to family court, child access issues, etc.

# Cost Orders
- Party and party basis
	- Civil scale, least generous awards. Only costs proper and necessary to obtain justice / defence rights.
- Solicitor and client basis
	- Necessarily and properly and reasonably incurred. Doesn't mean **everything** billed, but they are more generous form of party party.
- Indemnity basis
	- Full costs borne by the party.

# Reasonableness
- Magistrates' will tax costs on a basis that corresponds to "party and party" in civil proceedings.
- Only allow costs that were reasonably incurred of a reasonable amount.
	- A rolls Royce defence for a Holden commodore charge; e.g. speeding, organises a QC and instructing solicitor. Argument is that it is not reasonable for someone to go to that expense. 

# What costs should be paid?
- Only costs of a reasonable amount and reasonably incurred for the attainment of justice for defending the criminal proceedings will be allowed on taxation (Norton v Morphett (1995) 83 A Crim R 90).
- Solicitor reviews brief of evidence, obtain instructions, solicitor bill over 6 minute intervals, e.g. 10 units per hour. 
	- Includes them attending SCC.
	- Attending a CME.
	- Attending a hearing (barristers charge per day)

# What costs should we not pay?
- Appearance fees for adjournments cause by defence. 
	- Adjourned in absence of the accused; or
	- Admin adjournments.
	- Did defence unreasonably prolong proceedings?
	- Did the conduct of the defendant prolong the proceedings?
- Unnecessary or frivolous subpoena hearings.
	- E.g. subpoenad for things that **might** be there, fishing expeditions.
	- Or subpoena the brief itself, they are provided that brief normally.
- Costs associated with changing lawyers numerous times.
- Costs related to bail applications
	- That's their listing, their application, we are the respondent in the application.
- Costs which have been paid via cost fund certificate.
	- Cost run by the court, no fault of accused or prosecution for the matter to be adjourned.
	- No room for the matter to get heard on the day, admin adjourned, that's not Prosecution issue. Court fund normally pay defence fund. 
- Section 400 CPA 2009, court cannot award costs unless the party has had opportunity to be there.
- Lovejoy v Johnson VSC 20 October 1997
	- "the principle to be applied is one of reasonableness and there is no necessary link between allowing counsel's fees in full and the total amount claimed by a solicitor for attending to instruct."
	- "On one view an allowance of $800 per day for an instructing solicitor in a summary proceeding in the Magistrates' Court, where the counsel usually appear uninstructed may be regarded as munificent."
- Hourly rate reasonable:
	- Magistrates' Court Scale is approx $208 per hour
	- Supreme Court Scale is approx $417 per hour
- Expert Reports
	- Was it reasonable to engage an expert for summary matter?
	- Did Prosecution object to expert being called at hearing?
	- Are the fees excessive?
- Costa v Parks [2008] VSC 47
	- Where an accused is convicted on some charges, the general rule that a successful accused should not recieve costs.
	- E.g. if they contest some charges, but we WD on those contested ones and they plea to some, unlikely they will get costs.
- If charges laid which are committal stream -> first mention -> Filing Hearing is the normal order of procedure.
	- As in, if in the circumstances, the informant adds additional charges which would uplift the matter due to further evidence coming to light, costs are not justified. 

# What to consider as prosecutor

## Costs
1. Why are costs being sought?
2. Are the costs attributable to prosecution?
3. Has the accused contributed to delay?
4. Has the court contributed to delay?
5. Has an outside party contributed to delay?
6. Do you have enough information on the brief to make submissions in respect of costs?

## Withdrawal
1. Are the costs attributable to prosecution?
2. Why was the charge/charges withdrawn?
3. Are all of the charges being withdrawn?
4. Has the accused contributed to a delay in the charges being withdrawn?
	1. Non disclosure of exculpatory evidence?
	2. Frustrated the ability of police to investigate?
5. Any information on the brief to make submissions in respect of costs?

# If Costs are Granted
- Quantum (total amount) is reasonable and agreed:
	- Seek a stay of 3 months for payment of the costs; and
	- Obtain an extract and payment notice; and
	- Compile an adjournment with costs report on BMS and submit via unit manager.
- Quantum is not agreed
	- Ask the court to make the following order:
		- "Chief Commissioner of Police to pay the defendant's costs, quantum to be agreed, in default of agreement liberty to apply to the Costs Court."
- Note if the claim is over $20,000, it must be referred to VSGO. 
- More then $10,000, must have OIC permission to negotiate. 


# Material for Costs Negotiations
- Complete brief of evidence (including SCC sheets and email correspondence)
- Certified extract for costs
- Detailed adjournment with costs report. Report should contain relevant information useful for assessing the reasonableness of the claim. 
- Copies of receipts for any disbursements paid i.e. counsel marked back sheet.
	- Defence are obligated to provide this.
- Details of adjournments. Detailed information re: adjournments made, identify who made the application, reasons why, etc. 
- Details of any bail applications.
- Itemised bill from defence containing:
	- Description of each items of work undertaken;
	- Dates of the each items of work claimed undertaken;
	- Duration of time spent on each item;
	- Amount charged for each item;
	- Charge out rate of each solicitor that undertook work.
- Copy of any costs agreement entered between defence and the accused:
	- Mainly details of solicitor hourly rates and costs estimator provided to clients.
	- This is required by law. 

# Appealing Costs
- Section 17, Appeals Cost Act 1998
	- Reason for adjournment not attributable to the accused or the accused legal practitioner
	- Idemnity certificate for their costs.

# Can we apply for costs to Prosecution
- Yes, s. 401(1) CPA.
- Unusual, but does happen when court awards.
- Witness attendance, police members who have unnecessarily attended court.
- Prosecutors preparation of a matter.
- Victoria Police fees and charges - produced by the revenue branch and used to calculate what we charge external agencies for police time.
