
# Question 1
You are assisting in the family violence mention court. You are preparing to take a Family Violence Safety Notice (FVSN) into court, but neither the AFM nor the respondent has attended, although they have both been served. The allegations contained in the narrative include serious assaults over a long period of time, sometimes in front of the parties’ children. The FVSN includes full conditions prohibiting any contact except for family law purposes. You try to call the AFM but there is no answer, and you have no additional information but the facts in the narrative.

**Relying on the provisions within the Family Violence Protection Act, what would you seek?**

  

Select one:

a. Seek an adjournment and make application for an interim order with conditions prohibiting family violence and use of an agent pursuant to ss53(1)(c), 55(1)(a) and 55(2) of the _Family Violence Protection Act 2008_ (Vic).You can only seek conditions prohibiting family violence against the AFM, or causing another person to engage in conduct prohibited pursuant to the order, as s75 of the _Family Violence Protection Act 2008_ (Vic) prevents you from seeking further conditions on an interim basis without the AFM’s consent.

b. Seek an adjournment and make application for an interim order with conditions prohibiting any contact except for family law purposes on the basis that it is necessary to protect the AFM and the children pending a final decision about the application pursuant to s53(1)(a) of the _Family Violence Protection Act 2008_ (Vic). You rely on the affidavit contained within the FVSN to meet the evidentiary requirements of ss53(1)(c), 55(1)(a) and 55(2) of the _Family Violence Protection Act 2008_ (Vic).  

c. Seek a final order with conditions prohibiting any contact except for family law purposes. Given the FVSN indicates that there have been serious assaults over a long period of time, the court can be satisfied that family violence has occurred and is likely to occur again or continue to occur as required by s74(1) _Family Violence Protection Act 2008_ (Vic). Due to the serious nature of the allegations you seek an order for at least 3 years duration pursuant to s97 _Family Violence Protection Act 2008_ (Vic).

d. Seek to adjourn the application pursuant to s30(1)(b)(i) _Family Violence Protection Act 2008_ (Vic) for the AFM to attend court and seek legal advice, with the FVSN to continue acting as an interim order until the next hearing date pursuant to s31(1A) _Family Violence Protection Act 2008_ (Vic). Pursuant to s31(3)(b) _Family Violence Protection Act 2008_ (Vic), the application must be returned to court within five working days.

# Question 3
You are prosecuting in the Family Violence Mention Court.  An informant approaches you with an interim order application returning today. The interim order prohibits any contact between the AFM and respondent and was adjourned previously to engage the AFM with support services.  The informant confirms that despite the serious allegations in the application, which include physical assaults resulting in hospital admissions, the AFM stated she has not changed her view and does not want any intervention order to be made.

The matter is called, and the magistrate indicates that the matter should be finalised today.  The court confirm that both parties have been served however not attended court.

**What would you submit to the court?**

  

Select one:

a. Your Honour, neither party has attended court today; however, the informant advises that they have spoken to the AFM and she is still not supportive of any order being made. Pursuant to s75 of the _Family Violence Protection Act 2008_ (Vic), the court may make a final intervention order without the AFM’s consent, however such an order may only contain conditions listed in s 81(2)(a), (f), (g) and (h) of the _Family Violence Protection Act 2008_ (Vic). Accordingly, despite concerns that police have for the safety of the AFM, prosecution seek a final order with conditions prohibiting the respondent from committing family violence or causing another person to engage in conduct prohibited by the order.

b. Your Honour, neither party has attended court today; however, the Informant advises that they have spoken to the AFM. Prosecution seeks a final order pursuant to s74 of the _Family Violence Protection Act 2008_ (Vic) with conditions prohibiting family violence, excluding the respondent from the AFM’s residence, and prohibiting the respondent from approaching, telephoning or otherwise contacting the protected person pursuant to s81(2) of the _Family Violence Protection Act 2008_ (Vic).

c. Your Honour, neither party has attended court today; however, the Informant advises that they have spoken to the AFM and she is still not supportive of any order being made. Given that the application has already been adjourned to engage the AFM with support services and her view has not changed, the prosecution seeks that the application be stood down for us to seek instructions from a Sergeant to withdraw the application.

d. Your Honour, neither party has attended court today; however, the Informant advises that they have spoken to the AFM and she is still not supportive of any order being made. Given the serious nature of the allegations the prosecution seeks to adjourn the application with an interim order in place with limited conditions, prohibiting the respondent from committing family violence or causing another person to engage in conduct prohibited by the order pursuant to s 53(1)(b), as neither party is here to oppose such an order.

# Question 4
You are prosecuting in the Family Violence Mention Court when a FVSN comes across your desk. The narrative is as follows:

 _“The AFM and respondent are a married couple who recently migrated to Australia. Police were called to the address by neighbours, who reported that they hear screaming and yelling from the parties’ house about twice a week, as well as breaking furniture. They say that on these occasions they can hear the respondent screaming abuse at the AFM, who is often seen fleeing the house when this occurs. They further report that the AFM often presents with minor facial injuries. Police spoke to the AFM who confirmed that the respondent regularly becomes violent when drinking but insisted that no police action was required as it is culturally unacceptable and would result in community isolation.”_

The AFM is present in court insisting to the prosecutor that no intervention order is necessary, although the AFM acknowledges that the contents of the narrative are correct.  The respondent has been served with the FVSN and notified of the court date, however, has not attended.

**As the prosecutor, how would you proceed?**

  

Select one:

a. You assess that as the AFM is unsupportive and the only evidence is from the neighbours, you should seek authorisation to withdraw the application. You recognise that pursuing an application which may result in community isolation would be of further detriment to the AFM.  It is therefore inappropriate to persist with the application in these circumstances.

b. Based on the serious nature of the allegations, you seek a final intervention order with full conditions forbidding contact except for family law purposes pursuant to s75(1) _Family Violence Protection Act 2008_ (Vic). The affidavit contained within the FVSN indicates that there has been regular verbal and physical abuse and property damage, so the court can be satisfied on the balance of probabilities that the respondent has committed family violence and is likely to do so again, satisfying the requirements of s74(1) _Family Violence Protection Act 2008_ (Vic).

c. Since the AFM is unsupportive of any order being made, the court is restricted pursuant to s75 _Family Violence Protection Act 2008_ (Vic) from seeking any conditions beyond those contained in ss81(2)(a), (f), (g) and (h). Given this, you seek a final safe contact intervention order pursuant to s75 _Family Violence Protection Act 2008_ (Vic), recognising that a safe contact order may be more culturally acceptable.  You seek conditions prohibiting family violence and causing another person to engage in behaviour prohibited by the order, as well as a residential exclusion condition should the respondent be affected by alcohol.

d. Even though the AFM is unsupportive, you seek a no contact interim order pursuant to s53(1)(c) _Family Violence Protection Act 2008_ (Vic) to protect the AFM, as there is currently no justification to discontinue the protection of the AFM prior to a final decision being made. You submit to the court that community isolation is the primary concern of the AFM and that engagement with community support services may be beneficial prior to making a final decision about the application.  Should the court refuse the adjournment due to the AFM being unsupportive, finalise a safe contact order pursuant to s75 _Family Violence Protection Act 2008_ (Vic) with conditions limited to those contained in ss81(2)(a), (f), (g) and (h).

# Question 6
The respondent and AFM are husband and wife and have been married for 10 years.  
They have an **8 year old son**.  
Police made application for an intervention order against the respondent after he became increasingly aggressive and abusive towards the AFM.  
It’s suspected that the change in his temperament was a result of **drug and alcohol abuse**.  
The respondent has not agreed to a final order and you are now prosecuting the contested IVO hearing.

The AFM gives evidence that her son confided in her, stating that he was often subjected to verbal abuse when left alone with the respondent.  Defence object on the basis that the evidence is hearsay and that the AFM cannot give this evidence on behalf of their son.

The magistrate accepts that the evidence is hearsay and asks the prosecutor how the evidence can be admitted.

In the event an order is granted, the magistrate seeks guidance from the prosecutor as to whether it can protect the child from domestic violence in circumstances where their name was omitted from the application.

Please write your written response to **both** issues in IRAC format.

## Issue
Can hearsay evidence be admitted in a family violence intervention order proceeding?
Can a child be listed on an intervention order when their name was omitted on the application for that order?

# Rule
FAMILY VIOLENCE PROTECTION ACT 2008 - SECT 65
Evidence
(1)     Subject to this Act, in a proceeding for a family violence intervention order the court may inform itself in any way it thinks fit, despite any rules of evidence to the contrary.
(2)     The following provisions apply to a proceeding for a family violence intervention order—
(a)     [sections 13](http://www.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/vic/consol_act/fvpa2008283/s13.html), [30](http://www.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/vic/consol_act/fvpa2008283/s30.html), [31](http://www.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/vic/consol_act/fvpa2008283/s31.html) and 41 and Part 3.10 of the **[Evidence Act 2008](http://www.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/vic/consol_act/ea200880/)** ;
(b)     Division 2A of Part II of the **[Evidence (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act 1958](http://www.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/vic/consol_act/epa1958361/)** .
(3)     The court may refuse to admit, or may limit the use to be made of, evidence if the court is satisfied—
(a)     it is just and equitable to do so; or
(b)     the probative value of the evidence is substantially outweighed by the danger that the evidence may be unfairly prejudicial to a party or misleading or confusing.


FAMILY VIOLENCE PROTECTION ACT 2008 - SECT 67
Evidence given by children
    (1)     A child, other than a child who is an applicant for a family violence intervention order or a respondent, must not give evidence for the purposes of a proceeding under this Act or a litigation restraint order proceeding unless the court grants leave for the child to do so.
    (2)         In deciding whether to grant leave under subsection (1) the court must have regard to the following—
        (a)     the desirability of protecting children from unnecessary exposure to the court system; and
        (b)     the harm that could occur to the child and to family relationships if the child gives evidence.
    (3)     This section applies despite anything to the contrary in the **[Evidence Act 2008](http://www.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/vic/consol_act/ea200880/)** .

# Application
s. 65 FVPA: In this case, the AFM provided hearsay evidence (testimonial evidence from another person that she heard). However, this section provides the court may inform itself in any way it thinks fit, which would enable it to be accepted into evidence.

s. 67 FVPA: In this case, the child is an 8-year-old boy. We have established hearsay evidence can be admitted. In addition, s. 67 would add further weight to admitting the hearsay evidence to meet (2)(a) and (b), to protect the child from the court system.

s. 74 FVPA: In this case, the courts power to make a final order is here. The notes of this section reference s. 77 if a final order is made for the parent AFM and s. 77B(1) if a final order is not made for the parent AFM. 

s. 77 FVPA: In this case, if HH were to grant a final order for the mother, the court must make a final order for the child. As the RESP is contesting the order, I would refer to s. 77(2)(a), in that the circumstances outlined by the mother are substantially the same as that of the need for the child's protection. I would also refer to s. 77(4) in that it is necessary for this order to be made to protect the child from family violence committed by the respondent. 

s. 77B(1) FVPA: In this case, if HH were **not** to grant a final order for the mother, we would fall back onto this section. To cover off on s. 77B(3)(a)(i), we can refer back to s. 45(d) to point out that the mother is the parent/guardian of the child.

# Conclusion
Prosecutions submits that hearsay evidence is permitted in a family violence intervention order proceeding, pursuant to s. 65.
Prosecutions additionally submits that Your Honour must make an order for the child if Your Honour makes an order for the mother pursuant to s. 77 FVPA. 
Prosecutions lastly submits that if Your Honour does not make an order for the mother, the court may make an order for the child pursuant to s. 77B(1).
