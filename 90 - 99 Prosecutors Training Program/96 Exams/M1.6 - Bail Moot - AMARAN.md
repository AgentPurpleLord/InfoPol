# Key Cases
Re Ilpola [2020] VSC 578 *highlighting FV risks* 
Given that the applicant was subject to a CCO for family violence against his former partner, as well as a family violence intervention order, at the time of his alleged serious re-offending against her, the court was satisfied that he posed an unacceptable risk of harm, which could not be mitigated by a long list of conditions, if released on bail. [60]

Re Bertucci [2020] VSC 88 *previous FV even if not made out is relevant to future risk*
In a bail application where the central allegation involves family violence, a court may have regard to past instances where the complainant has made allegations of family violence against the applicant. The fact that they were withdrawn or not made out does not mean they are not relevant. They are material as they may throw light on the risk of future endangerment to the complainant and her children. [58]-[59]

Re JL [2020] VSC 785 *FV order may not be sufficient to mitigate risk*
Since preventing family violence in the community can be difficult, because of the reluctance of victims to come forward or their being easily dissuaded from pursuing complaints, the presence of a family violence intervention order may not be sufficient to mitigate the risks an applicant poses of committing further family violence which can be sufficiently ameliorated by conditions on bail. Particularly if the applicant has a history of poor compliance with previous court orders and a history of family violence. [55]-[57]

Re Villani [2021] VSC 638 *Availiability of treatment alone may not be enough*
The availability of a residential drug treatment will not always have force. In some cases, the alleged offending may be so serious that the risk to the physical safety of the community inherent in the release of the accused into a non-secure residential treatment facility may not be justified. [64]
At a minimum, sworn evidence from a person with detailed knowledge of the assessment made about the accused’s suitability for treatment, and the details of the treatment, will be of assistance to a court considering their release on bail with a residential treatment condition. [66]

Re JP [2024] VSC 691 *Absence of criminal record in FV cases is not unusual*
A bail condition must be consistent with any active family violence intervention order unless the court concludes that a proposed condition will better protect the safety or welfare of an alleged victim or protected person. [58]
The absence of a criminal record is not exceptional in cases involving family violence and the breakdown of an intimate relationship where it is not unusual for an accused to have no or a limited criminal record. [67]

Re Troon [2024] VSC 607 *A lack of criminal history alone does not establish compelling reason*
The lack of a criminal history supports, but is not itself establish, the existence of compelling reason. [31]
