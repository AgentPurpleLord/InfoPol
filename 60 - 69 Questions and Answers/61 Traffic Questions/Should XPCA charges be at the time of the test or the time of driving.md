It should be at the time of the test if the charge is a *within three hours* charge.

From Director of Public Prosecutions v Drucker (1997) 98 A Crim R 142:
>"“The relevant time” for the purposes of [s 50(1AB)](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U7&docFamilyGuid=I09ebefb0045111e99495db3043f758b0&pubNum=1100190&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&docVersion=Law+in+Force&ppcid=53c6a74082924ae6b471d315d7b12823&contextData=\(sc.DocLink\)) varies according to the particular offence. The magistrate was concerned with an offence under [s 49(1)(f)](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U7&docFamilyGuid=I09e38b1e045111e99495db3043f758b0&pubNum=1100190&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&docVersion=Law+in+Force&ppcid=53c6a74082924ae6b471d315d7b12823&contextData=\(sc.DocLink\)) of the Act. In my opinion, “the relevant time” under that paragraph refers to the time when the result of the analysis of the sample of breath as recorded was shown by the breath analysing instrument. In the present case the evidence clearly established that at the hour of 35 minutes past midnight on 7 June 1996 the reading was 0.113 g of alcohol per 100 ml of blood. That was the relevant time and that was the blood alcohol reading. Evidence which disputed that result would be relevant under [s 50(1AB)](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U7&docFamilyGuid=I09ebefb0045111e99495db3043f758b0&pubNum=1100190&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&docVersion=Law+in+Force&ppcid=53c6a74082924ae6b471d315d7b12823&contextData=\(sc.DocLink\))."
