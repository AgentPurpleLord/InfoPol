# Issue
If a victim is not supportive of charges and can make a section 18 Evidence Act application, can we adduce evidence of their DREC?

# Rule
[[Unavailability]]
[[Probative value]]
[[Section 18 Compellability of Spouses and Others]]
[[Section 65 Exception-criminal proceedings if maker not available]]
[[Section 137 Exclusion of prejudicial evidence in criminal proceedings]]


Norris v The Queen [2018] VSCA 137
“Where the trial judge finds on voir dire that a witness is genuinely fearful for their safety and excuses them, that witness is ‘not available’ under s 65(1), and their committal evidence may be admissible if it satisfies the reliability and fairness requirements of s 65 and s 137.” — [85†Norris, [17]–[18], [26]].

Lewis v The Queen [2018] VSCA 40
“Where a de facto partner is excused under s 18 of the _Evidence Act 2008 (Vic)_, they are ‘not available’ for the purposes of s 65(1), and their police statements may be admissible if the requirements of s 65(2)(b) or (c) are satisfied.” — [87†Lewis, [19]–[22]].

- Accused on trial in the County Court, 12 charges, family violence related assaults. Charge 10 related to persistent contraventions for contacting 'SW' by phone from prison.
- Charge 11 and 12 related to the accused attempting to dissuade 'SW' from giving evidence against him.
- 'SW' was a central prosecution witness in that trial.
- 'SW' made two statements to police concerning the events relating to Charge 11 and 12, as well as a further three statements in relation to other matters.
- 'SW' gave evidence that she did not wish to be called as a witness for prosecution in a preliminary argument. 'SW' invoked s. 18 of the Evidence Act. 
- The trial judge ruled in 'SW's favour and she was not required to give evidence.
- Prosecution gave notice under s 65 EA that it would seek to tender the two statements that 'SW' made to police in June 2016 (Charge 11 / 12) and the further three statements. 
- [19] The judge ruled in favour of the prosecution's application to lead evidence of SW's statements. Her Honour rejected the submission that SW was not relevantly “not available” to give evidence about the asserted facts. In her opinion, that submission was foreclosed by the decision of this Court in [Fletcher v The Queen](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U4&serNum=2036468916&pubNum=0004888&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&ppcid=188749a89aa34a7b8d5271ac3aea5fe2&contextData=\(sc.DocLink\)),[3](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U2&serNum=2043900869&pubNum=0006102&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&ppcid=defb56094d864138884cef4747928b71&contextData=\(sc.Keycite\)&comp=wlau#co_footnote_1204465~FULLTEXT~FTNT.!2) where a majority (John Dixon AJA with whom Weinberg JA agreed) held that where a de facto partner had been excused from giving evidence pursuant to s 18 of the Evidence Act, that person was “not available” for the purpose of s 65(1).

DPP v Nicholls [2010] VSC 397
“A complainant who is excused under s 18 of the _Evidence Act 2008 (Vic)_ due to potential harm is ‘not available’ for the purpose of s 65(1), and their signed police statement may be admissible as hearsay if the reliability criteria in s 65(2) are met.” — [86†Nicholls, [6], [13]].
# Application
If the victim achieves a s. 18 application, the below applies. If the application is unsuccessful and she is called, we would tender the DREC as the evidence-in-chief. 

**EA 2008 - Part 1 - Definitions - Cl 4 (1)(f)**: Victim will be called; all reasonable steps have then been taken to locate, secure victim attendance, but would be without success as s. 18 application would be made and might succeed.

**Lewis v The Queen [2018] VSCA 40**: If excused under s. 18, previous statements are admissible where s. 65(2)(b) or (c) apply.

| Case                 | Ratio                                                                         | Key Paragraph   |
| -------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------- |
| _Lewis v The Queen_  | s 18 unavailability = s 65(1) availability; statements admissible if reliable | [19]–[22]       |
| _Norris v The Queen_ | Witness excused due to fear = unavailable; committal evidence admissible      | [17]–[18], [26] |
| _DPP v Nicholls_     | s 18 excusal creates unavailability under s 65(1)                             | [6], [13]       |

**s. 65(2)(b)/(c)**: The DREC was provided within hours of the event and on first police attendance. The victim made a 000-call which corroborated her statement. The DREC depicts the victim who appears to be in a state of shock. The victim attested to the truthfulness of her DREC and narrated the events chronologically. There is further corroborative evidence with scorch marks near the front door, the accused shorts being burned and the accused having minor burn marks on his person. 

**s. 137**: Probative value of this evidence is high; it is the only witness to the offence; it provides direct observations of the offending. The evidence has *rational impact* upon the assessment of the probability of the fact in issue. YH or Jury can be given directions regarding the weight placed upon evidence.

# Conclusion
- DREC admissible under s 65 of EA; complainant would be unavailable if s. 18 successful or otherwise compelled to give evidence.
- Corroborative evidence and contemporaneous circumstances make strong argument for admissibility.
