# Elements
1. The offence occurred at the time and place alleged.
2. The offender was the accused.
3. That the accused intentionally distributed child abuse material.
4. That the accused knew the material distributed described or depicted a person who is, or who appears or is implied to be, a child as a victim of torture, cruelty or physical abuse; or a victim of sexual abuse; or engaged in a sexual pose or activity; or in the presence of another person engaged in sexual activity; or the genital or anal region of a person who is or appears to be or is implied to be a child; or the breast area of a person who is, appears to be or is implied to be a female child; and
5. Reasonable persons would regard as being, in the circumstances, offensive. 

# Explanatory Memorandum
New section 51D – Distributing child abuse material 
New section 51D replaces the offence in section 57A of the Classification (Publications, Films and Computer Games) (Enforcement) Act 1995. It locates the offence more logically with the principal child abuse material offences in the Crimes Act 1958, clarifies the meaning of the offence, and broadens its scope to cover conduct that may occur using the internet.
New section 51D(1) provides that a person (A) commits an offence if— 
(a) A intentionally distributes material; and 
(b) the material is child abuse material; and 
(c) A knows that the material is, or probably is, child abuse material. 
New section 51D(2) provides that the maximum penalty for this offence is Level 5 imprisonment (10 years maximum). The same penalty applies to all of the child abuse material offences. 
As Note 1 provides, new section 51A(2)(b) defines distribute. It is a broad definition that would include sending child abuse material in an email to another person, whether over the internet or other means (such as a local area network), and uploading material to a website where it was available for viewing or download by any other person. 
New section 51D(3) provides that it is unnecessary to prove the identity of any person to whom material was, for example, exhibited, or to prove that another person in fact accessed the material. For example, if A uploads child abuse material to a website where it is accessible to other viewers of the website, it is not necessary to prove that a particular person did access the material in order to prove the offence. 
New section 51D(4) and (5) give the offence extra-territorial application, so long as one of the key aspects of the offence is, or occurs, in Victoria. This will enable Victorian police to charge a person with the offence even if that person was interstate when the material was distributed, so long as the material was distributed in Victoria, for example by sending an email to a person in Victoria. 
Notes 2−4 refer to exceptions and defences that apply to this offence, and to what is not a defence.
