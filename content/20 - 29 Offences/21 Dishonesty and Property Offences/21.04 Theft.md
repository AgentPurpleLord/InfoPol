> [!INFO]
> [s. 74 Crimes Act](http://www8.austlii.edu.au/cgi-bin/viewdoc/au/legis/vic/consol_act/ca195882/s74.html "Theft") — A **person** steals if he **dishonestly** **[[Appropriated|appropriates]]** **property** **belonging to another** with the **intention of permanently depriving** the other of it. A person who steals is guilty of theft.

> [!SUCCESS]
> The Victorian Judicial College has a [Charge Book section](http://www.judicialcollege.vic.edu.au/eManuals/CCB/index.htm#5094.htm "Charge Book - Theft") relating to this matter.

## Example

Arty goes to the local 7/11 Store and pockets a Mars bar inside the store, leaving the store without making any attempts to pay.

## Power of Arrest

[Section 459 of the Crimes Act.](http://www8.austlii.edu.au/cgi-bin/viewdoc/au/legis/vic/consol_act/ca195882/s459.html "Section 459 of the Crimes Act.")

## Jurisdiction

Indictable Offence Triable Summarily (#iots) heard at the Magistrate's Court for offences up to $100,000.  
County Court for offences above $100,000 (#indictable).

## Defences

- A belief they had a right to deprive the item.
- A belief they had consent to deprive the item.
- The owner could not be located taking reasonable steps.

## Points of Proof

1. Identity
2. Dishonestly
3. Appropriates
4. Property
5. Belonging to Another
6. Intention to Permanently Deprive

## Charge

The accused at **PLACE** on **DATE** did steal **PROPERTY** being property belonging to **NAME** and valued at **DOLLARS**.

## Judicial College Notes

- Appropriation of Property Belonging to Another:
  - The accused must appropriate *something*; and
  - That *something* must be *property*; and
  - That *property* must belong to another *person*.
- Theft **must** involve appropriation either by:
  - Assuming any rights of the owner (Crimes Act 1958 s 73(4)); or
  - Adversely interfered with or usurped the owners rights in some way (Roffel v R [1985] VR 511).
- Assuming the rights of property requires:
  - Taking on the right to do something which the owner has the right to do by virtue of his or her ownership (Stein v Henshall [1976] VR 612).
  - The rights of an owner are determine with the civil law of property.
  - The rights generally include the right to control the property and the right to possess it (see, e.g. Roffel v R [1985] VR 511; Stein v Henshall [1976] VR 612; W (a child) v Woodrow [1988] VR 358).
    - This requirement is satisfied if the accused taken on **any** right. Prosecution does not need to prove all rights.
    - Appropriation can involve taking, using, damaging, extinguishing, lending, retaining or offering to sell another person's property.
- Adverse interference with the owner's rights:
  - The accused does not appropriate the property if she or he acted with the owners consent (R v Morris [1984] AC 320; Roffel v R [1985] VR 511).
  - However, it can be considered an appropriation if that consent was real and induced by fraud, deception or false representation (Lawrence v Metropolitan Police Commissioner [1972] AC 626; R v Baruday [1984] VR 685; R v Gomez [1993] VR 685).
    - In Lawrence v Metropolitan Police Commissioner, an Italian student caught a taxi and the taxi driver overcharged him. As the taxi driver knew the cost of the fare and overcharged him, it was dishonest behavior and therefore, appropriated the over-cost cash.
    - In R v Baruday, an insurance broker utilised false cheques for a workers compensation insurance policy.
    - In R v Gomez, an example of this was an assistant shop manager (Gomez) using stolen cheques to purchase goods from a shop manager (the cheques being the deception).
