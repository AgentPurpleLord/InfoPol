# Section 6 - Commencement of a criminal proceeding in the Magistrates' Court

> (1) A criminal proceeding is commenced—
> > (a) by filing a charge-sheet containing a charge with a registrar of the Magistrates' Court; or
> >
> > (b) if the accused is arrested without a warrant and is released on bail, by filing a charge-sheet containing a charge with a bail justice; or
> >
> > (c) if a summons is issued under section 14, at the time the charge-sheet is signed.
> >
> (2) If the charge-sheet is filed in accordance with the method prescribed by the rules of court for electronic filing, the requirements of section 8(1) of the Electronic Transactions Act 2000 are taken to have been met.
>
> (3) A charge-sheet must—
> > (a) be in writing; and
> >
> > (b) be signed by the informant personally; and
> >
> > (c) comply with Schedule 1.

## Practical Application

When a criminal proceeding comences is vital. Charge-sheets which are incorrectly listed can lead to significant issues later on in a prosecution.

### Signing Charge-Sheets

An accused faces three common dispositions:

1. Charge & Summons
2. Charge & Bail
3. Charge & Remand

#### Charge & Summons

A charge-sheet issued under Section 14 of the Criminal Procedure Act 2009 commences *on the date you sign the charge*.

The signature in question is **top** signature on the charge-sheet used by Victoria Police. 

There is a second signature box for *issuing* the charge, which is not in the prescribed form for a Charge & Summons under the [Magistrates' Court Criminal Procedure Rules 2019](https://content.legislation.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-12/19-143sra008-authorised.pdf) (see page 54, or 'Form 3').

It is still a requirement that the person who signs the charge-sheet is the same person who issues the charges under s 14 (as in, the informant).

#### Charge & Bail or Remand

The criminal proceeding is commenced once the charge is *filed with the court*. This occurs when the charge is sent to the court.
