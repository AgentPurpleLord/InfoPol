> 1. Personal service of a document must be completed in accordance with (2), (3) or (4) unless an Act (or this Act) says otherwise.
> 2. Personal service is effected by—
> 	1. **giving a copy of the document to the person** to be served; or
> 	2. **putting the copy down in the person's presence** and telling the person the nature of the document; or
> 	3. **leaving a copy** of the document for the person at their last known or usual place of residence **with someone 16 years or older**.
> 3. If the person to be served is in detention or immigration detention, personal service may be effected by sending a copy of the document by registered post addressed to the person at the place of detention.
> 4. If a legal practitioner has given written notice to the informant/prosecution that they represent the accused, and that they are instructed to accept personal service of the document on the accused's behalf, personal service may be effected by—
> 	1. **giving copy to legal practitioner**; or
> 	2. l**eaving copy at ordinary place of business** with person who appears to work there; or
> 	3. **sending copy by registered post to legal practitioners** ordinary place of business; or
> 	4. **delivering copy by fax or email to legal practitioner** if consent is provided to do so


>[!ABSTRACT]+ Section 391 - Simplified
>- Personal service can be completed in a number of ways which are listed above.
>- In short; if you cannot give a document to a person *in person*, then you can leave it where they live with an person 16 or older; or
>- You can contact their legal practitioner, get permission and then send it in the post, via email or fax as an alternative which is still considered 'personal service'.
