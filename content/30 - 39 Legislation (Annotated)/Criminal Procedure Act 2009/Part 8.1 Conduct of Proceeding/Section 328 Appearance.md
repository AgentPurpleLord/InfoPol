>A party to a criminal proceeding may appear-
>1. personally; or
>2. by a legal practitioner or other person empowered by law to appear for the party; or
>3. in the Magistrates' Court, in the case of an informant who is a police officer or PSO, by a police prosecutor; or
>4. if the proceeding was commenced by the filing, by a prescribed person or a member of a prescribed class of persons, of a charge-sheet containing a charge for an offender under-
>	1. Division 1 of Part 3C or Schedule 3 of the Sentencing Act 1991; or
>	2. section 160 of the Fines Reform Act 2014-
>by any other prescribed person or any other member of the prescribed class of persons within the meaning of the Sentencing Act 1991 or the Fines Reform Act 2014, as the case requires.

>[!ABSTRACT]+ Section 328 - Simplified
>This section relates to how persons involved in a criminal proceeding must appear (or go to) the Court.
>A party could be the accused person or the informant, the officer that charged the accused.
>- Section 328(c) enables sworn Police Officers to attend on behalf of the Informant as a party. This is essentially what allows Police Prosecutors to exist.
