*credibility* of a person who has made a representation that has been admitted in evidence means the credibility of the representation, and includes the person's ability to observe or remember facts and events about which the person made the representation.

*credibility* of a witness means the credibility of any part or all of the evidence of the witness, and includes the witness's ability to observe or remember facts and events about which the witness has given, is giving or is to give evidence.


- The difference between this is highlighted in the practical example of a Doctor who would have given evidence were he not dead. We can admit that evidence on a hearsay rule, but we still need to prove that the Doctor was a credible person.
- *Believability* and *Reliability* are the two components.
	- Believability is whether they have lied, their priors, etc.
	- Reliability is vision impairment, drunkenness, etc.

# Dictionary
The dictionary defines *credibility* as being "the quality of being trusted and believed in" or the "quality of being convincing or believable."
