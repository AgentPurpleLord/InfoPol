> In a criminal proceeding, the court *must* refuse to admit evidence adduced by the prosecutor if its [[Probative value]] is outweighed by the danger of unfair prejudice to the accused.

- Must be a criminal proceeding; and
- Must be evidence adduced by a Prosecutor.
- This is a mandatory exclusion, if something is found to be unfairly prejudicial, it's out.

# Onus on accused
R v DG (2010) 28 VR 127 held:
>The submission by counsel for the accused that the onus rests throughout upon the Crown when [s 137](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U7&docFamilyGuid=I4113349b037711e99495db3043f758b0&pubNum=1100190&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&docVersion=Law+in+Force&ppcid=d42ba781ccca4cf68e567bc51a39e098&contextData=\(sc.Category\)) is invoked is directly in conflict with the authorities. The New South Wales Court of Criminal Appeal has determined that, despite the fact that the section is couched in mandatory terms, and is not in that sense a discretion, the onus lies upon the accused to persuade the trial judge that the danger of unfair prejudice from the evidence outweighs its probative value. Of course, once that is established, there is no further latitude given, and the evidence must be excluded.


# Reliability of evidence is relevant to discretion
IMM (2016) 257 CLR 300 held that *probative value* of evidence within s 137 must assume that the evidence is reliable and credible. 

# Meaning of prejudice
Moore v The King [2024] HCA 30 at [33]:
>The interim report [underpinning the Evidence Act] described “prejudice” as “danger that the fact-finder may over-estimate the probative value of the evidence” or may decide the matter on an improper basis, “ie on a basis logically unconnected with the issues in the case”.[68] Neither the interim report nor the final report identified the absence of an ability to cross-examine the maker of a representation as a form of potential prejudice that might warrant the exclusion of the evidence of the representation. Having regard to the discussion of prejudice in the interim report, in Papakosmas v The Queen[69] McHugh J doubted whether unfair prejudice extended to “procedural disadvantages”, such as the inability to cross-examine the maker of the representation.
