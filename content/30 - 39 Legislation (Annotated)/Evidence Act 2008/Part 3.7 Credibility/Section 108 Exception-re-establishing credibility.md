> 1. The credibility rule does not apply to evidence adduced in re-examination of a witness.
> 2. REPEALED
> 3. The credibility rule does not apply to evidence of a prior consistent statement of a witness if—
> 	1. evidence of a prior inconsistent statement of the witness has been admitted; or
> 	2. it is or will be suggested (either expressly or by implication) that evidence given by the witness has been fabricated or re-constructed (whether deliberately or otherwise) or is the result of a suggestion—
> 	   
>    and the court gives leave to adduce the evidence of the prior consistent statement.


- For example; if the informant is cross-examined about their credibility, Prosecution can use re-examination to re-establish their credibility. Remember the rule of re-examination, you can only ask questions on things that were brought up during the cross.

## Example
- Call Prosecution witness.
- Cross-examined re: credibility issues.
- Defence accuse witness being a drug user and can't remember what happened.
- When you re-examine, permitted to ask questions that indicate witness is no longer a drug user.
