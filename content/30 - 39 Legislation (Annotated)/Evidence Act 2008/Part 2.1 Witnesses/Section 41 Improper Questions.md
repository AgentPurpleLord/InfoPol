>(1) Court **must disallow improper questions** put to witness during cross-examination or inform witness doesn't need to answer.
>(3) Improper question/questioning is question or sequence that-
>	(a) **misleading**, **confusing**; or
>	(b) unduly **annoying**, **harassing**, **intimidating**, **offensive**, **oppressive**, **humiliating** or **repetitive**; or
>	(c) put to witness in manner or tone that is **belittling**, **insulting** or otherwise **inappropriate**; or
>	(d) no basis other than **stereotype** (e.g. sex, race, culture, ethnicity, age, mental, intellectual or physical disability)
>(5) Question is not improper merely because-
>	(a) question **challenges** truthfulness/consistency/accuracy of **statement** by witness; or
>	(b) question requires witness to discuss subject **considered** **distasteful** to witness.
>(6) Party **may object** to question on improper question grounds.
>(7) Duty imposed on court by this section applies whether or not objection is made.
>(8) Failure by court to disallow question or inform witness not to answer **does not affect admissibility** of evidence if witness responds. 

It's on the Court to ultimately declare an improper question, but any party can make an objection on this basis. If the court or party don't object and evidence is given before realising improper question is applicable, it's still admissible.
