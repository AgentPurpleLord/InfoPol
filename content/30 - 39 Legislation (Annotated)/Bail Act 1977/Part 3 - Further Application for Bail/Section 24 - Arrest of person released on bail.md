>[!NOTE] Legislation Is Long
>The original legislative text for [Section 24 of the Bail Act 1977 ](https://www8.austlii.edu.au/cgi-bin/viewdoc/au/legis/vic/consol_act/ba197741/s24.html) is lengthy. I have reduced it to its basic points below.

>1. A MOPF (Police/PSO on duty designated place), may without warrant, arrest any person who is on bail-
>	1. if MOPF has BORG that person likely to breach bail; or has SORG that person is currently breaching/has breached;
>	2. if MOPF is notified by guarantor in writing that the guarantor has BORG that person likely to breach bail and guarantor wishes to be released from obligations; or
>	3. if MOPF has BORG for believing guarantor is dead / security no longer sufficient.
>2. If PSO arrests, must hand over person to Police ASAP.
>3. PSO may only utilise s.24 at designated place / vicinity of.
>4. If person arrested, police must-
>	1. bring person before bail justice ASAP / within 24 hours; or
>	2. if person arrested is bound to attend court within 24 hours of the time of arrest, bring them before the court at that time.
>5. Where person brought to bail justice / court and where bail justice / court-
>	1. is of the opinion that person breached / likely to breach bail, they may revoke the bail and remand person in custody.
>6. If person is a child, they may only be remanded for 21 clear days.

# Key Points
The power granted to Police and PSO's under Section 24 is most relevant after the March 2024 reforms. Those reforms repealed two key offences; committing an indictable offence whilst on bail and contravene bail conduct conditions. This has left many to presume we are powerless when a person breaches their bail.

- If you find a person breaching their bail conditions:
	- Arrest them under Section 24 of the Bail Act 1977 as you have BORG / SORG they are likely to breach or have breached their bail conditions; and
	- Bring them before the court within 24 hours, or a bail justice, seeking to remand them for doing so.
	- You (potentially) won't lay any charges against the accused, which is ok.
	- Exceptional Circumstances and Show Compelling Reasons may not exist, which is ok.
	- With the changes made to Unacceptable Risk, you may not be able to argue that the accused is an unacceptable risk either. 
	- Your remand argument branches entirely from Section 24 of the Bail Act, and you would outline as such:
		- The accused was arrested under Section 24 of the Bail Act 1977 as Senior Constable BLOGGS witnessed the accused on the 7-Eleven premises situated at 20 Davis Street, Melbourne, which the accused has a bail condition not to be within 200 metres of.
