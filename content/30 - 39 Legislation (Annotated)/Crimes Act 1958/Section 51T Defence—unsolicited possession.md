# CRIMES ACT 1958 - SECT 51T Defence—unsolicited possession

> It is a defence to a charge for an offence against section 51G(1) for A to prove on the balance of probabilities that—
> > (a) A did not intentionally come into possession of child abuse material; and
> >
> > (b) on becoming aware of having come into possession of child abuse material, A, as soon as it was practicable to do so, took all reasonable steps in the circumstances to cease possessing the material.

## [Explanatory Memorandum - Crimes Amendment (Sexual Offences) Bill 2016](https://www.legislation.vic.gov.au/bills/crimes-amendment-sexual-offences-bill-2016)

> New section 51T provides that it is a defence to a charge for an offence against section 51G(1) (possession of child abuse material) for A to prove on the balance of probabilities that—
> > (a) A did not intentionally come into possession of the child abuse material; and
> >
> > (b) on becoming aware of having come into possession of child abuse material, A, as soon as it was practicable to do so, took all reasonable steps in the circumstances to cease possessing the material.
>
> This defence is based on similar interstate provisions that provide a defence for the limited circumstance of unsolicited possession (e.g. section 130E(3) of the Criminal Code of Tasmania).
>
> For example, A receives an unsolicited email containing a child abuse material image.  Upon realising the contents of the email, A immediately takes reasonable steps to cease possessing the child abuse material, such as deleting the email permanently.
>
> The Note confirms that A in this section is the same A referred to in section 51G.
