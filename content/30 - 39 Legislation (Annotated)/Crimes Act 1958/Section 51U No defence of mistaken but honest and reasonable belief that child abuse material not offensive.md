# CRIMES ACT 1958 - SECT 51U No defence of mistaken but honest and reasonable belief that child abuse material not offensive

> It is not a defence to a charge for a child abuse material offence that, at the time of the conduct constituting the offence, A was under a mistaken but honest and reasonable belief that reasonable persons would not regard the child abuse material as being, in the circumstances, offensive.

## [Explanatory Memorandum - Crimes Amendment (Sexual Offences) Bill 2016](https://www.legislation.vic.gov.au/bills/crimes-amendment-sexual-offences-bill-2016)

> New section 51U provides that it is not a defence to a charge for a child abuse material offence that, at the time of the conduct constituting the offence, A was under a mistaken but honest and reasonable belief that reasonable persons would not regard the child abuse material as being, in the circumstances, offensive.
>
> This makes clear that an element in any child abuse material offence that a person (A) knows that material is, or probably is, child abuse material, does not apply to paragraph (b) of the definition of child abuse material, and the defence of honest and reasonable mistake of fact does not apply to paragraph (b).
>
> This means that the element of each of these offences (that reasonable persons would regard the material as being offensive) is an absolute liability element.
>
> This means that the prosecution do not need to prove that A knew, or probably knew, that reasonable persons would regard the material as being offensive. It also means that A does not have a defence if A had a reasonable but mistaken belief that reasonable persons would not regard the material as being offensive. Whether reasonable persons would regard material as offensive is intended to be an objective standard. It would defeat this purpose (and be very complicated) if a defence applied where the accused mistakenly believed that reasonable persons would not find material offensive.
>
> The Note confirms that A in this section is the same A referred to in the relevant offence provisions.
