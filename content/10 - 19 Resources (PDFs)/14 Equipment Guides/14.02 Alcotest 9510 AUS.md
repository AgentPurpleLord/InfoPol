# Conditions of Non-Compliance 
1. **Alcohol in Mouth** (Wait 15 mins)
2. **Interferent** Detected (Wait 15 mins)
3. **Range Exceeded** (Wait 15 mins)
4. Check **Airway** (hose blocked)
5. **Zero** Test Error
	1. Instrument is picking up alcohol in ambient air, e.g. subject is too close.
6. **Blowing** Not Allowed
	1. Subject Blows -> Sucks in Air -> Blows Again
7. Alcohol **Concentration** Not Stable
	1. Alcohol concentration has not plateaued (e.g. not enough air) advise subject to blow until bar is full.
8. Minimum **Volume** Not Achieved
	1. Insufficient breath sample
9. **Readiness** to Blow **Expired**
	1. No sample provided within 2 mins.
10. Blowing Time Too **Short**
	1. Insufficient breath sample
11. **Sucking** Not Allowed

# How Does It Work?
**Henry's Law** is the key principle behind alcohol breath analysis.
1. After consuming alcohol, it enters your **bloodstream**.
2. Your blood circulates through your **lungs**.
3. Some of the alcohol molecules move out of the blood and into the **air sacs** in your lungs.
4. When you **breath out**, those air sacks **release** the alcohol particles.

A majority of **Breath** Alcohol Concentration (often referred to as 'BrAC') instruments use a ratio to assist in the determination of their **Blood** Alcohol Concentration (often referred to as 'BAC').

**BrAC = BAC * 2100**

We are estimating their **Blood** Alcohol Level based on their **Breath** Alcohol Level with the above equation.
