## Search Without Warrant (DPCSA s.82)

>[!info]- Sub-Section (1) - Search Power for Drug of Dependence
>(1)    Where a police officer has reasonable grounds for suspecting that—
>
>    (a) on or in a vehicle in or upon a [[Public Place]]; or
>    (b) on an animal in a [[Public Place]]; or
>    (c) in the [[possession]] of a person in a [[Public Place]]; or
>    (d) on or in a boat or vessel, underway or not; or
>    (e) on or in an aircraft—
>    
>there is a drug of dependence in respect of which an offence has been committed or is reasonably suspected to have been committed under a provision of Part V, the police officer may with such assistance as he thinks necessary—
>
>    (f) search the vehicle, animal, person, boat, vessel or aircraft;
>    (g) seize and carry away any instrument device or [substance](http://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/vic/consol_act/dpacsa1981422/s4.html#substance) which he reasonably believes to be used or capable of being used for or in the [manufacture](http://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/vic/consol_act/dpacsa1981422/s70.html#manufacture), sale, preparation for [manufacture](http://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/vic/consol_act/dpacsa1981422/s70.html#manufacture), preparation for sale, or use of any drug of dependence;
>    (h) seize and carry away the drug of dependence—
>    
>and deal with it according to law

>[!info]- Sub-Section (2) - Search Power for Psychoactive Substance
>(2)     If a police officer has reasonable grounds for suspecting that there is a psychoactive [substance](http://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/vic/consol_act/dpacsa1981422/s4.html#substance)—
>
>    (a) on or in a vehicle in a [public place](http://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/vic/consol_act/dpacsa1981422/s70.html#public_place); or
>    (b) on an animal in a [public place](http://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/vic/consol_act/dpacsa1981422/s70.html#public_place); or
>    (c) in the [[possession]] of a person in a public place; or
>    (d) on or in a boat or vessel, underway or not; or
>    (e) on or in an aircraft—
>    
>the police officer may with any assistance that the police officer thinks necessary do any of the following—
>    
>    (f) search the vehicle, animal, person, boat, vessel or aircraft;
>    (g) seize and carry away any instrument, device or [substance](http://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/vic/consol_act/dpacsa1981422/s4.html#substance) which the police officer reasonably believes to be used or capable of being used for or in the production, sale, [commercial supply](http://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/vic/consol_act/dpacsa1981422/s80zf.html#commercial_supply) or preparation for sale or commercial supply of any psychoactive [substance](http://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/vic/consol_act/dpacsa1981422/s4.html#substance);
>    (h) seize and carry away the psychoactive [substance](http://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/vic/consol_act/dpacsa1981422/s4.html#substance)—
>    
>and deal with it according to law.

### Summary 
>[!Note] PSO Powers
Under Section 82A, PSO's may exercise these powers, except for searching a boat, vessel or aircraft & they may not carry away anything seized (must be handed to police).

- Where a **police officer** has SORG that a [[Drug of Dependence]] is:
	- on / in **vehicle** in a public place; or
	- on an **animal** in a public place; or
	- in the [[possession]] of a **person** in a public place; or
	- on or in a **boat** or **vessel**, underway or not; or
	- on or in an **aircraft**
- May **search** as they think necessary:
	- the **vehicle**; or
	- the **animal**; or
	- the **person**; or
	- the **boat**; or
	- the **vessel**; or
	- the **aircraft**
- May **seize** the following items from the result of the search:
	- Any **instrument** or **substance** which:
		- Is to be used or is capable of being used in the **manufacture** or **sale**; or
		- in the preparation of **manufacturing** or **sale**
	- Any **instrument** or **substance** which:
		- Is to be used or is capable of being used to consume a [[Drug of Dependence]]
	- Any [[Drug of Dependence]]
- And deal with the items seized according to law.
- Subsection (2) is as above but for **psychoactive substances**.


## Detaining for the Search 
I have queried Prosecution Frontline Support Unit regarding the exact nature of **how** we can detain a person under s.82. They have provided:

>[!info] From WestLaw Research re: Indictable Offences
> "Where police are **entitled to conduct a search** then it is generally **accepted** that they are able to use **reasonable force** to carry it into effect. 
>
>What is **reasonable force** can only be determined on the circumstances of the particular case, but if police exceed what is reasonable then they are liable for criminal assault and for a civil claim of trespass against the person. 
>
>Where **police are not entitled** to conduct a search then the **use of any force at all is unreasonable**. 
>
>Where police act under a genuine misapprehension of fact or law this may lessen the seriousness of the trespass and may even justify  a search on the basis the officer held the requisite reasonable belief. 
>
>However, where police have acted in disregard of the law and to excess the court s have expressed strong censure of their action."

There is a further argument to be made in relation to the legislation:
> [!QUOTE]
> Force can be implied via s.82(1) being: 
> 
> "The police officer may with such **assistance** as he thinks necessary search the vehicle, animal, person, boat, vessel or aircraft."

The key word being "assistance" as implied force. An argument is also made on the 'absurdity' ground, where if any act is so absurd (e.g. running away during a search which you must be present for), it doesn't need to be written in law to be justified. 

Were a person to resist or obstruct an officer, there is also an argument for a s.458 arrest to be made to prevent the offending from continuing.

# Reasonable Suspicion
>[!info]
>A reasonable suspicion involves less than a reasonable belief, but more than a possibility. Some factual basis must be shown before you can form suspicion on reasonable grounds.

>[!warning]
>A suspicion is a **'positive or actual apprehension or mistrust'** that requires more than **'mere idle wondering'**.

Section 82 should be interpreted as balancing the need for an effective criminal justice system against the desirability of protecting individuals from arbitrary interference with their person and property. Meaning you **MUST** consider:

- You are depriving the suspect of their liberty.
- This should only be done if you have formed a reasonable suspicion of a crime being committed.

## Murray, Hale and Olsen v The Queen (2017)
[The Supreme Court of Victoria's Court of Appeal heard a case in relation to a drug search of a vehicle conducted by a Sergeant and First Constable.](https://jade.io/article/546867 "Supreme Court Ruling")

On the 16th of February 2015, Murray, Hale and Olsen's vehicle was intercepted by Police and searched. The decision to search the vehicle was made by the Sergeant, who formed his suspicion from the following reasons:

- The attitude of the owner of the vehicle (Olsen) was 'almost identical' to a person who he knows who is a convicted drug dealer.
- When the Sergeant arrived, he saw Olsen had already left the vehicle and believed he had done so to dispose of something.
- The Sergeant believed that all three persons in the vehicle displayed nervous behaviour, such as squiring and fidgeting.
- The Sergeant had observed the vehicle turn sharply down a side-street and a believed speed of 60.

Police searched the car after forming their SORG and located in excess of $1,200 cash, four large bags (110.1 grams) of methamphetamine of 90% purity. Murray was also searched and was found to have $3,525 in his underwear, along with over 1kg of cocaine.

**As a result of these grounds for suspicion, the judge ruled:**
The Sergeant's suspicion came from his prior experience with offenders and that the suspicions he held could not have been held by that of a reasonable person.

### Findings
Based on case-law and legislation, the follow are my recommendations for conducting drug searches under Section 82:

1. You must form suspicion that a normal person could form; meaning it is not tainted by your prior experiences with drug dealers or experiences as a police officer.
2. You must weigh up whether the search and depriving liberties from a person is worth it in order to obtain a good criminal justice result.
3. If you cannot form your suspicion; DO NOT SEARCH.
