# Overview
The defence of **sudden or extraordinary emergency** is found at Section 322R of the Crimes Act 1958. 
- The person *reasonable believes* that circumstances of *sudden or extraordinary emergency exists* (**subjective**); and
- The person believed the conduct was the only reasonable way to deal with the emergency (**subjective**); and
- The conduct itself was a reasonable response to the emergency (**objective**).

# The nature of the emergency
The emergency can be either *sudden* or *extraordinary*, it does not need to be both.

The wording of "sudden or extraordinary emergency" is not defined or clarified in the Crimes Act. Given that the defence originates from the common law, it would be expected that the court would refer to those cases to assist them.

The common law only recognised the following as allowing the accused to use this defence:
- Threats of death; and
- Serious physical harm; and
- Sexual assault; and
- Suicide

# The accused's belief
An accused cannot be ignorant of the circumstances that gave rise to their conduct, in Limbo v Little (1989) 98 FLR 421:
>It is not relevant or permissible in seeking to establish the defence of necessity to attempt to prove facts which were not evident to the offender at the time of the offence.


# The reasonable person test
An accused's response to the situation is normally measured against the reaction of a reasonable person in the position of the accused.
