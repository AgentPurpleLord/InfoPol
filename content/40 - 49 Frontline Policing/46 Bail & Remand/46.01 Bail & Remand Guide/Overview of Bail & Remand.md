>[!Info]- [Section 4 of The Bail Act 1977](http://www8.austlii.edu.au/cgi-bin/viewdoc/au/legis/vic/consol_act/ba197741/s4.html) **Entitlement to bail**
>*"A person accused of an offence & being held in custody in relation to that offence, is **entitled to be granted bail** unless the [[Bail Decision Makers (BDM)]] is **required to refuse bail** by this Act (being the Bail Act 1977)"* 

The Remand process involves a #bail-decision-maker (*BDM*) deciding whether to grant a person #bail or have them #remanded to a future court date. 

The process uses *default logic*, whereby it is assumed everyone *should* be granted bail unless there is a reason otherwise. 

To determine this process, we refer to **Schedules** and **Tests** from the Bail Act 1977. By applying the facts of a scenario, we can determine what **Tests** are relevant for the BDM to consider.

> [!important] Important: The Most Severe Disposition
> Remand is the **most** severe disposition. Your written remand application **matters**. Thoroughness & attention to detail are crucial to avoid mistakes and obtain results.
