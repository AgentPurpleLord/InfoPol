# What is an Issue Cover Sheet?
The Issue Cover Sheet is intended to provide a brief overview regarding an issue for the attention or approval of management. Its use is preferred throughout the organisation, as it:
- Streamlines approval processes
- Eliminates the need for duplicate reports
- Supports and enables managers to make decisions at appropriate levels of the organisation
- Provides relevant and timely feedback to people putting forward recommendations

# When should an Issue Cover Sheet be used?
The aim of the Issue Cover Sheet, like other forms of business writing such as memorandums or reports, is to communicate information that helps others to complete tasks or make decisions. For example we write to inform, inquire, request, motivate, or persuade. (Petelin and Durham 1992: 11)

The Issue Cover Sheet can be used in a range of situations, particularly when an issue needs to be considered at a higher level or forwarded to another department for endorsement.

The Issue Cover Sheet can be used to:

- Provide answers or responses to specific questions or issues
- Keep others informed about major activities or projects
- Provide an executive summary of detailed reports (ie. Briefing notes, attachments, appendices etc)
- Help plan and coordinate activities
- Analyse the elements of an issue
- Instruct others or recommend a course of action
- Ensure accountability (Petelin and Durham 1992: 11)

**Format and Style**

A standard format is available on the VicPol Templates [Form 1028]. This form was recently revised to make it more user-friendly.

The Issue Cover Sheet should:

§  Be no more than one page in length — additional supporting material may be included as attachments or appendices

§  Include a file number reference

§  Define the issue and explain the facts

§  Present arguments and analysis clearly and logically

§  Be signed and dated by the author with a telephone number included for contact purposes

§  Include a distribution list following a chain of command that identifies decision-makers at appropriate levels of the organisation

# How the Issue Cover Sheet works

The circulation of the Issue Cover Sheet follows the order determined by the author. In determining the distribution list for the issue at hand, the author should consider the role and responsibilities of managers to identify the appropriate decision-makers.

To streamline approval processes it is important that the author limit the distribution list to those managers with the appropriate authorisation to make a decision about the subject matter. The author may consider sending a courtesy copy to relevant stakeholders.

Once the author submits the Issue Cover Sheet for endorsement, it then becomes the responsibility of each person on the distribution list to consider the information provided on the cover sheet, along with any supporting material, and decide whether to support or not support the proposal. The author's contact details are provided on the form if clarification is required at any stage of the process.

If recipients support the proposal, they should sign and date the cover sheet and forward it to the next person on the distribution list. Comments can be included on the cover sheet in the space available next to the recipient’s signature. **There is no need to prepare a separate memo to progress the Issue Cover Sheet.**

Once the Issue Cover Sheet has been circulated to all recipients on the distribution list, the document is then returned to the author for information and where necessary to the person nominated to implement the proposed action.

If recipients do not support or endorse the proposal, they should provide feedback to the author on their reasons for not supporting the proposal. The recipient can either contact the author directly or note the comments/suggestions on the Issue Cover Sheet and return to the author via the distribution list. This feedback will then help the author to reassess the proposal and consider how it might be progressed further.

The extent or significance of any changes required will determine how the issue is progressed. For example:

§  If the author makes minor amendments to the document in line with the comments/suggestions, the endorsement process would continue with the recipient who suggested the changes. In this case, a courtesy copy of the revised document, highlighting the amended sections, should be provided to the people on the distribution list who had already seen the document prior to the amendments being made.

§  If the author undertakes a major revision of key facts or aspects of the proposal, such as timeframes, costs or resources, the endorsement process should start again with the first person on the distribution list.

**What information is required in an Issue Cover Sheet?**

**File No:**

Central Records file number, correspondence or administration record.

**Issue:**

Provide a brief description of the issue or topic.

**Background:**

Provide the key facts about the topic or issue to give the reader the necessary information to address the issue. Present the facts in a logical sequence (chronological if possible) to enhance the reader’s understanding of the issue.

**Comment:**

Highlight the implications of the issue. Aim to tell the reader all they must know to make an informed and confident judgement about the issue. Present the analysis and argument clearly and logically. Be succinct but thorough.

To help the reader consider the issue, include references to any supporting file, folio or appendix. Attach copies of relevant supporting documents if necessary.

**Recommendation:**

Provide a list of recommendations to address the implications. These recommendations should outline the proposed action to be undertaken, identify who will be involved or responsible for implementation, and supply the timelines for commencement or finalisation.

**Distribution List:**

Enter recipient’s details (name and title) according to a chain of command that identifies decision-makers at appropriate levels of the organisation.
