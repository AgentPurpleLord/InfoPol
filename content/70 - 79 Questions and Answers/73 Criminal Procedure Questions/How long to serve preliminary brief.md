
# Issue
1. If an informant commences a criminal proceeding by charge-and-bail, when **must** a preliminary brief be served?
2. Can defence be awarded costs in the circumstances outlined below?

## Outline of Informants Issue
- The accused was arrested, charged and bailed on 29/11/2024 to attend the Magistrates' Court on 01/02/2025.
- The informant later received repeated requests over the course of a week for the preliminary brief to be disclosed, sent by the accused's solicitor.
- The informant replied numerous times, informing the solicitor the preliminary brief was not yet authorised.
- The solicitor replied with the following email a number of days later:

----

>To date you have still failed to provide a preliminary brief to the accused or his legal representation despite multiple requests from both parties. 
>
>Per legislation namely s 24 of the Criminal Procedure Act once a charge sheet is filed the Informant must serve a preliminary brief on the accused within 21 days after the day on which the charge sheet is file. As noted previously, the charge sheet in this matter was filed on 29 November which is 55 days (not inclusive of today) ago. 
>
>Our office has made multiple requests for this BOE in addition to the accused who has also made multiple attempts of his own to get the brief. 
>
>We require the brief promptly and by no later than 1 February 2025. If we do not receive this BOE by 01.02.25 we will be making an application for costs.

---

- The work-unit OIC was cc'd on the email. The OIC reached out to the local Prosecutions office. As a result, a half-completed preliminary brief was immediately disclosed whilst the informant was out of the office.
- The preliminary brief had been disclosed *within 14 days* of the initial request made by the solicitor.

# Rule
**Criminal Procedure Act 2009**
*s. 5 How a criminal proceeding is commenced*
>A criminal proceeding is commenced by—
>>==(a) filing or signing a charge-sheet in accordance with section 6; or==
>>(b) filing a direct indictment in accordance with section 159; or
>>(c) a direction under section 415 that a person be tried for perjury.


*s. 6 Commencement of a criminal proceeding in the Magistrates' Court*

>==(1) A criminal proceeding is commenced—==
>>==(a) by filing a charge-sheet containing a charge with a registrar of the Magistrates' Court; or==
>>(b) if the accused is arrested without a warrant and is released on bail, by filing a charge-sheet containing a charge with a bail justice; or
>>(c) if a summons is issued under section 14, at the time the charge-sheet is signed.
>
>(2) If a charge-sheet is filed in accordance with the method prescribed by the rules of court for electronic filing, the requirements of section 8(1) of the Electronic Transactions (Victoria) Act 2000 are taken to have been met.
>
>(3) A charge-sheet must—
>>(a) be in writing; and
>>(b) be signed by the informant personally; and
>>(c) comply with Schedule 1.
>
> (4) The informant may include a request for a committal proceeding in a charge-sheet containing a charge for an indictable offence that may be heard and determined summarily.

*21 Police or public official may serve notice to appear*
>(1) A police officer or a public official acting in the performance of his or her duties may serve on a person a notice to appear if the police officer or public official reasonably suspects that the person has committed—
>>(a) a summary offence; or
>>(b) an indictable offence that may be heard and determined summarily.
>
>(2) A notice to appear must—
>>(a) state the name of the person serving the notice; and
>>(b) if the notice is served by a public official, state the name of the employer of the public official; and
>>(c) state a telephone number and email address at which the person serving the notice may be contacted and the business address of the person and may state similar details for a person authorised to act in relation to the notice on behalf of the person serving the notice; and
>>(d) state the full name and address of the person served with the notice; and
>>(e) state the offence that the person served with the notice is suspected of having committed; and
>>(f) state in general terms the circumstances of the suspected offence; and
>>(g) direct the person served with the notice to appear at a venue of the Magistrates' Court on a date (at least 28 days after the date of service of the notice) and at a time specified in the notice to answer any charge in respect of the suspected offence; and
>>(h) include a summary of this Division; and
>>(i) be signed by the person serving the notice.
>
>(3) A notice to appear must be served by—
>>(a) giving it to the person to be served; or
>>(b) if the person does not accept it, by putting it down in the person's presence and telling the person the nature of the document.

*24 Preliminary brief to be served if charge-sheet filed*
>==If a charge-sheet is filed in accordance with section 22(1), the informant must—==
>>==(a) serve a preliminary brief on the accused within 21 days after the day on which the charge-sheet is filed; and==
>>(b) on the return date have available a copy of the preliminary brief for provision to the accused or the legal practitioner representing the accused, on request.

*35 When preliminary brief is to be served*
>(1) If required to do so by section 24, the informant must serve a preliminary brief on the accused.
>
>==(2) At any time after the commencement of a proceeding, the accused, by written notice to the informant, may request that a preliminary brief be served.==
>
>==(3) If the accused gives notice under subsection (2), the informant must serve on the accused a preliminary brief within 14 days after receipt of the notice.==
>
>==(4) Nothing in this section prevents the informant from serving a preliminary brief on the accused at any other time==

*401 Costs in Magistrates' Court*
>(1) Unless otherwise expressly provided by this or any other Act or by the rules of court, the costs of, and incidental to, all criminal proceedings in the Magistrates' Court are in the discretion of the court and the court has full power to determine by whom, to whom and to what extent the costs are to be paid.
>
>==(2) In exercising its discretion under subsection (1) in a criminal proceeding, the Magistrates' Court may take into account any unreasonable act or omission by, or on behalf of, a party to the proceeding that the court is satisfied resulted in prolonging the proceeding.==
>
>(3) If the Magistrates' Court strikes out a charge under section 14(3), the court may award costs against the informant.
>
>(4) This section and section 410 apply to a purported proceeding in the Magistrates' Court which is beyond the jurisdiction of the court as if the purported proceeding were within jurisdiction.
>
>(5) If the Magistrates' Court determines to award costs against an informant who is a police officer, the order must be made against the Chief Commissioner of Police.

*402 Notice to Appear*
>(1) If a notice to appear lapses under section 22(1) and notice is given in accordance with section 23, the police officer or public official who served the notice is not liable for any costs of the person served with the notice in respect of the serving or lapsing of the notice.
>
>(2) If a notice to appear lapses under section 22(1) and notice is not given in accordance with section 23, the Magistrates' Court may award costs against the police officer or public official who served the notice as if the notice to appear were a criminal proceeding in the Magistrates' Court.

**Lawyers Practice Manual VIC**
*1.3.402 Order of events in summary stream*
>The usual order of events is as follows:
>> ==The police serve the accused with a Notice to Appear and file a charge sheet (see [1.3.501]); _or_ the police charge and serve the accused with a summons (see [1.3.604]); _or_ the police charge the accused and either release them on bail or apply to have them remanded in custody (see [1.3.604]).==
>> The police/informant serve the accused with the preliminary brief (see [1.3.502]). This is mandatory in cases where a Notice to Appear has been served ([CPA, s 24]); but a preliminary brief is optional where a proceeding has been commenced by way of charge and summons or charge and bail ([CPA, s 35(4)]). If the accused requests a preliminary brief, it must be served within 14 days ([CPA, ss 35(2)]) and (3)).

*1.3.501 Notice to Appear and charge sheet*
>If a person is reasonably suspected of having committed a summary offence or an indictable offence that may be heard and determined summarily, a police officer or public official (the informant) may personally serve that person with a Notice to Appear ([s 21(1) of the Criminal Procedure Act 2009] ([CPA]), Rules 14 and 16, and Form 7, [Magistrates' Court Criminal Procedure Rules 2019 (Vic)] ([MCCPR]). However, note that the Notice to Appear does not actually commence a criminal proceeding ([CPA, s 26]); it is the filing of the charge sheet (see below) that commences the proceeding.
>If a Notice to Appear is used, then it must include:
>>- the name and address of the alleged offender;
>>- the name of the person serving the notice;
>>- the offence that the person is alleged to have committed;
>>- a direction that the person appear at a Magistrates' Court at a specified time and on a date (“the mention date”) at least 28 days after the date of service of the notice;
>>- in general terms, the circumstances of the suspected offence;
>>- the contact details for the police officer;
>>- a summary of [Division 1 of Part 2.3 of the Criminal Procedure Act 2009]; and
>>- the signature of the person serving the notice ([CPA, s 21(2)]).
>>  
>The Notice to Appear will lapse unless, within 14 days after it has been served, the police officer files (with the registrar of the Magistrates' Court) a charge sheet, a copy of the Notice to Appear and evidence of service ([CPA, s 22(1)]). A proceeding commences only when the charge sheet is filed.
>
>If the notice does lapse, then the police officer must ensure that written notice is given to the person that the charge sheet has not been filed and that they are not required to appear at court on the mention date. The police officer must also notify the court that the Notice to Appear has lapsed ([CPA, s 23]).
>
>The informant may choose to use a Notice to Appear in more straightforward matters, such as driving offences, where there is minimal additional investigation required.
>
>For information about taking instructions and other preliminary matters when an accused person seeks your advice and representation, see [1.3.601])–[1.3.625]], [1.3.625]).

*1.3.604 Court documents*
>Examine closely the court document(s) given to the client (see further below) in order to be clear about the charges, the court venue and the hearing date. A police officer or other prescribed person can issue the documents, either in person ([s 14(2)]), (3), (6) of the _Criminal Procedure Act 2009_ ([CPA]) or electronically ([CPA, s 6(3)]).
>
>If your client has not been given copies of the documents or has mislaid them, then you can obtain copies from the informant. If you do not know who the informant is, then check the Magistrates' Court website at “Go to Daily Law List and EFAS” – it should list the name of the informant and their location. The registrar of the Magistrates' Court is obliged to supply a copy of the charges ([CPA, s 32]. You may also request the informant to provide a copy of the brief before the mention date. An additional copy of the documents will be available at court on the first mention date ([CPA, s 53A]. The range of possible documents are:
>
>>==**Undertaking of Bail (bail notice)**. This will give the court venue, the hearing date and the conditions of bail (see sample at Appendix E at [1.3.3050]. The accused's copy may be blue or white but may not detail the charge(s) in full. It is unusual for this document to identify the informant.==
>>
>>**Charge and Summons**. This form ([CPA, s 13]); Rule 11, Form 3 of the [Magistrates' Court Criminal Procedure Rules 2019]) ([MCCPR]); see sample at Appendix F at [1.3.3060] will usually be served on the accused sometime after the incident but no later than 14 days before the mention date ([CPA, ss 16], [17]). If it is a summary matter, then process must be issued no later than 12 months after the date of the alleged offence ([CPA, s 7)]), unless there is a specific exception for that offence. The summons will contain details of the charge(s), the informant's name, address and telephone number, details of the court venue and the court's telephone number. The reverse of the court copy of this document carries information about proof of service. This document must also include a Form 6, which contains information about the availability of advice in a criminal proceeding in languages other than English ([Rule 12(1)(a) of the MCCPR]).
>>
>>**Charge**. This form ([CPA, s 13]; [Rule 11], Form 1 [MCCPR]; see sample at Appendix G at [1.3.3070]) will usually mean your client has been arrested (and may be on bail). The Charge form contains similar details to the Charge and Summons.
>>
>>**Charge and Warrant to Arrest**. This form ([CPA, ss 12]), [13]; [Rule 11], Form 5 [MCCPR]); see sample at Appendix H at [1.3.3080] indicates that the court has ordered the arrest of your client. It also includes details of the charge and of the informant's reasons for requesting a Warrant to Arrest.
>>
>>**Continuation of Charges**. This form ([Rule 11]), Form 2 of the [MCCPR] provides space for listing charges additional to those covered on the Charge and Summons and Charge.

# Application 

*s. 5 CPA*: Pursuant to s. 5(a), the informant commenced the criminal proceeding by way of charge-sheet and undertaking of bail.

*s. 6 CPA*: Pursuant to s. 6(1)(a); a criminal proceeding is commenced by filing a charge-sheet with the Registrar of the court.

*s. 21 CPA*: This does not apply. A **notice to appear** was not utilised to commence the proceeding. An **undertaking of bail** is not a notice to appear. 
`This process is seldom used by police. The vast majority of members commence proceedings by way of charge-and-summons or charge-and-bail.`

*s. 22 CPA*: This section also does not apply; as above.

*s. 24 CPA*: This section does not apply; as above.
`Were the proceeding to be commenced with a notice to appear, the informant has 21 days from filing the charge sheet to serve the preliminary brief.`

*Lawyers Practice Manual 1.3.402*: Clearly draws distinction between **notice to appear** and **charge** procedures as two separate methods of commencing a criminal procedure.

*Lawyers Practice Manual 1.3.501*: Further evidence of the above distinction.

*Lawyers Practice Manual 1.3.604*: In this matter, the explanations of a 'Undertaking of Bail' and 'Charge' are the most relevant.

*s. 35 CPA*: In this matter, this section applies. s. 35(2) is relevant. The informant received a written request via email. The informant therefore had 14 days upon receipt of the email by which the informant *must* serve the preliminary brief.

```
This raises a potential issue with informants or workplaces panicking at requests. In this matter a semi-completed preliminary brief was disclosed with a lack of evidence. It won't yield an early resolution compared to a thorough preliminary brief. 

Additionally, 14 days to complete a preliminary brief when considering the requisite need for a Sergeant to check and authorise, or an informant to amend these issue is problematic. 
```


*Form 8 Magistrates' Court Criminal Procedures Rules 2019*: This form greatly assist in our understanding of the legislation. The informant has not refused disclosure. There may have been an argument of a failure to complete the disclosure obligation of s. 35 CPA had the preliminary brief not been provided.

*s. 46 CPA*: In this matter, if the accused was not satisfied with the pre-hearing disclosure, they could utilise this provision to obtain an order for disclosure. It could equally be used if a preliminary brief were not served within the 14 day period.

*s. 401 CPA*: In this matter, the accused cannot utilised s. 402 (failing preliminary brief service costs where notice to appear is used). They could attempt a costs application via s. 401, but would be unsuccessful. 
```
If the informant were to fail to disclose the preliminary brief within 14 days (matter commenced via charge-and-summons or charge-and-bail), they could make a costs application via s. 401.
```


*Best practice guide - summary procedure - pp 4*: In this matter, the best practice guide has relevance in the following excerpt: "The credibility, accuracy and adequacy of the preliminary brief will significantly impact upon the potential for early resolution."

# Conclusion
The informant complied with s. 35(3) CPA and has not failed their pre-hearing disclosure obligations. 
The solicitor identified the incorrect provisions and would be better guided by the pro-forma letters found in the Lawyers Practice Manual (see appendices).
The accused cannot make an application for costs pursuant to s. 401 or s. 402 of the CPA. 
See below if they were to make a costs application in circumstances where s. 35(3) was *not* complied with.

## Submissions on Cost Applications
A submission were costs to be sought:
>"Your Honour, the purpose of pre-hearing disclosure is to enable early resolutions and to inform the accused of the allegations against them. The Victorian Legal Aid best practice guide states that well prepared preliminary briefs are credible, accurate and adequate when prepared well. My ultimate submission is that preparing that brief of evidence takes time and would lead to the best outcome for the accused."

If the matter of not complying with s. 35 CPA disclosure obligations was pressed:
>"Your Honour, the Criminal Procedure Act provides an appropriate method of dealing with failures of disclosure, found at Section 46. Your Honour can make an order to the informant ensuring disclosure within a timely manner. This would effectively ameliorate the need for the costs application, as this is the first mention of the matter."

It would depend upon the Magistrate / Defence arguments on the day as to whether costs would be awarded, as we have clearly breached legislation in the matter if it hasn't been disclosed within 14 days of the written request.
