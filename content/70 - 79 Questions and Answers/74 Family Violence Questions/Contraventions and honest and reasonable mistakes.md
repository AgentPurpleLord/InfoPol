In [_Beukes_ (2011) 205 A Crim R 406; [2011] SASC 9](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U2&serNum=2024554664&pubNum=0005341&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&ppcid=7d8ba9798b1e4782a8c3222f1c434cf9&contextData=\(sc.Keycite\)), Vanstone J went on to consider whether the defence of honest and reasonable mistake of fact was available, and observed at [11] that it was “at best, doubtful” whether the respondent's out of court assertions in a police interview (that he had been told of the revocation of the order) were sufficient to enliven the defence. In obiter commentary at [12]–[13], Vanstone J inclined towards the view that a mistake as to whether the order subsisted was a mistake of fact and not of law, and expressed some doubt as to whether such a mistake was reasonable.
