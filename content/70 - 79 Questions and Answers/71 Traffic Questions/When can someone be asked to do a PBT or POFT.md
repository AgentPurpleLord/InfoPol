>[!QUESTION] When can a person be requested to complete a Preliminary Breath Test or Preliminary Oral Fluid Test

# Research
- A formal demand to undergo a PBT is not required if the accused has expressed willingness to provide a sample of breath and the informant has legitimate reason for administering the breath test. [Walker v Director of Public Prosecutions (Vic) | Cases | Australia | Westlaw](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U2&serNum=1993377933&pubNum=0006272&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&ppcid=1b3e0630c78a4f9c8360908bf8d8c2b4&contextData=\(sc.Category\)&comp=wlau)
- Specific intent to commit the offence is not required [Director of Public Prosecutions (Vic) v Vaa | Cases | Australia | Westlaw](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U2&serNum=2005585238&pubNum=0006272&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&ppcid=1b3e0630c78a4f9c8360908bf8d8c2b4&contextData=\(sc.Category\)&comp=wlau)
- It is not clear whether a 'conditional response', e.g. I won't do the test until I speak with a lawyer, amounts to a refusal. s 464C(4) of the Crimes Act 1958 provides the right to speak with a lawyer. But an offence under s 49(1) has an exemption for this. **NEED TO FIND CASE LAW RE THIS**
- Prosecution must prove that the accused knew what was required of them.
- For a requirement to be validly made no particular form of words is required. The test of whether a requirement was validly made is whether the person was given reasonably sufficient information to know what is required of them and why. [_Rankin v O’Brien_ [1986] VR 67](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U2&serNum=1986183896&pubNum=0004888&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&ppcid=1b3e0630c78a4f9c8360908bf8d8c2b4&contextData=\(sc.Category\)); (1985) 2 MVR 503 at 73 (VR), per Southwell J; [_Sanzaro v County Court_ (2004) 42 MVR 279; [2004] VSC 48](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U2&serNum=2004203462&pubNum=0006272&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&ppcid=1b3e0630c78a4f9c8360908bf8d8c2b4&contextData=\(sc.Category\)); [_Director of Public Prosecutions v Serbest_ (2012) 211 A Crim R 588; 60 MVR 150; [2012] VSC 35](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U2&serNum=2027076932&pubNum=0005341&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&ppcid=1b3e0630c78a4f9c8360908bf8d8c2b4&contextData=\(sc.Category\)); [_Director of Public Prosecutions v Novakovic_ (2012) 225 A Crim R 468; 62 MVR 94; [2012] VSC 397](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U2&serNum=2028817325&pubNum=0005341&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&ppcid=1b3e0630c78a4f9c8360908bf8d8c2b4&contextData=\(sc.Category\)). 
- In the case of a prosecution for an offence under [s 49(1)(e)](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U7&docFamilyGuid=I09e38b1e045111e99495db3043f758b0&pubNum=1100190&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&docVersion=Law+in+Force&ppcid=1b3e0630c78a4f9c8360908bf8d8c2b4&contextData=\(sc.Category\)), of failing to comply with a requirement under [s 55(1)](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U7&docFamilyGuid=I163d513f045111e99495db3043f758b0&pubNum=1100190&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&docVersion=Law+in+Force&ppcid=1b3e0630c78a4f9c8360908bf8d8c2b4&contextData=\(sc.Category\)), the requirement is an essential element of the offence and must be proved and waiver is irrelevant. Consequently, if upon the whole of the evidence, including any permissible inferences, the court is not satisfied that the accused was given reasonably sufficient information to know what was required of him and why, the prosecution will fail. [_Sanzaro v County Court_ (2004) 42 MVR 279; [2004] VSC 48](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U2&serNum=2004203462&pubNum=0006272&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&ppcid=1b3e0630c78a4f9c8360908bf8d8c2b4&contextData=\(sc.Category\)&comp=wlau) at [24], per Nettle J. Whilst Nettle J’s comments are limited to [_Road Safety Act 1986_, ss 49(1)(e)](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U5&docFamilyGuid=I09e38b1e045111e99495db3043f758b0&pubNum=1100190&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&docVersion=Law+in+Force&ppcid=1b3e0630c78a4f9c8360908bf8d8c2b4&contextData=\(sc.Category\)) and [55(1)](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U5&docFamilyGuid=I163d513f045111e99495db3043f758b0&pubNum=1100190&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&docVersion=Law+in+Force&ppcid=1b3e0630c78a4f9c8360908bf8d8c2b4&contextData=\(sc.Category\)) logically they are applicable to requirements made in relation to other refuse offences. See also [_Director of Public Prosecutions v Piscopo_ (2011) 33 VR 182; 210 A Crim R 126; 59 MVR 200; [2011] VSCA 275](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U2&serNum=2026135092&pubNum=0004888&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&ppcid=1b3e0630c78a4f9c8360908bf8d8c2b4&contextData=\(sc.Category\)) at [38], per Ashley JA.

- The requirements to:
	- leave the motor vehicle;
	- undergo a POFT;
	- accompany;
	- remain until the oral fluid sample is furnished, tested and if necessary a part of the sample is given to the accused;
	- remain until the accused has complied with any requirement made of them under [s 59](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U7&docFamilyGuid=I1658c842045111e99495db3043f758b0&pubNum=1100190&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&docVersion=Law+in+Force&ppcid=1b3e0630c78a4f9c8360908bf8d8c2b4&contextData=\(sc.Category\));
	- remain until any further oral fluid sample required under [s 55E(5)](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U7&docFamilyGuid=I16458ef5045111e99495db3043f758b0&pubNum=1100190&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&docVersion=Law+in+Force&ppcid=1b3e0630c78a4f9c8360908bf8d8c2b4&contextData=\(sc.Category\)) is furnished, tested and if necessary a part of the sample is given to the accused;
	- remain for up to three hours; and
	- furnish the sample of oral fluid for testing or allow a doctor or health professional to take a sample of blood for analysis
