

>[!INFO] What is Family Violence?
>An ongoing pattern of coercive control or violent behaviour, occurring in current or past family, domestic or intimate partner relationships. It includes physical abuse, direct/indirect threats, sexual abuse, emotional abuse, psychological abuse, economic control, property damage, social isolation, with-holding of children and behaviour which causes the other person to live in fear.

# Police Role
As defined in the [Family Violence Code of Practice 2019](https://www.police.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2022-11/Code-of-practice-for-the-investigation-of-family-violence-Edition-4-Version-2.pdf):
1. **Encourage reporting** of FV by responding to all reports, regardless of origin; and
2. **Minimise harm** experienced by families during the police response; and
3. **Provide safe spaces** for victims who disclose harm; and
4. Be **cautious** of **influence/power** imbalance/manipulation of RESP; and
5. **Optimise safety/support** by investigating all detected/reported FV; and
6. **Pursuing all enforcement opportunities** which increases RESP accountability; and
7. Early **intervention / disruption** to break the cycle of FV; and
8. Provide appropriate, consistent, transparent & **accountable response** to FV; and
9. **Support victims** to remain in their home safely; and
10. **Utilise referral agencies**

# Fundamental Principles of the FVPA
Section 1 of the FVPA 2008 provides:
> The purpose of this act is to-
> 1. maximise **safety for children and adults** who have experienced family violence; and
> 2. **prevent and reduce family violence** to the greatest extent possible; and
> 3. **promote the accountability** of perpetrators of family violence for their actions.


# The Purpose of an Intervention Order
[From the Family Violence Bench Book 2.1](https://resources.judicialcollege.vic.edu.au/article/1053062/section/198):
>A family violence intervention order is a court order designed to protect a person from family violence. The order requires the respondent to engage or refrain from engaging in the conduct specified in the order.

## Variations of Intervention Orders
At times, an IVO or IIVO may need to be changed or amended to best suit the conditions of the situation. From the [Family Violence Bench Book 2.3](https://resources.judicialcollege.vic.edu.au/article/1053062/section/843431):
>The Magistrates’ Court and the Children’s Court have jurisdiction to revoke, vary or extend a family violence intervention order.

And the court considers these following elements in the application to vary or revoke an order:
1. the applicant’s **reasons** for seeking the variation or revocation; and
2. the **safety** of the protected person; and
3. the **protected person’s views** about the variation or revocation; and
4. whether or not the protected person is legally represented; and
5. if the protected person has a guardian, the guardian’s views.

And the Bench Book further provides:
>The court may consider the seriousness of the protected person’s original allegations even if the intervention order was made by consent without admissions. Even if the court does not have sufficient material to establish whether or not the original allegations are true, the seriousness of the alleged conduct affects the court’s assessment of whether varying or revoking the intervention order may pose a risk to the protected person.
