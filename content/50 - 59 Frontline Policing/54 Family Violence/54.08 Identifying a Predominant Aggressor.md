>[!TIP] Predominant Aggressor
>"The party to the FV incident who (via history & actions) has caused the most **physical harm**, **fear** and/or **intimidation** against the other."
>
>The primary aggressor is the person who is using **violence** and **coercion** to exercise  **control** over their partner or family member.

>[!IMPORTANT] Pattern of Coercive, Controlling & Violent Behaviour
>Whomever demonstrates a **pattern** of this behaviour is most likely the predominant aggreessor.

# Risks of Wrongful Identification
- Lack of immediate protection being provided to those who need it.
- Lowering trust in victims/survivors of the police.
- Creates significantly higher amounts of work for future police.

# Perpetrator Behaviours
Below is a list, which is not exhaustive or conclusive (remember you must identify a PATTERN):
- Overly present as a victim of violence, e.g. repeated attempts to get police to **side** with their story.
- Narratives about the injustice of the system and how it isn't working or them.
- Cites substance abuse by the other party to support their claims.
- Blames mental health as the cause of the other parties behaviours.

# Key Factors to Identify
- Respective Injuries to the Parties (defensive wounds like scratch marks)
	- The party who has scratch wounds tends to be the one who began the assault and the victim tried to fight them off.
- A party acted in self-defence
- A party acted in anticipation of violence or on-going abuse
- The capacity or likelihood for another party to inflict further injury

- **Patterns** of coercion, intimidation and/or violence by either party
- History of violence or L17's
- Accounts from other household members or witnesses if available

# Situational Factors
- Who called police first and why?
- Which party is most in fear?
- Which party most needs protection?
- What is the relationship between parties?
- What are the previous incidents?
- How much time between those incidents?

# Useful Questions
Has anyone in the household:
- Made you afraid?
- Insulted you or put you down?
- Humiliated you?
- Pushed, hit, punched, kicked, hurt or threatened to hurt you?
- Made you concerned for anyone's safety?
- Made you concerned for pets safety?

# Things to Avoid
Avoid doing the below. They can affect your judgment in determining the aggressor:
- Avoid mutualising language to describe family violence (e.g. mutual violence, bi-directional, "they're both as bad as each other")
	- This often occurs when situations are complex and the process of identifying a predominant aggressor is long, challenging and uncertain.
- Taking the easiest route, e.g. you have someone in custody, just issue something to them because of it.
	- Again, this occurs because it can be hard to identify the predominant aggressor. Don't accept a direction to do this unless you are satsified.
