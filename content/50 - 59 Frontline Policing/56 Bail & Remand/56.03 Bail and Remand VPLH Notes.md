# Primary Purpose
1. Understand the repeal of Bail Act offences.
2. Removal of Item 1 and Item 30 from Schedule 2.
3. Amendments to [[Unacceptable Risk Test]]
4. Recognise prohibition on refusal of bail for Schedule 3 (summary offences)
5. Understand expansion of:
	1. Section 3A (Aboriginal Persons)
	2. Section 3B (Children)

As with any legislation, it is important to read and understand the **purpose** of it. In short summary as per the Bail Amendment Act 2023:
1. Make changes to making determination of bail by:
	1. Removing Schedule 2 offences; and
	2. Ensuring bail is granted in respect of summary offences (unless on Schedule 3); and
	3. Make changes to what bail decision makers must take into account when determining bail for Aboriginal persons or Children.
2. To repeal contravening conduct conditions of bail and commit indictable offence whilst on bail

# Relevant Tests
> Investigators and police BDM's must identify which relevant 'test' within the Bail Act applies to an accused.

1. Exceptional Circumstances Test
2. Show Compelling Reasons Test
3. Unacceptable Risk Test


# Show Compelling Reasons Test & Schedule 2 Amendments
>[!IMPORTANT] Show Compelling Reasons Test
>1. Person is accused of Schedule 2 offence; and
>2. Exceptional Circumstances Test does **not** apply; or
>3. Non-schedule offence + terrorism record/risk
>   
>   Onus is on the accused to demonstrate compelling reasons.

As of 25/03/2024 the following have been repealed (removed) from the Bail Act 1977:
- [[Item 1 of Schedule 2]] being that the accused commits an offence whilst on summons/bail/warrant/CCO/parole order for another indictable offence.
- [[Item 30 of Schedule 2]] being Commit Indictable Offence Whilst on Bail, Contravene Conduct Conditions of Bail or Fail to Answer Bail.

==**These offences are no longer "remandable" offences**.==

# The New Schedule 3
> Where a person is charged with **only** an offence contained in the Summary Offences Act 1966 they must **not** be refused bail.
> UNLESS -> that offence is listed on Schedule 3 of the Bail Act 1977

This essentially functions as Schedule 1 and Schedule 2; a list of offences with which we should remand people if the circumstances are right. Schedule 3 is a new list of summary offences with which we can remand someone on if we **only** charge them with that offence.

# Bail Conduct Conditions
Emphasis has been placed on imposing the most appropriate bail conditions at all times.
- They must be well considered.
- The BDM must believe they will reduce the likelihood that the person is an unacceptable risk.
- They must not be more onerous than required to reduce risk.
- Human rights must be considered.
- They must be reasonable considering the nature of the allegations.

# Scenarios from VPLH
## Scenario 1 - TOMV
- Person on bail for TOMV.
- Person commits further TOMV
- Item 1 Schedule 2 is repealed and no longer used.
- Item 30 Schedule 2 is repealed and no longer used.
- Person would only be subject to the #unacceptable-risk-test

## Scenario 2 - MTK
- Person charged with [[23.08 Threats to Kill|Threats to Kill]] which is also a family violence offence.
	- Item 7 - Schedule 2 of The Bail Act 1977
- Person was in Show Compelling Reasons Test and granted bail as they demonstrated the test.
- 1 month later, person charged with **Persistent Contravention of Intervention Order**
	- Item 19 - Schedule 2 of The Bail Act 1977
- Person has now been charged with Schedule 2 offence (Persist Contra) whilst on bail for Schedule 2 offence (Threats to Kill FV Offence).
	- Exceptional Circumstances Test applies per Section 4AA(2)(c).
	- Key Point Here: LEAP does not show if an offence is a family violence offence or not on the priors screen or bail screen. Highly recommend now for each offence you attempt to quickly view the incident and verify it is not FV offending. 

## Scenario 3 - Schedule 3 In Action
- Person blocks roadway during protest. Arrested, charged and bailed on #summary-offence of **obstruct roadway** (s.4(e) Summary Offence Act)
- Person arrested on another roadway, doing same thing on the same day hours later. 
- Despite repeat offending, BDM is prohibited from refusing bail as person is **only** charged with #summary-offence which is **not** found in Schedule 3 of the Bail Act.

# Bail Variation
- Conduct conditions can only be changed by application to the court once they are imposed.
- If a person believes they cannot sign on, member must tell the person to make an application to the court to vary the bail.

# BDM Consideration
- Surrounding circumstances of the person and the offending
- Bail conduct conditions to alleviate risk
- Family violence risks
- Child accused
- Aboriginal accused
- Vulnerable adult accused
- Section 3AAA of the Bail Act 1977 must be assessed at each test.
	- Time on remand
	- Seriousness of offence
	- Circumstances of offending
	- Strength of prosecution case
	- Availability of treatment
	- Persons background
	- Presence of family violence.

Section 3AAA is important. Courts place the most weight on this part. If someone is unlikely to receive a term of imprisonment at all for their offending, they are unlikely to remain in custody. 

# Children
- Section 345 of the Children Youth and Families Act 2005
	- Legal presumption that we are in favour of legal proceedings commencing by way of summons with children involved.
- Organisational policy / VPM's suggest caution in the first instance with children if at all possible (should only be not cautioning if circumstances are serious).
- If considering to remand, recommended you contact the local Youth Law Unit and after-hours you can contact D24 for PFSU number.
- Flow charts from Youth Law Unit are best guide.

# Aboriginal Persons
- Mandatory notification when Aboriginal in custody to VALS.
- Does not change relevant test for bail, but does change / add factors to surrounding circumstances considerations.
	- Any issues due to their aboriginality
		- Historical and ongoing systemic factors resulting in Aboriginal people being over-represented in criminal justice system.
		- Risk of harm and trauma of being in custody
		- Importance of maintaining connection to culture, kinship, family, Elders, country and community.
	- History, culture of circumstances
	- Impact of experience of trauma and intergenerational trauma, including abuse, neglect, loss and family violence
	- Any experience of social or economic disadvantage including homelessness and unstable housing
	- Any other relevant cultural issue or obligation.
	- Aboriginal persons family and community
	- Providers of Aboriginal bail support services
- **Mandatory to contact Youth Law Unit or PFSU when remanding aboriginal child.**
