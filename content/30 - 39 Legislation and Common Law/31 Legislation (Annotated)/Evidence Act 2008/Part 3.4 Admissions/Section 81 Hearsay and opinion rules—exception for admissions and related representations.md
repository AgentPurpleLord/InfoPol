> 1. The hearsay rule and the opinion rule do not apply to evidence of an [[Admission]].
> 2. The hearsay rule and the opinion rule do not apply to evidence of a [[30 - 39 Legislation and Common Law/36 Legal Concepts/Previous representation]] (e.g. the context that the accused was making that admission in)-
> 	1. that was made in relation to an [[Admission]] at the time the [[Admission]] was made, or shortly before or after that time; and
> 	2. to which it is reasonably necessary to refer in order to understand the [[Admission]].

>[!NOTE] Breaking Down the Rule
>(1) is its own rule, separated from (2).
>
>You only need to prove that it is an admission for (1) to be live. You do not need to go down (2) if (1) is alive.
>
>(2) exists to cover off on the context or the questions that might have been asked to the accused. E.g. during interview, the informants questions are admissible.
