>1. This section only applies to criminal proceedings.
>2. A person who, when required to give evidence is; **spouse**, **de-facto partner**, **parent** or child **of** accused, may **object** to being required-
>	1. to give **evidence**; or
>	2. to give evidence of **communication** between person and accused-
>	   
>as a witness for the prosecution.

>3. Objection to be made **before** person gives evidence or **as soon as practicable** after person becomes aware of right to do so.
>4. Court **must** make person aware of this section if it becomes apparent.

>5. If jury present, court must determine objection in absence of jury.
>6. A person who makes objection under this section (as to evidence of communication), must not be required to give evidence if court finds-
>	1. likelihood harm would / might be caused to person, relationship between person and accused; and
>	2. nature and extent of harm outweighs desirability for evidence given.
>7. Without limiting matters, Court must take into account-
>	1. **nature** + **gravity** of offence;
>	2. **substance** + **importance** of evidence might give;
>	3. whether any other evidence which person might give could have **alternative** **evidence** to prove that fact;
>	4. **nature of relationship** between accused and person;
>	5. in giving evidence, person would disclose a matter that was told to them in confidence.
>8. If objection made under this section, **Prosecutor may not comment** on-
>	1. the **objection**; or
>	2. **decision** of court in relation to objection; or
>	3. **failure** of person to give evidence.

>[!ABSTRACT]- Sub-section's (1) & (2) - Simplified
>- Spouses, de-facto partners, parents or children (i.e. family) of the accused can say they don't want to give evidence.
>- If they say they don't want to, we cannot make them give evidence.

>[!ABSTRACT]- Sub-section's (3) & (4) - Simplified
>- The Court has to tell the family member as soon as they can that they're allowed to say no to giving evidence.
>- The family member (or defence) can make an objection (interruption) about this, but it has to be done **before** the person gives evidence. Otherwise, they have to do it as soon as they can.

>[!ABSTRACT]- Sub-section's (6) to (8) - Simplified
>- If the Court finds that the family member giving evidence would cause harm to a relationship, and that the harm caused would be worse then whatever evidence they could tell us, then they cannot be required to give evidence.
>- When figuring that out, the court thinks about how bad the offence was, how important the evidence might be, whether any other evidence could be given by someone else and what kind of relationship the accused and the family member have.
>- The Prosecution cannot make any comment about all of this, because it wouldn't be fair or appropriate.
