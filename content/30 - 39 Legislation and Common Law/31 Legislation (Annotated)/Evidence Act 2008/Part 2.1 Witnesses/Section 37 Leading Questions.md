> 1. [[Leading Question]] must not be put to witness in **[[Section 29 Manner and form of questioning witnesses and their responses|examination in chief]]** or in **[[Section 39 Limits on re-examination|re-examination]]** unless-
> 	1. the court gives **leave**; or
> 	2. the question relates to a matter introductory to the witness's evidence; or
> 	3. no objection is made to the question and (leaving aside the party conducting the examination in chief or re-examination) each other party to the proceeding is represented by an Australian legal practitioner or prosecutor; or
> 	4. the question relates to a matter that is not in dispute; or
> 	5. if the witness has specialised knowledge based on the witness's training, study or experience—the question is asked for the purpose of obtaining the witness's opinion about a hypothetical statement of facts, being facts in respect of which evidence has been, or is intended to be, given.
> 2. Unless the court otherwise directs, subsection (1) does not apply in civil proceedings to a question that relates to an investigation, inspection or report that the witness made in the course of carrying out public or official duties.
> 3. Subsection (1) does not prevent a court from exercising power under rules of court to allow a written statement or report to be tendered or treated as evidence in chief of its maker.

>[!NOTE] Rationale behind no leading questions
>Because the witness is called by the party doing the EIC; therefore the witness will likely be bias to that party.
# Can you ask leading questions?
Sometimes, leading questions can be used. The following circumstances enable leading questions during evidence-in-chief:
1. Formal and introductory matters; name, address, occupation. 
2. Matters not in dispute; varies from case to case. You must still ask the question as non-leading as possible. E.g. "On the 1st of May last year, in the afternoon, can you tell us where you were?"
3. Directing attention:
	1. Towards something; "After you left home, where did you go?"
	2. Away from something; "I don't want you to tell us anything that was said, but did you speak to someone?"
