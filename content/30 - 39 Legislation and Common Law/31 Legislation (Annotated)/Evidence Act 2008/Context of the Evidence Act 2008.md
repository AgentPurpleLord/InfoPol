The [[Evidence Act 2008]] (also known as the 'Victorian Uniform Evidence Act') has a history in the Australian Law Reform Commission (ALRC) inquiry that commenced in 1979. This inquiry ultimate led to every state and territory of Australia creating new Acts to stipulate rules surrounding evidence. 

# Underlying Policy
As with all legislation, context is important in interpretation. For criminal matters, the underlying policy is:
1. **Accusatorial System**: Criminal trials do not resolve disputes. The defendant is presumed innocent until proven guilty and has no obligation to assist the prosecution.
2. **Minimise Wrongful Convictions**: It is paramount to the community to avoid or minimise the risk of wrongful convictions. 
3. **Definition of Central Question**: Can the Prosecution prove the defendant guilty beyond a reasonable doubt? The community must be confident the defendant committed the offence.
4. **Recognition of Rights**: Defendants are entitled to a fair trial which provides them with dignity and integrity. 
5. **Assisting Adversarial Contest**: Defendants are entitled with protection consistent with "the idea of the adversary system as a genuine contest"
