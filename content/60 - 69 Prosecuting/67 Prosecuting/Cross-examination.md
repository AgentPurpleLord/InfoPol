# General Objectives
- Find and expose the *truth* of *facts in issue*.
- Elicit facts which may be used to *cross-examine other witnesses*.
- Cast *doubt* upon the *accuracy of other witnesses' testimony*.
- Cast *doubt* upon the *accuracy of the witness*.
- Put the *prosecution case* to the witness.

# Phase 1 - Clarification and Commitment
1. Gain concessions favourable to Prosecution case.
2. Identify *escape routes* and commit the witness to areas you will later attack.
	1. Hide your intent - be indirect.
3. Identify *inconsistencies* between evidence.
4. Identify *inconsistencies* from their testimony.
5. Tie the witness to their inconsistent accounts.
	1. Visualise and test everything they say.
6. Constantly apply *innocent person test*: Ask yourself, "What should he have done at *every* stage? What did he do?"
7. Identify *absurdities* or *weaknesses* in logic and plausibility of his account.
8. Consider:
	1. Is the witness lying? Where? Why? How do you exploit it?
	2. Is the witness merely mistaken? Where? Why?
	3. Could the witness be truthful and correct?
	4. Is our case theory valid? Does it have real merit?

# Phase 2 - Commitment and Puttage
1. Seal any *escape routes* they might have open to them.
2. Re-apply the *innocent person* test, think *before*, *during* and *after* the offence.
3. Help the witness dig a hole for themselves; commit them to facts you know are untrue.
4. Put the prosecution case to the witness.
5. Reconsider their motives for lying.

# Phase 3 - Attack and Dislocation
1. Demonstrate the witness has lied or is mistaken:
	1. Use *leading questions* about their *contradictions*, *inconsistencies* and *absurdities* in their accounts.
2. Attribute motives for *lying*:
	1. Love, friendship, loyalty, duty, guilt, fear, greed, hatred
3. Attribute reasons for *mistake*:
	1. Defective observation; lack of care (attention/time); faulty eyesight/hearing; confusion; fear; anger; alcohol or drugs.
	2. Defective recollection; no reason to remember; no notes; effects of time elapsed.

# Phase 4 - Exploitation
1. Force *concessions* and *admissions* from the witness:
	1. Strong *leading questions* to exploit most *convincing* or *damaging* points.
2. Leave the court with a clear picture of the prosecution case:
	1. Succinctly put *prosecution case* to the witness; *how* the offence was committed; *why* the offence was committed; *consequences* of the offence.
