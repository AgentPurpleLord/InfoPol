When a Respondent objects to the making of a final FVIO, the matter will be adjourned for a contested hearing. Prior to an application being adjourned to a contested hearing, the matter is listed for a directions hearing. The Prosecutor will be responsible for case management until the matter is allocated (either to a Prosecutor or a Police Lawyer). The Police Applicant should be notified of the details of the contested hearing as soon as possible after the date for directions hearing is decided.
The purpose of the directions hearing is to assess the positions of parties involved, determine the amount of time the matter will take to be heard and how many witnesses will be called during the hearing. In preparation for the directions hearing appearance, the police lawyer is required to obtain detailed instructions from the Police Applicant regarding the case.
Considerations when booking the directions hearing and contest dates:
• Confirm the availability of the Police Applicant on the proposed dates
• Consider all the evidence and form the opinion that there would be a reasonable prospect of success, as is the case in all stages of a proceeding, in obtaining a final FVIO. The Prosecutor should be satisfied, on the balance of probabilities, that the Respondent has committed family violence towards an AFM or a child (or children) and the Respondent is likely to continue to commit Family Violence.
• Ensure that a FVIO directions hearing checklist is completed, signed and a copy provided to the Court registrar for the file, in accordance with the Magistrate’s Court Practice Direction 10 of 2015.
• Advise the Police Applicant as soon as possible that the matter has been booked for a directions hearing and contest. 
• An email must be sent to the Police Applicant and the FVLO PBEA requesting the provision of particular documents and evidence that will assist in the preparation of the directions hearing.
The following material (where relevant) must be obtained in preparation of the directions hearing:
• Full IVO Brief
• Witness statements, including a statement from the Police Applicant
• Photographs (in colour)
• SMS, phone records, phone bills
• Transcripts of any ‘000’ calls
• Priors including details of any FVIOs previously ordered and the circumstances regarding previous FVIOs
• Summaries of police attendance at any family violence incidents
• Details pertaining to any breaches of FVIOs
• Copies of any related orders from other Courts i.e. Family Law Orders or Children’s/Parenting Orders
• Any other evidence that may be relevant to the case
• Updated risk assessment
The Police Applicant is not required to attend the directions hearing. However, the Prosecutor should arrange for the Police Applicant to be available by phone. In circumstances where the Police Applicant is not available, arrangements should be made with the Applicant’s OIC to provide instructions on the day of the directions hearing.
The Police Applicant must be available to attend the contested hearing.
If interpreters are needed, the Prosecutor should instruct the Police Applicant to arrange these for the Prosecution’s witnesses. A request will be made from the Court registry staff to book an interpreter/s for Respondent witnesses
