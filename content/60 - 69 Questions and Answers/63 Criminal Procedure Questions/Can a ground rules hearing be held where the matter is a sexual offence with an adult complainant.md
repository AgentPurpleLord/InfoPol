**SHORT ANSWER: YES, THEY SHOULD ALWAYS BE HELD WHERE ADULT COMP AND SEXUAL OFFENCE**

[Justice Legislation Amendment (Victims) Bill 2017](https://content.legislation.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/3e776e90-56c2-3352-b3ce-bc7410139452_581451exi1.pdf)

Clause 25 inserts a new Part 8.2A into the Criminal Procedure Act 2009. Division 1 of new Part 8.2A, which contains new sections 389A–389E, provides for ground rules hearings in certain criminal proceedings. Division 2 of new Part 8.2A, which contains new sections 389F–389K, provides for the appointment of intermediaries in criminal proceedings for certain witnesses.

Intermediary schemes aim to protect and empower vulnerable witnesses to give their best evidence. Intermediaries are not advocates or support workers. Their role is to facilitate communication with the witness. Ground rules hearings are used to discuss and establish how certain witnesses will be enabled to give their best evidence. The introduction of such a scheme in Victoria was endorsed in Ward v The Queen [2017] VSCA 37.

These reforms are based on recommendations 30 and 31 in the Victorian Law Reform Commission's report The Role of Victims of Crime in the Criminal Trial Process (August 2016) and recommendations 59 and 60 of the Royal Commission into Institutional Responses to Child Sexual Abuse ("the Royal Commission") in its Criminal Justice Report. The reforms are based on the ground rules and intermediaries scheme in England and Wales, and the pilot intermediaries scheme in New South Wales.

New section 389A(1) provides that ground rules hearings are available in criminal proceedings relating to a variety of offences against the person, being offences to which Divisions 5 and 6 of Part 8.2 of the Criminal Procedure Act 2009 (which include procedures for witnesses and complainants under 18 years or with a cognitive impairment) apply.

New section 389A(2) provides that ground rules hearings apply at any stage of the criminal proceeding, including an appeal or rehearing.

New section 389A(3) provides that ground rules hearings may be held with respect to any witness other than the accused, provided that the witness is under the age of 18 years or has a cognitive impairment. New section 389A(4) provides that any reference to "witness" in Division 1 is a reference to "witness" as referenced in subsection (3).

[Justice Legislation Amendment (Sexual Offences and Other Matters) Bill 2022](https://content.legislation.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/bills/591338exi1.pdf)

Clause 69 substitutes section 389A(3) of the Criminal Procedure Act 2009 to extend the application of Division 1 of Part 8.2A to a complainant in relation to a charge for a sexual offence. Division 1 currently applies to a witness (including a complainant) other than the accused, in a proceeding referred to in section 389A(1) (which includes a proceeding that relates to a charge for a sexual offence), who is under the age of 18 years or has a cognitive impairment.

**The effect of the amendment is that Division 1 will also apply to an adult complainant in relation to a charge for a sexual offence.** The intention is for the ground rules hearing division to continue to apply in the same manner to the intermediaries scheme, as set out in Division 2 of Part 8.2A.
