Lutz v JK (2016) 310 FLR 392 at [23]:
>A sentencing court should afford the parties an opportunity to address on penalty. This does not mean that the court must signal its intention in relation to sentence, but it does mean that, if the court is contemplating a sentencing outcome that has not been raised by the parties and is not for other reasons obviously “on the table”, then the parties should be offered the opportunity to address that matter. In this case, the prosecution was not offered an opportunity to make submissions about the outcome.

And at [25]:
>Consequently, the failure to invite the appellant to make submissions about the appropriateness of a [s 17](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U7&docFamilyGuid=I0375ad30033e11e99495db3043f758b0&pubNum=1100190&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&docVersion=Law+in+Force&ppcid=1f8afbfd60794cb7b7a0e0b6c9113208&contextData=\(sc.DocLink\)) dismissal amounted to a denial of procedural fairness.

And at [26]:
>The grounds of lack of procedural fairness and manifest inadequacy are interrelated; had it been moderately obvious that a dismissal under [s 17](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U7&docFamilyGuid=I0375ad30033e11e99495db3043f758b0&pubNum=1100190&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&docVersion=Law+in+Force&ppcid=1f8afbfd60794cb7b7a0e0b6c9113208&contextData=\(sc.DocLink\)) was “on the table”, then it would not have been something that the sentencing court needed to clearly identify and, inferentially, such a sentence may not have been manifestly inadequate.

The case itself was from ACT. To overview it:
- The ACT DPP appealed a matter where a Magistrate had made a dismissal order for an offence of negligent driving causing death.
- The accused was 17 years old and on her probationary licence when she lost control of her vehicle, swerved into the left at 40km/h and hit an oncoming vehicle killing the driver. 
- The accused pled guilty. 
- During sentencing, the Chief Magistrate noted that no one submitted for a custodial sentence. HH also stated community service would be unwarranted and made reference to a GBB and a financial penalty. 
	- "the lack of any prior criminal history, the low level of criminal culpability, the significance of the recording of a conviction for a young woman at this age and a lack of any need in my estimation for specific deterrence and the low role to be played by general deterrence."
- The accused submitted that a "simple good behaviour order" be would appropriate. **Prosecution did not contest this.** 
- At no point did the Chief Magistrate indicate that she was contemplating dismissing the matter without a good behaviour bond.

Hua v Qiao [2024] ACTSC 121 expands on the above a bit better, at [22]:
>Applying those principles, the context of what occurred during the hearing is important for the resolution of this case. The various sentencing alternatives will always be known possibilities and during a hearing, there are some alternatives that may receive a greater focus than others. Others may not be discussed at all, either because they are impliedly accepted by the parties not to be appropriate or because they are unavailable due to previous choices made as to whether to invoke statutory procedures that are necessary to enliven them (such as the ordering of an intensive correction assessment or eligibility assessment for a drug and alcohol treatment order).

In this case, it was found that there was a procedural unfairness towards the prosecution because the Magistrate proceeded on a non-conviction good behaviour order without discussing the appropriateness of non-conviction vs conviction with the parties.
