Parity is recognised at [[Section 1 - Purposes]] and at common law. 

Parity is ensuring consistency in punishment. This *does not* mean that co-offenders must receive the same punishment. 

# Principles
1. Parity = all things being equal, offenders should receive the same penalty.
2. The principle applies not only to those charged, but to *all offenders* involved in the same crime.
3. In determining weigh to parity, regard is had for:
	1. the role of each offender; and
	2. the aggravating factors which apply to each offender; and
	3. the mitigating factors that apply to each offender.
4. The greater dissimilarity between the above factors (3(a), (b), (c)), then less weight is given to parity.
5. Parity is also used to justify significantly different penalties where the factors are significantly different.
6. On appeal, parity can only operate where disparity is significant.
7. On appeal, parity can reduce a sentence even if the sentence appealed from is not manifestly excessive.
8. Parity has less scope in Crown appeals.
