**[[Leading Question|Leading Question]]**: (a) directly/indirectly suggests a particular answer to the question OR (b) assumes existence of fact which in *dispute* and as to existence of which witness not given evidence to.

**[[Section 41 Improper Questions|Improper Question]]**: misleading, confusing, annoying, harassing, intimidating, offensive, oppressive, humiliating, repetitive, belittling, insulting, inappropriate, no basis other than stereotype.

**[[Section 55 Relevant evidence|Relevance]]:** could rationally affect (directly/indirectly) the assessment of the probability of the existence of a fact in issue.

**[[Section 59 The hearsay rule - exclusion of hearsay evidence|Hearsay]]:** a [[30 - 39 Legislation and Common Law/31 Legislation (Annotated)/Evidence Act 2008/Dictionary/Previous representation|previous representation]] made by a person is not admissible to prove existence of a fact that it can reasonably be supposed that the person intended to assert by the representation.

**[[Section 76 The opinion rule|Opinion]]:** opinion is not admissible to prove the existence of a fact about the existence of which the opinion was expressed.

**[[Section 81 Hearsay and opinion rules—exception for admissions and related representations|Admissions]]:** hearsay and opinion rules do not apply to evidence of an admission; they also do not apply to a previous representation in the context of the accused making an admission (e.g. the person asking the question).

**[[Section 97 The tendency rule|Tendency]]:** evidence of character, reputation or conduct of a person, or a tendency that a person has/had, is not admissible to prove that a person has/had a tendency to act in a particular way.

**[[Section 98 The coincidence rule|Coincidence]]:** evidence of 2 or more events occurred is not admissible to prove that a person did a particular act/had particular state of mind; having regard to similarities in events/circumstances in which they occurred; or any similarities in both the events/circumstances in which they occurred. 

**[[Section 101A Credibility evidence|Credibility]]:** credibility evidence is relevant only because it affects the assessment of the credibility of the witness or person.

**[[Section 110 Evidence about character of an accused|Character]]:** hearsay, opinion, tendency and credibility do not apply to evidence adduced by an accused to prove (directly/implication) that the accused is a person of good character; if accused adduced this evidence, the same rules do not apply to prove accused is not of good character.

**[[Part 3.9 Identification Evidence|Identification Evidence]]:** visual identification means ID evidence based wholly/partly on what person saw; picture identification means ID evidence based wholly/partly on examining pictures kept for use of police officers.

**[[Section 137 Exclusion of prejudicial evidence in criminal proceedings|Mandatory Exclusions]]:** Criminal proceeding + unfairly prejudicial outweighed; and improperly or illegally obtained evidence.

**[[Section 135 General discretion to exclude evidence|Discretionary Exclusions]]:** The court may refuse to admit evidence if its probative value is substantially outweighed by the danger that the evidence might be; unfairly prejudicial; misleading/confusing; undue waste of time. Court can limit if danger of being unfairly prejudicial; misleading/confusing.
