>1. If a motor vehicle is impounded/immobilised, a person whose interests are substantially affected by the impoundment/immobilisation may apply to the Magistrates' Court for an order that the motor vehicle be released on the ground that the impoundment/immobilisation is causing, or will cause exceptional hardship to the applicant or any other person.
>2. An application under (1) may be made at any time while the motor vehicle remains impounded/immobilised, including where it continues for longer than the designated period as a result of non-payment of the designated costs.

(2A) An applicant under (1) must give 7 days written notice of the application.
(2B) A notice under 2A must state the grounds for the claim of exceptional hardship.
(2C) The Magistrates' Court must not deal with an application under (1) unless (2A)/(2B) satisfied.

>3. Subject to (3A), (3B) and (3C), an application made by (1), the Magistrates' Court can release the vehicle if they are satisfied that the impoundment/immobilisation is causing, or will cause exceptional hardship to the applicant or any other person.

(3A) If—
(a) the offender is disqualified from obtaining a drivers licence or learners permit; or
(b) the offender's driver licence or learner permit is suspended—
for a period longer than the period of the impoundment/immobilisation, the Magistrates' Court **must** not consider any exceptional hardship caused to the offender.

(3B) The Magistrates' Court must not make an order under (3) relating to the offender's employment unless the applicant satisfies the Court that—
(a) driving the vehicle is essential (not merely convenient) for the offender's employment; and
(b) no other transport to his or her place of employment is available to the offender; and
(c) the offender, after making reasonable enquiries is unable to arrange for another person to drive the offender to his or her place of employment. 

(3C) Magistrates' Court must have regard to safety of the public and public interest in prevent the vehicle from being used and further driving offences.
(4) If the Magistrates' Court makes an order under (3) it may order that the applicant is not liable to pay all or part of the designated costs.
