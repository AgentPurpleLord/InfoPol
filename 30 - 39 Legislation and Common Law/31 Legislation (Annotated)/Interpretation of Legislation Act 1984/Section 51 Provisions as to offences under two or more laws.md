>(1) Where an act or omission constitutes an offence under two or more laws, the offender shall, unless the contrary intention expressly appears, be liable to be prosecuted under either or any or all of those laws but shall not be liable to be punished more than once for the same act or omission.
>(2)     In subsection (1) "law" means—
>	(a)     an Act or a provision of an Act;
>	(b)     a subordinate instrument or a provision of a subordinate instrument; or
>	(c)     common law.

## Explanatory Memorandum
Clause 51 provides that where an act or omission constitutes an offence under two or more laws, the offender shall not be liable to be punished more than once for the same act or omission. The clause restates section 28 of the Acts Interpretation Act 1958 but is wider in its application than that section in that it applies where a defendant is charged with offences against different sections of the same Act. Further, the clause makes it clear that, although a person cannot be punished more than once for the same act or omission, that person can nevertheless be prosecuted simultaneously for more than one offence based on the same act or omission.
