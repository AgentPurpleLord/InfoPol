applies in addition to section 108A.
> 1. If the person referred to in that section is an accused, the credibility evidence is not admissible unless the court gives leave.
> 2. Despite subsection (2), leave is not required if the evidence is about whether the accused—
> 	1. is biased or has a motive to be untruthful; or
> 	2. is, or was, unable to be aware of or recall matters to which his or her previous representation relates; or
> 	3. has made a prior inconsistent statement.
> 3. The prosecution must not be given leave under subsection (2) unless evidence adduced by the accused has been admitted that—
> 	1. tends to prove that a witness called by the prosecution has a tendency to be untruthful; and
> 	2. is relevant solely or mainly to the witness's credibility.
> 4. A reference in subsection (4) to evidence does not include a reference to evidence of conduct in relation to—
> 	1. the events in relation to which the accused is being prosecuted; or
> 	2. the investigation of the offence for which the accused is being prosecuted.
> 5. Another accused must not be given leave under subsection (2) unless the previous representation of the accused that has been admitted includes evidence adverse to the accused seeking leave.
