>1. Party may **adduce** evidence of contents of document by tendering **document in question** by—
>	1. adducing evidence of admissions made by party to proceeding as to contents of document in question; 
>	2. tendering a document that-
>		1. purported to be copy of **document in question**; and
>		2. produced or purports to be produced by **device** (e.g. photocopier or word processor) that reproduces contents of documents.
>	3. if document in question is **audio/sound**, or words in code (e.g. short-hand writing), tendering a document purporting to be transcript of words (e.g. tape recording or DVD);
>	4. tendering document that—
>		1. forms part of **records** kept by business; and
>		2. copy of or **extract summary** of document in question; 
>	5. if public document, has been printed-
>		1. by authorised person; or
>		2. by authority of government; or
>		3. by authority of parliament
>2. Subsection (1) applies to document in question whether it is available to party or not.
>3. If party adduces evidence of document under (1)(a), evidence may only be used-
>	1. in respect to party's case against other party who made admission; or
>	2. in respect of other party's case against party who adduced evidence in that way.
>4. Party may adduce evidence of document that is not available to party by—
>	1. tendering copy of or extract summary of document in question; or
>	2. adducing from witness evidence contents of document in question.


# Explanatory Memorandum
>Clause 48 provides that evidence of the contents of a document can be adduced by tendering the document or by any one or more of the following methods—
>- adducing evidence of an admission made by another party about its contents; 
>- tendering a copy of the document produced by a device (for example, a photocopier or a word processor) that reproduces the contents of documents;
>- if the content of the document is not in visible form (for example, a tape-recording) or is in a code (for example, shorthand notes), tendering a transcript;
>- if the document is an article or thing on or in which information is stored in such a way that it cannot be used unless a device is used to retrieve, produce or collate it, tendering a document produced by the device (for example, computer output or a document produced by an optical laser disk reader);
>- tendering a business record that is an extract from, or a summary or copy of, the document;
>- if the document is a public document, tendering an official printed copy of the document.
>The clause provides that a party to whom a document is not available may lead evidence of the contents of the document by tendering a copy, extract or a summary of the document or adducing evidence of the contents of the document from a witness.


# Section 48(1)(c) and (d) audio or sound documents
The ALRC explained the proposal on which this provision is based (ALRC 26, vol 1, para 651):
> The provision is included to permit the tendering of secondary evidence of the contents of modern information storing media. In particular it deals with:
> 
> - audio tapes and words stored in codified form (this would include a word processor). It may be given by tendering a transcript;
> - computers and like devices. It is not necessary to tender the original of the tape, etc, containing the computer instruction or data (this is likely to be meaningless to the court in any event) but the “printout” may be tendered. The printout will have to be authenticated.

It is sufficient if the tendered document “purports”12 to be a transcript (of a tape or shorthand recording) under (c) or “purports” to have been produced by use of a device used to retrieve, produce or collate stored information under (d); See [_Eastman v The Queen_ (1997) 76 FCR 9; 158 ALR 107](http://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?findType=Y&serNum=1997401810&pubNum=0005520&refType=U2&originationContext=ebook&RS=ebbp3.0&vr=3.0&comp=wlau).

However, in applying s 48(1)(c), the Full Court of the Federal Court has expressed the following view:
> Where, on the evidence adduced in a particular case, there is doubt or disagreement whether the transcript, or part of it, accurately deciphers the sounds captured on the tape, it seems to us that [receiving the transcripts into evidence only as an aide-memoire for the sounds emitted by the playing of the tape etc] should be the role of the transcript, notwithstanding the provisions of s 48(1)(c) of the Evidence Act
