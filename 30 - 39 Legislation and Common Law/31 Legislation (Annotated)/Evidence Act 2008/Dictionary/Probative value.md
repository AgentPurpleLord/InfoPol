> *probative value* of evidence means the extent to which the evidence could rationally affect the assessment of the probability of the existence of a fact in issue;

The probative value of evidence determines how much weight the evidence carries in proving or disproving a particular fact in question. 

- **Rational Impact**: Probative value is about whether the evidence can reasonably influence how likely a fact is to be true or not. In other words, does this evidence make a difference in determining the truth of the matter at hand?
- **Evidence in Legal Context**: In a legal context, evidence with high probative value is strong and persuasive. It helps establish the truth of a fact that is important to the case. For instance, if a fingerprint is found at a crime scene and it matches the defendant’s fingerprint, this piece of evidence has high probative value because it significantly affects the probability that the defendant was present at the crime scene.
- **Balancing Act**: Sometimes, courts also consider the balance between the probative value and any potential prejudicial effect. If the evidence is highly probative but also highly prejudicial (meaning it could unfairly sway the jury), the court might limit its use.
