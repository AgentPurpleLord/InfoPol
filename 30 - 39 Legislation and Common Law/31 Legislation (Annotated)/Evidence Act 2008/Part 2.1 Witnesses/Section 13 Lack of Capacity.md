>1. A person is **not** **competent** to give evidence about a fact if-
>2.  person does **not** have **capacity** to **understand** question about fact; or
>3.  person does **not** have **capacity** to give **answer** that can be understood- 
>	   
>and that incapacity **cannot be overcome**.

>4. A person who is **not** competent under (1) to give evidence about a fact may **still** be competent to give evidence about **other facts**.

>5. A person who is competent to give evidence about a fact is not competent to give sworn/affirmed evidence if person does **not** have **capacity** to **understand** that, in giving evidence, obligated to give **truthful evidence**.


>6. A person who is not competent to give sworn/affirmed evidence may, subject to (5), be competent to give unsworn evidence or evidence not affirmed about the fact.
>7. A person under (3), can still give evidence if the court has informed them-
>8.  it is important to tell the **truth**; and
>9.  they may be asked questions they don't know answers to, or **cannot remember** and that they should say if that's the case; and
>10.  they may be asked questions that suggest statements are true or false, and that they should feel **no pressure** to agree either way.

>11. It is presumed, unless the contrary is proved, that a person is **not** **incompetent** because of this section.

>12.  Evidence that has been given by a witness does **not** become **inadmissible** merely because, before the witness finishes giving evidence, he or she **dies** or ceases to be competent to give evidence.

>13.  For the purpose of determining a question arising under this section, the court may inform itself as it thinks fit, including by obtaining information from a person who has relevant specialised knowledge based on the person's training, study or experience.


>[!IMPORTANT]+ Context for Section 13
>[Section 13 of the Evidence Act 2008](http://www8.austlii.edu.au/cgi-bin/viewdoc/au/legis/vic/consol_act/ea200880/s13.html)[^1] is about a persons competence, as in to have an ability to do something successfully, when they are called as a witness in court.

>[!ABSTRACT]- Sub-section (1) - Simplified
>- A person is unable to give evidence if:
>	- They can't understand what questions they're being asked; or
>	- They can't answer a question they're asked
>- This only applies if there is nothing the court or question-asker can do to overcome that inability.

>[!ABSTRACT]- Sub-section (2) - Simplified
>Just because someone can't answer or understand one question about one fact, doesn't mean they can't still answer a different question about a different fact. 
>
>For each question, a person ability to answer/understand needs to be tested.

>[!ABSTRACT]- Sub-section (3) - Simplified
>- When a person gives evidence in court, they have to swear or affirm themselves in.
>- Swearing or affirming means that the person understands they are legally obligated to tell the truth, or they might face punishment.
>- If a person isn't able to understand the swearing/affirmation process, then they're deemed not to be capable of giving evidence on that question or fact.

>[!ABSTRACT]- Sub-sections (4) & (5) - Simplified
>- If a person doesn't understand their affirmation/sworn oath to tell the truth, there's an alternative.
>- The courts can let the person know that it is:
>	- Important to tell the truth; and
>	- If they get asked a question they don't know an answer to, or can't remember the answer to, they can just say exactly that; and
>	- They might be asked about whether someone else's words are true or false. When they get asked that, they shouldn't feel pressured to agree or disagree.

>[!ABSTRACT]- Sub-section (6) - Simplified
>It must be proven that a person isn't able to understand or answer questions. You can never assume it's the case in court.

>[!ABSTRACT]- Sub-section (7) - Simplified
>- Court can sometimes be adjourned ('paused') until the next day because people want to go home.
>- If someone was called to give evidence on the first day and died after that day before the second day when they would give more evidence, the court doesn't remove their evidence from the first day.
>- This includes if their competence changes, e.g. if they have a major accident or a brain injury that would affect their competence.

>[!ABSTRACT]- Sub-section (8) - Simplified
>- The court can get doctors, psychologists or other professionals to assist them in understanding if someone is able to answer/understand questions asked to them.
