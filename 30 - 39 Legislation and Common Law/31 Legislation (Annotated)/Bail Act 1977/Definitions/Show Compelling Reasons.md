*Show compelling reasons* is found within [[Section 4AA - When 2 step tests apply]] and most typically is referring to [[Bail Act Schedule 2]] offences.

It refers to a 'test' that the *accused/defence* bear the onus of proving in a bail application.

## Circumstances
The below circumstances create the need for the accused to satisfy the court that *compelling reasons* exist for them to be granted bail:

- **s. 4AA(3)**: Sch 2 committed + s. 4AA(2) (exceptional circumstances) doesn't apply.
- **s. 4AA(4)(a)**: Neither Sch 1/Sch 2 + terrorism record.
- **s. 4AA(4)(b)**: Neither Sch 1/Sch 2 + s. 8AA terrorism record.

## Case Law
- Re Ceylan [2018] VSC 361
	- Compelling reasons would likely be shown if there existed forceful and therefore convincing reason showing that, in all the circumstances, the continued detention of the applicant in custody was not justified. It is not, however, necessary for an applicant required to show compelling reason, to show a reason which is irresistible or exceptional.
- Re Alsulayhim [2018] VSC 570
	- Compelling reasons can be made out by a number of circumstances relating to the strength of the prosecution case, the applicant's personal circumstances and those mentioned in s. 3AAA.
- Re Johnstone (no 2) [2018] VSC 803
	- Compelling reasons can also be made out by a single factor.
