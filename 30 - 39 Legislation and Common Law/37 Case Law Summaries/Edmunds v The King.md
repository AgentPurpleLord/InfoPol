This was an appeal to the Victorian Court of Appeal by Daniel Edmunds against his conviction for the murder of his partner, Sarah.

The appeal raised key issues about the trial judge's directions to the jury, the availability of the provocation defence, and whether a miscarriage of justice had occurred.

# Factual Background
- Daniel Edmunds killed his partner, Sarah, by stabbing her 16 times at their home.
- Their relationship had a history of domestic violence, with prior assaults by Edmunds and threats made against Sarah.
- On the night of the incident, neighbours reported yelling and the sound of a struggle.
- Edmunds claimed Sarah had provoked him by insulting him and making threats about taking their children away.
- He fled the scene and was arrested two days later.

# Procedural History
- Edmunds was tried in the Supreme Court of Victoria and found guilty of murder.
- He was sentenced to 27 years’ imprisonment with a non-parole period of 21 years.
- He appealed on several grounds, principally:
    1. That the jury directions were inadequate regarding provocation.
    2. That the verdict was unreasonable or could not be supported by the evidence.
    3. That the trial judge erred by not leaving manslaughter to the jury as an alternative verdict.

# Issues on Appeal
- **Provocation**: Should the trial judge have left the partial defence of provocation to the jury?
- **Unreasonable verdict**: Was the guilty verdict open to the jury on the evidence?
- **Jury directions**: Were the jury directions inadequate regarding self-defence, intoxication, and mental impairment?
- **Alternative verdict**: Should the judge have left open a manslaughter verdict?

# Key Legal Reasoning
- **Provocation**: The Court found that there was no "evidence of sudden loss of control" sufficient to justify putting provocation to the jury. The attack was sustained, disproportionate, and lacked spontaneity, making the defence untenable.
- **Unreasonable Verdict**: The Court upheld that the jury had ample evidence to find intent to kill or cause serious injury. The number and nature of the stab wounds, along with post-offence conduct, supported this.
- **Jury Directions**: The Court rejected claims that the trial judge failed to adequately direct the jury on intoxication and self-defence. The directions were considered appropriate and aligned with _Criminal Procedure Act 2009 (Vic)_ and the _Jury Directions Act 2015 (Vic)_.
- **Alternative Verdict**: There was no evidence to support manslaughter (either by provocation or excessive self-defence). Therefore, the judge was not obliged to leave it to the jury.
