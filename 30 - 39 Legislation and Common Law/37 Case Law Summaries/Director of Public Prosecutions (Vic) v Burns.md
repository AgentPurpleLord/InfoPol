# Court Summary
_Criminal Law — Appeal on a question of law — Crown appeal — Whether assertion that ‘undue weight’ given to evidence a proper question of law — Whether open to Magistrate to dismiss the charges — Whether Magistrate bound to convict — Appeal dismissed_

# Appeal Question
“Did the learned Magistrate err in law in dismissing the charges of dangerous driving and exceeding speed limit by placing undue weight upon evidence from Mr Burns as ‘evidence to the contrary’ to rebut the presumption in section 79 of the Road Safety Act 1968 [[sic]] as to proof of the speed of the motor vehicle driven by the accused as detected by the prescribed speed detector?”

# Summary
The case focuses on [[Section 79 Evidence of speed]] and 'evidence to the contrary.' It is unfortunately a difficult case to understand without providing a more detailed account of the case.

# The Case
- On 10 March 2015, police were performing traffic patrol on Warrien Road, Croydon North. They were parked in a 50km/h zone and using a ProLaser III Laser Speed Detector.
- At 5:00pm, the accused was travelling towards them. The doppler audio alerted them that the accused was moving quickly.
- S/C SPURREL activated the ProLaser and got the accused at 106km/h at a distance of 179.1 metres.
- They intercepted the accused and conducted a field interview which was digitally recorded.
- The accused stated: "I wasn't doing that fast male. Look my wife suffers from a, she's had a kidney transplant. I promised her I'd be home at 5 o'clock, and if I was going a little quicker than I should have been, I wasn't doing 106 km/h."

At the contested hearing of the matter, the accused gave evidence:
- The accused stated he drives on that road 20 to 30 times a week.
- The accused said there were 11 to 12 cars in front of him and that he could barely reach the speed limit of 50km/h due to the traffic.
- That a blue Mazda 323 was parked on the road he turned right onto and that it slowed him down.

# The Outcome
The Magistrate weighed both the police officers evidence and the accused. He found both to be reliable and credible. On Appeal, the appellate justice found:
- The Magistrate had noted that the accused admitted he didn't look at his speedometer and had self-interest in denying the charges; and
- The accused's denials of the speed alleged and the state of the traffic were consistent with the statements made to the police on interception; and
- The Magistrate found all parties to be truthful which therefore created some level of reasonable doubt in the case.
