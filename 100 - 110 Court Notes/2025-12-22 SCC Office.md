## Withdrawal Narrative
Charge 1 - Unlawful Assault - W/D
Charge 2 - Wilful Damage - W/D

This matter relates to two parties previously known to each other as friends. 
The accused had previously loaned the victim a sum of $900. Their friendship had since broken down. The accused made numerous attempts to contact the victim to get this money back, but received no replies to texts and calls.

The accused went to the victim's address and spoke to the victim at the front door. The accused asked for his money and the victim told him he could not afford to pay him back. The victim then closed the door. The accused shouted out that he would pay some of his friends to get his money back.

The victim returned to the front door and proceeded to exit the house, grabbing the accused by the collar. The accused grabbed a hold of the victim. A grapple has occurred between the parties, resulting in both of them receiving minor injuries.

The victim's roommate, Mr. MANSARAY, broke the fight up between the pair. He did not see the initial altercation. There may allegedly be a second witness, a female neighbour, but that person was never identified by the attending police members. It was only when the accused raised this in his field interview were they made aware of this person.

Upon review of the body-worn camera evidence, F/C HOLMES BWC captures the victim providing his account to C/ LAM during his statement. The victim says, "I decided now to grab him, so we grab each other" when asked about how the altercation started. The victim also makes admissions to be alcohol affected. The victim makes admissions to biting the accused's hand. 

**Withdrawal Decision**
There is no reasonable prospect of conviction in this matter, due to the following:
- There is a word-on-word (or 'Liberato') issue, in which there would be no way for a jury or Magistrate to believe one account compared to the other; and
- There is no corroborative evidence which could surmount the Liberato issue, in that, both have injuries, there is no forensic evidence at the scene, no secondary witness, etc; and
- There are reliability issues with the victim's evidence, in that he was alcohol affected; and
- The accused can raise self-defence and we have no evidence to rebut this, being that no one else saw what had happened; and
- The accused provided a reasonable account, also explaining unprompted that he had not called police because he did not want to get the victim in trouble for what had occurred.

Authorisation to withdraw from SGT/WATSON.
