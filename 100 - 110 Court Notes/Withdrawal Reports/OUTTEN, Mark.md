Charge 1 - Failing to comply with reporting obligations without a reasonable excuse - Committed on 27 December 2023 and 19 February 2024 (s 46(1) SORA 2004) - Recommend W/D

Matter relates to 40 year-old OUTTEN. On 21 September 2007, OUTTEN was convicted of sexual penetration of a child and placed on the SOR for life. 

On 18 October 2023, OUTTEN attended Werribee Police Station for his annual interview and given a copy of his reporting obligations. On page 2 of 9 of this document, the following is provided: "The names, ages, residential addresses and telephone numbers of any children with whom you have contact."

On 19 February 2024, OUTTEN emailed Westgate SOCIT to report that his sister, Natalie SERGI had a baby. S/C LAPETINA requested the babies information and what contact OUTTEN might have with it.

OUTTEN reported Stella SERGI's (the child) date of birth as 27 December 2023 and reported "I see my sister baby Stella 1 or 2 times each week, either I go to my sister house or my sister come over to my house. No unsupervised contact."

OUTTEN further reported that he first had contact with Stella SERGI on 4 January 2024.

OUTTEN was interviewed on 19 June 2024 in relation to Charge 1. OUTTEN provided:
- He could not recall the first time he had contact with Stella SERGI, but believed it was at the Hospital or his sister's house. OUTTEN said she had said, "Hi" to Stella SERGI; and
- He went to his sister's house twice a week, or she would come to OUTTEN's house with Stella; and
- He holds Stella in his arms for a few minutes during these visits; and
- He suffers from difficulties learning, remembering and understanding things; and
- He was able to explain several of his reporting obligations. 

# Defence Material
Defence have obtained a medico-legal neuropsychology assessment report dated 21 May 2023 from Dr. Evrim MARCH (clinical neuropsychologist). I have read the report and the key take-aways are:
- OUTTEN, when asked to explain his reporting obligations, did not unprompted make reference to reporting contact with any children. 
- OUTTEN falls within the mild intellectual disability range.
- OUTTEN has low average working memory / attention memory and weak mental arithmetic (cannot hold onto basic problems in his mind requiring repetition each time).
- OUTTEN has slower than average processing ability and at times performed the slowest in his agre-group.
- OUTTEN has limited ability to analyse and integrate visual information.
- OUTTEN's verbal memory is poor and in the lowest range. On story-recall tasks he was unable to recall much information. He becomes easily overwhelmed, but has some benefit from repeated information. He is prone to distraction. 
- OUTTEN displayed significant executive dysfunction and rigid thinking style with high rates of impulsivity. 
- At para 64: "While Mr OUTTEN could possibly acknowledge the reporting requirements, when these are raised with him, if left to his devices, he is likely to have difficulty generalising across situations. Even though he has adequate functional day to day memory for routine information, and he may understand a given piece of information, he requires a degree of external monitoring, noting his significant verbal processing difficulties, limited memory for novel information and his rigid and concrete thinking style and cognitive impulsivity."

# Prosecutorial Discretion
This matter **does** have a reasonable prospect of conviction based upon the evidence. 

## Public Interest
This matter **does not** weigh in the favour of the prosecution proceeding on public interest grounds. 
- The *seriousness of the offence* is medium. They are failures to report contact with children. It represents a level of risk, however, in the circumstances (supervised visits), that risk was lowered.
- The *offenders culpability* is low. OUTTEN did not plan to breach his SORA. OUTTEN has participated in what would have been a celebrated family event, the birth of a niece. His sister may have actively encouraged him, as other family might have, to attend the Hospital to meet the new child. Throughout his DVD ROI and Psychological Interview, OUTTEN maintains that he didn't realise he was breaching.
- Further to his *culpability*, the offence was identified by OUTTEN sending an unprompted email to Westgate SOCIT. Police would not be aware of these breaches if OUTTEN had not emailed.
	- This is corroborated by his own self-reporting of updates to vehicles since 2007. Whilst obvious that OUTTEN understands some level of reporting obligations, his culpability in breaching them is clearly low, given he has been self-reporting for a long time. 
- The *offenders background & mental health/disability*, as outlined in the psychological report, supports that OUTTEN has limited cognitive function and might struggle without constant supervision to comply with his reporting obligations.
- OUTTEN has participated in all of his annual reporting interviews, self-reported this matter and fully complied with police throughout it.
- The *victim's attitude* remains unknown. The Informant has made limited contact with the sister, but the sister has dodged police when attempts were made to obtain a statement. 
- There is a strong need for *community protection* in this matter. SORA breaches and the SORA are arguably the orders of the utmost importance to community protection. This factor weighs strongly in favour of prosecution continuing. General deterrence reigns strong as well, however, specific deterrence is diminished by the facts of the matter. OUTTEN self-reported. OUTTEN now understands the needs to report this obligation. There are no further pending charges beyond this matter. i.e. the outcome has been obtained - he is complying with his order.
- The *likely sentence* would be an adjourned undertaking or fine. OUTTEN has a single previous conviction of SORA breach on 2016 which was a complied with adjourned undertaking. OUTTEN also has 5 struck out charges from 2024 of SORA breaches.
	- The withdrawal narrative for 22-B-002465156 relates to the struck-out charges. "Accused charged with 5 x breach of reporting obligations. Obligations include a telephone number he had for 48 hours and email address and 3 x website user names. Accused has significant mental health issues. defence produced a detailed Neuropsych report, which supported a defence under Section 46(2)(b). As such there was little prospect of a successful prosecution. The inft was spoken to about the matter and whilst not agreeing with the decision, understood why the decision was made. Discussions with A/S/Sgt Timberlake at Sunshine pros responsible for the matter, supported the withdrawal"
- Continuing to prosecute this matter would result in a lengthy trial. Expert witnesses would need to give evidence. Victoria Police would need to locate and pay for an expert witness, which would cost considerable money.
	- S/C MCCARROLL has made numerous enquiries from LTDU and SSOPU about obtaining an expert witness to counteract the neuropsych report. This has been fruitless. 
- The question I would conclude on is whether or not OUTTEN represents a risk to the community.
	- The informant is of the opinion that OUTTEN understands his obligations and doesn't put much in place to help himself.
	- I respectfully disagree with the informant. OUTTEN has a troubled childhood and clearly learned to be independent throughout his life. The exact circumstances of these breaches remains unknown and I do not believe it is out of the question that OUTTEN might have been encouraged to visit the newborn by his family. Navigating complex family social relations whilst being on the SOR would be incredibly difficult to do, and given his psychological report, might not be within his means.
