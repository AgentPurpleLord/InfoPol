The accused was charged and bailed for the matter on the same night. A preliminary brief was compiled by the informant and a memo was attached to the front of the brief recommending the withdrawal of the matter. The memo was signed by their correspondence sergeant. 

The memo raised the following points:

The matter was initially reviewed by Prosecutor MCCARROL who requested the informant to determine if the victim was supportive, obtain a statement from the victims husband (first complaint), the victims housemate and the accused's wife. MCCAROLL also asked for phone checks and the 000 call.

The informant replied to the request the following day after making attempts to obtain the statements (which did not pan out) and said he would make attempts to get the 000 call. 

Approximately one month later, Prosecutor HAYMES has reviewed the matter. No 000 call had been obtained. A review of the matter was conducted and an argument outlined to the informant and the authorising Sergeant that the memo was raising points which didn't constitute a withdrawal.

# Withdrawal Reasoning
The argument surrounding who the threat needed to be directed at is not relevant. 
The corroborative evidence (or lack thereof) argument is also not relevant. 

The matter needs to be withdrawn because it is impossible for prosecution to prove that the threat meets the definition of a "sexual offence" as outlined at ss 43(2) of the Crimes Act 1958 (Vic).

The threatening words used by the accused were: "I am going to fuck your vagina with my penis, this way and that way."

ss 43(2)(a) is the relevant provision here. That requires that the prosecution prove beyond a reasonable doubt that the accused makes a treat by words or conduct with the intention of sexually penetrating 'B' or 'C' without their consent.

The issue of whether the threat was made to 'B' (the victim) or 'C' a potential person the accused was on the phone with, doesn't matter. The issue is whether that statement reviewed objectively made it clear that they would commit a sexual offence, e.g. rape or sexual assault. Plainly, it is not an offence to "fuck your vagina with my penis, this way and that way." Crudely put, this could be a persons way of 'dirty talk', whilst strange and inappropriate, it is not necessarily criminal. 

It is on that basis that the matter is to be withdrawn. Prosecution cannot satisfy the elements of the offence beyond a reasonable doubt. 

# Legislation and Explanatory Memorandum
43 Threat to commit a sexual offence 
(1) A person (A) commits an offence if— 
(a) A makes to another person (B) a threat to rape or sexually assault B or a third person (C); and 
(b) A intends that B will believe, or believes that B will probably believe, that A will carry out the threat. 
(2) Words or conduct may constitute a threat for the purposes of subsection (1) if by those words or that conduct an intention to do any of the following is conveyed— 
	(a) to sexually penetrate or sexually touch B or C without B or C's consent;
	(b) to cause B or C, without B or C's consent, to sexually penetrate or sexually touch— 
		(i) A; or 
		(ii) C or B (as the case requires); or 
		(iii) themselves; or 
		(iv) another person; or 
		(v) an animal; 
	(c) to cause B or C, without B or C's consent, to be sexually penetrated or sexually touched by another person or by an animal. 
(3) A person who commits an offence against subsection (1) is liable to level 6 imprisonment (5 years maximum). 
(4) For the purposes of this section, a threat may be made by words or conduct and may be explicit or implicit.

Crimes Amendment (Sexual Offences and Other Matters) Bill 2014
>New section 43—threat to commit a sexual offence 
>New section 43 provides for the offence of threat to commit a sexual offence. 
>New section 43(1) provides that a person (A) commits an offence if—
>- A makes to another person (B) a threat to rape or sexually assault B or a third person (C); and 
>- A intends that B will believe, or believes that B will probably believe, that A will carry out the threat. 
>New section 43 replaces the existing offence of assault with intent to rape in current section 40 of the Crimes Act 1958, which includes threats to assault with intent to commit rape. 
>New section 43(2) provides that words or conduct may constitute a threat for the purposes of subsection (1) if by those words or that conduct an intention to do any of the following is conveyed— 
>- to sexually penetrate or sexually touch B or C without B or C's consent; 
>- to cause B or C, without B or C's consent, to sexually penetrate or sexually touch—
>	- A; or
>	- C or B (as the case requires); or
>	- themselves; or 
>	- another person; or 
>	- an animal. 
>New section 43(3) provides that the maximum penalty for the offence of threat to commit a sexual offence is level 6 imprisonment (5 years maximum).
>New section 43(4) provides that a threat may be made by any conduct and may be explicit or implicit. 
>The note to new section 42 provides that an offence against subsection (1) may be heard and determined summarily by the Magistrates' Court if section 29 of the Criminal Procedure Act 2009 is satisfied.
