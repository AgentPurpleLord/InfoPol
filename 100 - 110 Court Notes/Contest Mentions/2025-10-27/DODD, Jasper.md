# Withdrawal
Charge 1 - Theft of Motor Vehicle - W/D

## Background
This matter relates to an incident where detectives in an unmarked patrol vehicle were in the Point Cook area when they observed an outstanding stolen motor vehicle drive past them. The vehicle was brought to their attention due to a smashed window. 

DSC DE OLIVERIA identified the accused as being seated in the passenger seat as the vehicle passed them. The accused has distinct blue eyes and pointed ears. The detectives attempted to follow the vehicle, but it accelerated to high speeds and they decided not to continue pursuing it. 

The vehicle was seen 800 metres from DODD's normal place of residence.

The accused's lawyer was contacted and he waived his right to an interview.

## Proceedings
At first mention, defence raised identity and knowledge that the vehicle was stolen. A prosecutor subsequently requested for a further statement from DE OLIVERIA to assist in identity. This was provided, but did not adequately outline how DE OLIVERIA had come to know the accused.

The matter was booked to a contest mention on this issue.

## The Issue
Identity in this matter is difficult. It depends upon the identification of 'prior recognition' made by DSC DE OLIVERIA. Depending on the background to ones prior recognition, this evidence can be very strong or inherently weak.

I called and spoke with DSC DE OLIVERIA and her only previous dealings with the accused related to one prior investigation in which he was associated with another accused. There had never been any direct or regular dealings with him.

In conducting research into this subject, I located the following passages from cases:

R v Turnbull [1977] 1 QB 224:
> Recognition may be more reliable than identification of a stranger; but even when the witness is purporting to recognise someone whom he knows, the jury should be reminded that mistakes in recognition of close relatives and friends are sometimes made.

Beckford v The Queen (1993) 97 CR App R 409:
> The need to give the general warning even in recognition cases where the main challenge is to the truthfulness of the witness should be obvious. The first question for the jury is whether the witness is honest. If the answer to that question is yes, the next question is the same as that which must be asked concerning every honest witness who purports to make an identification, namely, is he right or could he be mistaken?

Shand v The Queen [1996] 2 Cr App R 204 (PC) Lord Slynn of Hadley giving the advice of the Board said (at 209):
> The importance in identification cases of giving the _Turnbull_ warning has been frequently stated and it clearly now applies to recognition as well as to pure identification cases. It is, however, accepted that no precise form of words need be used as long as the essential elements of the warning are pointed out to the jury. The cases in which the warning can be entirely dispensed with must be wholly exceptional, even where credibility is the sole line of defence.

In _Langford v The State_ [2005] UKPC 20 the Board advised (at [27]):
> Judges could usefully refer to the reminder given by Lord Lane CJ in R v Bentley [1991] Crim LR 620, that many people have experienced thinking that they had seen someone in the street whom they knew, only to discover that they were wrong. The expression “I could have sworn it was you” is an apposite remark to describe such an honest mistake.

# Decision
This matter does not prove beyond a reasonable doubt the first element of the offence charged, identity. Per the DPP Policy, for a prosecution to proceed, there must be a reasonable prospect of conviction.

It can be said from the case-law authorities, that DSC DE OLIVERIA might be able to give the evidence of "I could have sworn it was DODD", but that ultimately, this would not be proof of identity. 

The only other factor is the proximity to his address. Unfortunately, 800 metres is a wide enough area that it does not add any substantial level of inference of circumstantial evidence to enable us to prove ID.

It is on that basis that I have withdrawn this charge. Authorisation from A/SGT MARSHALL.
