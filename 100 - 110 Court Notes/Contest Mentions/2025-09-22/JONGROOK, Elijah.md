## Background
This matter occurred on 12 April 2024 at 12:30AM. Detectives observed a Mazda 6 with 5 heads on-board in Truganina and attempted to intercept the vehicle. The vehicle evaded the detectives shortly after.

DSC ROBERTSON observed the driver of the vehicle (Chan JOSEPH). Police air-wing picked up the vehicle and proceeded to keep it under observation. 

At time-code 1:11 of the air-wing footage titled 'start', the Mazda 6 pulls overs. The drivers door opens and an occupant exits the vehicle. That occupant uses the rear passenger door to get into the car. The car drives off. No other person can be seen on the footage getting into the drivers seat. 

At time-code 9:00 of the same footage, the Mazda 6 pulls into a car-park. 3x female occupants exit the vehicle from the passenger side doors. The right-hand side passenger door opens and 2x males exit from it a short time later.

The occupants of the vehicle were arrested nearby by attending police. 

## Reasonable Prospect of Conviction
At SCC and CME, Defence raised the issue of identity of the accused. Defence deny that the accused ever drove the vehicle and said that the air-wing footage did not satisfy this element.

Identity is the first element of all criminal charges. In this matter, the argument for identity is put as:

The air-wing footage depicts constant observation of the vehicle throughout the police follow. One driver is observed exiting and going into the back. The car moves shortly after. The car is then parked and 5x people exit the vehicle. 

The last person to exit the vehicle is the accused. The accused exits via the right-hand passenger door. By way of inference, the accused must have been the driver.

Whilst this argument is compelling, my view is that it does not prove identity beyond reasonable doubt. Ultimately, my view is that on the balance of probabilities it is proven, but it does not meet the criminal burden of proof. In order to meet this, we would have needed observations of the driver moving into the backseat by a police officer or air-wing footage. 

## Further Issues re: Charge 2 - Refuse PBT
A refuse PBT offence requires that a lawful request to undergo a PBT is given under s 53 of the Road Safety Act. s 53(1)(a) is the specific provision in this scenario, as the vehicle was not involved in an accident. 

On construction of that legislation, it states that "a police officer may at any time require any person he or she finds driving a motor vehicle or in charge of a motor vehicle."

The key words being "he or she finds." The police officer who observed the person driving must be the person who requested the PBT. 

Maitland v Swinden [2007] VSCA 44 at [8] finds that the power given under s. 53(1)(a) to be wide reaching power for police to PBT a person observed driving. It's noted that the circumstances of how police find the accused driving isn't specified and therefore all that needs to have happened is that police somehow observed the accused driving.


Harvey v Police (SA) (2009) 105 SASR 234; 54 MVR 37; 265 LSJS 353; [2009] SASC 302 at [10], whilst being a SA case, provides that the meaning of a 'driver' or 'driving' is a question of fact and should be judged on the circumstances.

In looking at the facts in this case, there is nothing that could have been so subjectively determining to F/C OWER as to have him form the belief that the accused was driving at the time. The PBT was put on this basis when he wasn't observed. It should have been an EBT demand put on him because he believed he was driving within 3 hours. 

## Conclusion
It is based upon the above reasoning that I do not believe the matter has a reasonable prospect of conviction. The accused has a defence case available to him (that it wasn't him driving) which we cannot rebut with direct evidence. 

I do not believe the inferential evidence in this case is enough to achieve a beyond reasonable doubt conviction.
