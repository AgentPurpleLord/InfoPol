# Charge Breakdown
## Charge 1 - Refuse to Provide Preliminary Oral Fluid Sample Under Section 55D
- The accused is captured on *in-car video* driving the motor vehicle. 
- The accused was required to undergo a *PBT* and did so.
- The accused was required to undergo a *POFT* and refused.
	- The requirement is captured on *body-worn camera*.


> The accused at Wyndham Vale on the 9th of June 2024 within 3 hours of being the driver of a motor vehicle was required to undergo a preliminary oral fluid test by a prescribed device in accordance with Section 55D(1) of the Road Safety Act 1986 and did refuse to provide a sample of oral fluid in accordance with Section 55D.

Road Safety Act 1986 No. 127/86 - Section 49(1)(eb)
**Charges Laid Date:** 09/09/2024

## Legislation
**s 49(1)(eb) RSA**
>(eb) refuses to provide a sample of oral fluid in accordance with section 55D or 55E when required under that section to do so or refuses to comply with any other requirement made under that section; or 

**Section 55D(1)**
>(1) A police officer may at any time require—
>	(c) any person who he or she believes on reasonable grounds has within the last 3 preceding hours driven or been in charge of a motor vehicle when it was involved in an accident; or
> to undergo a preliminary oral fluid test by a prescribed device and, for that purpose, may further require the person, if inside a motor vehicle, to leave the motor vehicle for the purpose of undergoing the test.
>(3) A preliminary oral fluid test must be carried out in accordance with the prescribed procedure.
>(6) A person required to undergo a preliminary oral fluid test must do so by placing the prescribed device, or the collection unit of the device, into his or her mouth and carrying out the physical actions that are necessary to ensure that, in the opinion of the person who, under this section, is requiring the test to be undergone, a sufficient sample of oral fluid has been captured by the device or unit.
>(6A) A person who, under this section, is requiring another person to undergo a preliminary oral fluid test may give any reasonable direction as to the physical actions that are necessary for the person to undergo the test.
>(7) Without limiting section 54(3), a person required to undergo a preliminary oral fluid test is required to remain at the place at which the test is being carried out until the sample of oral fluid provided has been tested by a prescribed device.
>(8) A person is not obliged to undergo a preliminary oral fluid test if more than 3 hours have passed since the person last drove, was an occupant of or was in charge of a motor vehicle
## Elements
The prosecution must prove beyond reasonable doubt:
- the accused is *correctly identified as the alleged offender*; and
- the accused was:
    - a *person believed by the officer on reasonable grounds* to have, within the *last preceding three hours*, driven or been in charge of a motor vehicle when it was involved in an accident; or
- the accused was required to:
    - if they are inside a motor vehicle, leave the motor vehicle for the purpose of the test; and/or
    - *undergo a POFT in accordance with* [s 55D](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U7&docFamilyGuid=I16458ee8045111e99495db3043f758b0&pubNum=1100190&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&docVersion=Law+in+Force&ppcid=8971a9728deb4430b7c121fd25d2e4a0&contextData=\(sc.Keycite\)); and
- the requirement was made within three hours of the accused driving, being in charge of or being the occupant of a motor vehicle; and
- the accused refused to:
    - leave the motor vehicle; or
    - comply with the requirement to undergo a POFT.


# Relevant Authorities
## Formal Requirement
In relation to the words of a requirement, in the below case, the accused was required to provide a blood sample. Two issues were raised on appeal. Whether it was necessary to prove that the informant had made the requirement. And whether it was essential to prove that the officer making the requirement had stated that the requirement was to allowed a registered medical practitioner to take the blood sample.
**Director of Public Prosecutions v Novakovic (2012) 225 A Crim R 468**, at [46]:
"2. The informant was not obliged to use any particular form of words or to make any of the requirements in imperative terms, but he was obliged to give Mr Novakovic reasonably sufficient information to convey what he was being required to do, and why."
