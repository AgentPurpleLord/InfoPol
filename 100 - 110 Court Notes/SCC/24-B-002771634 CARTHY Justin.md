## Incident 1
TFMV (plates) (Charge 12) + HSG (Charge 13 + 14)
**Evidence:**
- CCTV evidence depicts accused at the service station with the plates on his vehicle.
- Accused has distinct facial tattoo's and neck tattoo's that go along his chin. CCTV appears to depict these tattoos; also captured on his custody photographs.
- DVD ROI; accused stated that his friend Sam dropped the Hyundai off at his house. Accused stated he did check the plates and that they flagged as cancelled or something.

**Defence Issues:**
- Defence stating that the accused did not handle the plates and did not appropriate the plates.
	- Appropriation involves the accused *assuming the rights of the owner of the object* AND *adversley interfered with or usurped the owner's right in some way*.
	- It is not necessary to prove the absence of consent of the owner in order to prove theft.
	- Prosecution does not need to prove ALL those rights being assumed; merely ANY of those rights.
	- From Criminal Charge Book (Judicial College) Part 7.5.1, paragraph 15: "1. If the accused came by the property innocently, but later assumed a right to it by keeping or dealing with it as an owner, s/he will have appropriated it (_Crimes Act 1958_ s73(4))." and Paragraph 16: "However, if s/he purchased the property (or any "right or interest" in property) in good faith for value, but received a defective title, his/her assumption of the owner’s rights are deemed not to be theft (_Crimes Act 1958_ s73(5))."
