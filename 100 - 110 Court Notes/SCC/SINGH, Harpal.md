# Background
On 31 October 2024, the accused's vehicle was observed by C/ BRITTAIN and SGT/ APPS driving erratically. The vehicle followed police into a carpark in Hoppers Crossing.

Both police officers observed the accused crawl out of the drivers seat and into the backseat when the car was intercepted. 

As the police officers exited their vehicle, the accused's wife also exited the vehicle. C/ BRITTAIN approaches the rear passenger seat (right-hand side) and locates the accused in the back. The accused adamantly denies he was driving.

SGT APPS requests the accused's drivers licence. The accused asks SGT APPS what power he has to do so. A heated back-and-forth occurs between SGT APPS and the accused. The accused is dragged out of the vehicle, placed in handcuffs and under arrest to verify identity. 

Two minutes later, his identity was confirmed and he was released from custody. C/ BRITTAIN placed a formal PBT demand on the accused, who refused to do it on the basis that he was not driving. 

# Issues Raised
Upon my review of the matter, my belief was that a public interest withdrawal ought be made. This was handed to A/S/SGT FOX to review. A/S/SGT FOX disagrees and believes it is in the public interest to proceed.

Upon further review of the DPP Policy, I also agree. Whilst there are public interest factors (e.g. bringing Victoria Police members into disrepute, etc), these are issues that can be put as mitigating factors to the court in sentencing.

# Outline of the Law / Legal Argument
- The accused is observed driving a motor vehicle by the police officers in question.
- The vehicle was intercepted and stopped. The wife exited the passenger side front door. The accused was observed climbing into the backseat. The accused was the registered owner of the car.
- Under **s 59 of the RSA** *(General duty of driver or person in charge of motor vehicle)*, the accused was obligated to stop the motor vehicle and produce his drivers licence or state his name and address.  
- There is also an argument that the accused failed his **s 60 obligations** *(nominating a driver)* as it's his motor vehicle. 
- SGT APPS can be argued to have used **s 76 of the RSA** *(arrest without warrant).*
	- This provides a power to police to arrest a person for failing to give name and address when they believe the person has committed an offence under **s 42 or s 49 of the RSA.**
	- In this case, SGT APPS can be said to have believed that the accused committed an offence under **s 49(1)(a) or (b).**
- Once the accused provided his name and address, SGT APPS released him from custody as no power of arrest existed any longer.
- The PBT demand was then read by C/ BRITTAIN and the request was put under **s 53 of the RSA** (preliminary breath tests):
	- (1)(a) provides that any person they find in-charge of or driving a motor vehicle can be PBT'd.
- [Tsolacis, George v Kelly, Simon Neish [1997] VicSC 317 (28 July 1997)](https://www.austlii.edu.au/cgi-bin/viewdoc/au/cases/vic/VicSC/1997/317.html?stem=0&synonyms=0&query=tsolacis&nocontext=1) is an unreported judgment that speaks to contemporaneous driving.
	- Essentially put, the demand can be made after the accused has stopped driving where the police observed the accused driving earlier.

Ultimately, it's accepted that when SGT APPS might need to give evidence, he is going to cop significant criticism for his actions. However, the law is on the prosecution side regarding the matter.
