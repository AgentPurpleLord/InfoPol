Regarding Dangerous Article (Charge 5):
1. The offence occurred at the place and time alleged.
2. The offender was the accused.
3. The accused possessed or carried a dangerous article.
4. The accused was in a public place.
5. The accused did not have a lawful excuse to possess or carry the dangerous article.

>Dangerous article is defined in Control of Weapons Act 1990 s 3(1) to mean:
>(a) an article which has been adapted or modified so as to be capable of being used as a weapon; or
>(b) any other article which is carried with the intention of being used as a weapon ...

>An item that is not ordinarily considered a weapon is not a dangerous article unless the accused had it with them with the intention of using it as a weapon: Deing v Tarola [1993] 2 VR 163.

- The accused made admissions to going over to the victim's house **with the shovel**.
- The accused **used the shovel as a weapon which resulted in a minor laceration to the victims chin**. 
- The accused **did not provide any lawful excuse**, which per COW s 7(2) are:
	- (a) the pursuit of any lawful employment, duty or activity; and
	- (b) participation in any lawful sport, recreation or entertainment; and
	- (c) the legitimate collection, display or exhibition of the article; and
	- (d) the use of the article for the purpose for which it is designed or intended ...


# Withdrawal Narrative
Matter relates to 
1. RCI - WD
2. Assault with a weapon - PG
3. Threat to damage property - PG
4. Threat to kill - WD
5. Carry dangerous article - PG

Dispute between neighbours. Victim statement is vague on details in lead up to the argument. Appears that the victim and the accused ended up in the street having a verbal argument. Both have armed themselves with shovels. 

They have then engaged what can only be described as an old-fashioned duel with shovels. The victim came off worse for wear when one of his defending blows has parried the accused's shovel directly into his chin, causing a small bruise / laceration to the victim's chin. 

The victim's statement does not provide the wording of a threat made in the SOAF. This was therefore removed from the SOAF. Defence agreed to the above PG offer and we proceeded on this basis. 

No reasonable prospect of conviction on the RCI / Threat to Kill charge on this basis. 

Authorisation for W/D from SGT Alexander Turner.
