# Guss v Traffic Camera Office
road safety cameras — certification that prescribed test used
Was the certification sufficient to prove the offence?
Held:
(1) Here where the magistrate, relying on the certificate issued under the Road Safety Act 1986 (Vic), s 83A(1), found the offence (of entering the intersection when a red traffic arrow was showing) proven, the s 83A certificate satisfied the evidentiary requirement that all elements of the camera system were tested and used in the prescribed manner.
(2) Pursuant to the Road Safety (General) Regulations 2009 (Vic), reg 30, prescribed road safety cameras include the (presently relevant) camera system known as the “Gatsometer GTC-GS11”. The reference in the s 83A certificate to the Gatsometer GTC-GS11 was a reference to the “Gatsometer GTC-GS11 camera system”, of which the vehicle detectors were a part.
(3) By inference, the magistrate rejected the appellant's evidence to the contrary of the content of the s 83A certificate (namely that he entered the intersection on a green light). It was open to the magistrate to do so.


# Director of Public Prosecutions v Meade [2011] VSC 358
9 [Section 83A of the Road Safety Act 1986](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U5&docFamilyGuid=I167f630a045111e99495db3043f758b0&pubNum=1100190&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&docVersion=Law+in+Force&ppcid=10e59a75227a435bb2332e238779085c&contextData=\(sc.DocLink\)) forms part of the owner onus provisions of the Act [6](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U2&serNum=2031616471&pubNum=0006090&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&ppcid=8be444734e4741608c45c6811ea10679&contextData=\(sc.Keycite\)&comp=wlau#co_footnote_938832~FULLTEXT~FTNT.!6) and, as such, one of the purposes of the section is to streamline the prosecution of speeding offences; reliance upon the certificate obviating the need to call witnesses in every case. [7](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U2&serNum=2031616471&pubNum=0006090&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&ppcid=8be444734e4741608c45c6811ea10679&contextData=\(sc.Keycite\)&comp=wlau#co_footnote_938832~FULLTEXT~FTNT.!7) To adopt the learned Magistrate's approach would clearly frustrate and contradict the legislative purpose. [8](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U2&serNum=2031616471&pubNum=0006090&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&ppcid=8be444734e4741608c45c6811ea10679&contextData=\(sc.Keycite\)&comp=wlau#co_footnote_938832~FULLTEXT~FTNT.!8) The section does not require the authorisation of the person issuing the certificate to be established independently, that is, as a pre-condition to the admissibility of the certificate. To do so would be to deny the meaning of the word “purporting”. The section does not require the person to be actually authorised, only purporting to be so, but read in conjunction with [s 83A(2)](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U7&docFamilyGuid=I167f630a045111e99495db3043f758b0&pubNum=1100190&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&docVersion=Law+in+Force&ppcid=10e59a75227a435bb2332e238779085c&contextData=\(sc.DocLink\)) effects no injustice. The section provides that in the absence of evidence to the contrary, the certificate is evidence of the matters contained therein. It would still be possible to call evidence challenging matters in the certificate and the authorisation of the person certifying, but that is quite distinct from the prosecution having to prove the authorisation of the person certifying in order to prove the offence. It follows, by reason of the above, that the answer to the question posed on the appeal is no.

# Director of Public Prosecution v Juchnowski (2008) 185 A Crim R 66
Held: (allowing the appeal) The magistrate relied on Redlich J's statement in [Ciorra v Cole (2004) 150 A Crim R 189 at [80]](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U2&serNum=2005468879&pubNum=0005341&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&ppcid=a5f9364b1b034dd9b99836b20dd27bc2&contextData=\(sc.DocLink\)) that “[t]he basis upon which the speed limit is to be determined need not be set out in the charge. Though it be a fact necessary to be proved by the prosecution” to find that, although the prosecutor tendered a photograph showing the speed alleged and the speed limit, it did not go far enough to show how the speed limit was to be proved. The learned magistrate erred because this statement in [Ciorra](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U4&serNum=2005468879&pubNum=0006272&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&ppcid=a5f9364b1b034dd9b99836b20dd27bc2&contextData=\(sc.DocLink\)) only applied to situations where the prosecution adduced oral evidence where there was no reliance on photo images. The structure of [s 81](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U7&docFamilyGuid=I167f637e045111e99495db3043f758b0&pubNum=1100190&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&docVersion=Law+in+Force&ppcid=a5f9364b1b034dd9b99836b20dd27bc2&contextData=\(sc.DocLink\)) and its nature as a facilitative provision strongly suggests a legislative intent that [s 81(2)](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U7&docFamilyGuid=I167f637e045111e99495db3043f758b0&pubNum=1100190&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&docVersion=Law+in+Force&ppcid=a5f9364b1b034dd9b99836b20dd27bc2&contextData=\(sc.DocLink\)) provide a complete means of proving an applicable speed limit, without the need to prove the basis on which the speed limit is determined. [Section 81(1)](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U7&docFamilyGuid=I167f637e045111e99495db3043f758b0&pubNum=1100190&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&docVersion=Law+in+Force&ppcid=a5f9364b1b034dd9b99836b20dd27bc2&contextData=\(sc.DocLink\)) provides a mechanism for proving the speed, while [s 81(2)](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U7&docFamilyGuid=I167f637e045111e99495db3043f758b0&pubNum=1100190&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&docVersion=Law+in+Force&ppcid=a5f9364b1b034dd9b99836b20dd27bc2&contextData=\(sc.DocLink\)) provides a mechanism for proving the speed limit. Collectively, those subsections provide the means by which two of the essential elements of the offence in r 20 can be proved. It would be odd if, despite the legislative intention appearing from this drafting, it were necessary for a prosecutor to prove as well the basis on which a speed was determined where [s 81(2)](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U7&docFamilyGuid=I167f637e045111e99495db3043f758b0&pubNum=1100190&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&docVersion=Law+in+Force&ppcid=a5f9364b1b034dd9b99836b20dd27bc2&contextData=\(sc.DocLink\)) provides a prima facie means of proving the speed limit. On a proper construction of [s 81(2)](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U7&docFamilyGuid=I167f637e045111e99495db3043f758b0&pubNum=1100190&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&docVersion=Law+in+Force&ppcid=a5f9364b1b034dd9b99836b20dd27bc2&contextData=\(sc.DocLink\)), this additional proof is not required.
