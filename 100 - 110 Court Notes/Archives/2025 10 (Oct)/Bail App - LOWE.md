# Bail Position
**NOT SHOW COMPELLING OR EXCEPTIONAL**
*UNACCEPTABLE RISK ONLY*
> YH, the accused is an unacceptable risk only in this matter. 
> The prosecution submit that the applicant is an unacceptable risk of:
> s 4E(1)(a)(i), *endangering safety/welfare of other persons*: The accused has shown an intense lack of regard for the victims safety by throwing a Molotov cocktail into their carport. This was a weapon prepared ahead of time and the attack was pre-meditated. Prosecution submits that there is an appreciable risk from the nature of the accused's conduct, that if released on bail, he would commit further attacks of a similar nature. These types of attack truly hold the potential to kill someone. 
> s 4E(1)(a)(iii), *interfere with witness*: The accused is a former partner of the victim. They are vulnerable and known to the offender. This attack was at their safest place, their home. Prosecution submits that there is an appreciable risk that the accused, if granted bail, would interfere with the witnesses by contacting or communicating with them, or by committing a further attack on them.
> s 4E(1)(a)(iv), *fail to surrender into custody*: The accused has previously had 20 warrants dating back to 2015 (10 years). 3 of these warrants were for failing to answer bail. 5 relate to contravening CCO's. The accused is also a high-risk FV respondent, as assessed by Caroline Springs FVIU. A combination or head sentence is well within range for this offending. Prosecution submits that the accused has an appreciable risk of not surrounding into custody due to the pending outcome of this matter, due to the likely outcome being imprisonment of some form. 


# Arguments against defence
- Any delay would be ameliorated by the time served idea, the likely sentence here would be imprisonment.
- Any argument regarding drugs being the main reason behind the offending, the offending is pre-meditated involving essentially an explosive weapon. How can someone have the right mind to prepare a Molotov cocktail, but not the right mind to remember what happened?
- If there is no mental health report before the court, or evidence of mental health diagnosis, it shouldn't be given heavy weight.


# Why no show compelling!?
The informant has laid a charge under s 197(1). Schedule 2 of the Bail Act 1977 requires the charge to be for s 197(2) and for the conduct to have the 'intent to endanger life'. 

This won't be able to be proven, the nominal has confirmed this. Therefore, no Schedule 2 offending.
