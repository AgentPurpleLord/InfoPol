# Reasonable Prospect of Conviction

## The Evidence
9. Police obtained statements from the three victims. Across their three accounts, I have identified the following issues:
	1. All three victims had drank 8 standards drinks at the party and an unknown quantity of alcohol before the party.
	2. KEEP provides no background to the fight beginning. From other accounts that night. KEEP appears to have potentially instigated the initial fight.
	3. MESTO provides that an argument about "touching up girls at the party" occurred before the fight broke out. After this, MESTO saw KEEP fighting and went to help him.
	4. MESTO is the only victim to provide a description of the offender; 185cm tall, islanders.  
10. Police obtained statements from a significant number of people who had attended the party. These do not provide any evidentiary value. 
11. Police obtained statements from Jason JURY and Amanda JURY, who lived across the road.
	1. Jason provides that at 11:35pm, he heard yelling outside and went to investigate. He went outside and stood behind his car in the driveway. He observed 20 people on the road fighting. 
	2. Jason goes on to give an account of a white male getting knocked out and having his head stomped on by a male he describes as: Islander descent, white tank-top, 18-19 years old, short, solid build. 
	3. Jason went back inside once police arrived 10-15 minutes later. Police approached him another 15 minutes later, at which point he points out FINAU to police. Police arrest FINAU.
	4. Jason is confident in his identification of the offender, but says that he cannot remember the offenders face. 
	5. Amanda provides that she also heard the commotion at 11:30pm and called out for Jason. She went outside as well. 
	6. Amanda observed a Australian male provoking others and states that "he was affected by drugs of some sort because he seemed irrational."
	7. Amanda saw the Islander male continuously return to the male lying in the street to stomp on his head.

## Discussion of reasonable prospect of conviction factors
20. The reliability of the witness testimony will be stale given the offence occurred 10 years ago.
21. Further issues exist with the reliability and credibility of the victims, given how alcohol affected they were at the time.
22. No direct evidence of identification exists in this matter. The witnesses and victims did not know FINAU personally. None of them participated in a photo board. None of them can describe FINAU's face. They can only describe his clothing.
23. The informant has confirmed the following witnesses would attend court:
	1. Yousef MESTO
	2. Jacques LANGLOIS
	3. Alexander WILSON
	4. Jarrod SMITH
	5. Amanda JURY
	6. Jason JURY
24. I conducted enquiries into Lachlan KEEP. He appears to have moved to Queensland and has a growing criminal history in that state. I do not believe he would be an compellable witness.
25. Without KEEP as a witness, his charges cannot proceed. The question then turns to MESTO as a victim in the matter.
26. The prosecution case is a circumstantial one. Circumstantial cases in criminal matters must utilise facts that do not directly implicate the offender in an offence to infer that there could be no other alternative but for that offence to have occurred.
27. The strongest piece of evidence we have is the shoe print. MESTO's face has that shoe print bruised upon his face. Presented with the account of Amanda and Jason JURY, the reasonable prospect of conviction would be decent.
28. However, I believe the following issues exist with this circumstantial case:
	1. The shoe prints were never forensically compared and matched to the bruises on the face.
	2. The shoes were never tested for DNA.
	3. The Nike Cortez Runner was a popular shoe in 2016.
	4. Amanda and Jason JURY did not maintain constant observation of the accused in a crowd of 30-50 youths until the time of his arrest. 
29. The question I would then turn to, is that of inferences from circumstantial evidence.

In [_Shepherd v The Queen_ (1990) 170 CLR 573; 51 A Crim R 181; 97 ALR 161](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U2&serNum=1990308475&pubNum=0003586&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&ppcid=006b97db933b465e880d0e0eec9953b9&contextData=\(sc.Default\)) Deane J, giving the leading judgment, said (at 578; 163–164; 183–184):

> The learned trial judge gave the customary direction that, where the jury relied upon circumstantial evidence, guilt should not only be a rational inference but should be the only rational inference that could be drawn from the circumstances.

30. Other reasonable hypothesis might be:
	1. Could someone have changed with the accused?
	2. Could another person have been wearing the same style of shoes?
	3. Could Jason and Amanda JURY have pointed out the wrong person to police?
31. My opinion is that there is no reasonable prospect of conviction for this matter.
