# Issues
Accused is disputing the alleged speeds. Is represented by expensive barrister regarding this. 
- Informant has failed to comply with disclosure, however, satisfied to proceed to a contested hearing based upon what we have and call the informant as a witness.

## Issues at CME
- Defence have raised that the charge-sheets are invalid due to making reference to a statutory provision which is now repealed.
	- The identified error on the charge sheet is that the statutory provision is RR 20(3), when it should be RR 20(1); and
	- The charge wordings include, "which is not a heavy vehicle" and it shouldn't be there.

## The Charge Issue
My reading of DPP v Fox is that this amendment would be allowed. This is explored from [32] - [45]. The proposed amendments being:
1. Change RR 20(3) to RR 20(1) on Charge 1 and 2; and
2. Remove the wording "which is not a heavy vehicle" on Charge 1 and 2.

The argument would be:
- The charge-sheet itself sufficiently discloses the nature of the offence; and
- The wrong road rule being cited is a typographical error by the informant; and
- The additional wording within the charges is a typographical error by the informant; and
- The accused was served with a preliminary brief which sufficiently outlined the offence that had occurred - speeding 45km/h or more over the speed limit.

It is conceded that we are outside of the limitation period for this offence. We must therefore satisfy s 8(3) and s 8(4).
- This amendment doesn't amount to a new offence, as the offence which has been referred to incorrectly was effectively the same offence. 
- The original wording of RR 20(3) was: 
  *(3) A driver of a vehicle other than a heavy vehicle must not drive at a speed which exceeds the speed-limit applying to the driver for the length of road where the driver is driving by 45 kilometres per hour or more*
- The current wording of RR 20(1) is:
  (1) A driver must not drive at a speed over the speed-limit applying to the driver for the length of road where the driver is driving. (c) for a driver of a vehicle other than a heavy vehicle that exceeds the speed-limit by 45 kilometres per hour or more—
- As expressed in DPP v Fox at [45], the incorrect RR 20(3) provision and the charge wording from the brief correctly identify an offence known to law - speeding. 
- There is no injustice in this case. The amendments are to make the current charges accurate to legislation, a case prepared for this charge is effectively identical to a case prepared for the incorrect provision.

# Speed Limit Sign
Pelligra v Forbes [2024] VSCA 242
28
After reviewing relevant authorities, the judge observed that the magistrate was correct to regard the unchallenged evidence of the respondent as establishing that a 100 kilometre per hour speed limit applied. The evidence established that it was a sign posted speed sign, gave the location of the relevant signs and established the speed limit on the signs. The judge did not consider that it was a necessary element for the prosecution to establish that the particular signs were under [r 21 of the Road Safety Road Rules](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U7&docFamilyGuid=I0d11909104fe11e9a1c3f5499e2cc758&pubNum=1100190&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&docVersion=Law+in+Force&ppcid=f6fab45b9a9a4ddcb6cef9ccb9a6dd39&contextData=\(sc.Default\)).[14](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Document/I5a66b1d08cdf11efaf09a53406c15bac/View/FullText.html?originationContext=typeAhead&transitionType=Default&contextData=%28sc.Default%29&comp=wlau#co_footnote_1660163~FULLTEXT~FTNT.!14) Rather, in the context of the respondent's evidence, it was open to the magistrate to infer that the references to the 100 kilometre signposts were references to physically-compliant signs.
