# Withdrawal Narrative
Charge 1 - Criminal Damage - W/D
Charge 2 - Possess Controlled Weapon - W/D
Charge 3 - Possess Heroin - PG
Charge 4 - Unlicensed Driving - PG

Matter is a family violence incident. Accused and victim had dispute. Victim left address and locked out accused from address. Accused seen by neighbour and on CCTV utilising axe in the garden to damage front door. Police attending and observed accused driving motor vehicle. Police searched motor vehicle and located axe and drugs inside vehicle. Drugs were TruNarc tested, positive for heroin. Accused made a full no comment interview, but some level of admissions to informant/corroborator in the interview room prior to recording (unknown if these ever put on tape to accused).

Informant obtained statement from neighbour and CCTV of the incident. Informant and Corroborator both provided statement. Brief is thoroughly prepared with high quality investigation.

Victim provided a statement of no complaint on 06/01/2025 to police. Victim specifically states that she gave consent to the accused to damage the front door.

Regarding Charge 1 (criminal damage), there is an available defence to the accused; that he had consent to damage the front door. Were the statement of no complaint not in existence, this defence wouldn't be satisfied. But after conducting research, where the accused specifically has consent provided to them and we have evidence of this, the defence elements are satisfied. Therefore Charge 1 does not have a reasonable prospect of conviction.

Regarding Charge 2 (Possess Controlled Weapon), the charge is not accurate to the offence. An axe is not considered a controlled weapon under the Control of Weapons Act. It would therefore be considered a 'dangerous article'. Dangerous article requires a 'public place' as an element; the front of a persons house, specifically the front door area would not be considered a 'public place'. Therefore Charge 2 does not have a reasonable prospect of conviction.

The remaining charges (3 & 4) are proceeding as a plea of guilty. The charge elements for each of these charges is satisfied.
