## [39.110] Explanation of Notice
Explanation of notice

The requirements for and contents of explanations about safety notices are specified in [_Family Violence Protection Act 2008_ s 35](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U5&docFamilyGuid=I118b64d4045511e99495db3043f758b0&pubNum=1100190&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&docVersion=Law+in+Force&ppcid=59a834b721bb4325878399c1b55bbc34&contextData=(sc.Search)).

There is no provision in the legislation for proof of an explanation by certificate or similar, so oral evidence is ordinarily required. The abrogation of rules of evidence in [s 65](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U7&docFamilyGuid=I11996e9e045511e99495db3043f758b0&pubNum=1100190&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&docVersion=Law+in+Force&ppcid=59a834b721bb4325878399c1b55bbc34&contextData=(sc.Search)) applies only to proceedings for applications for intervention orders, and so does not dispense with the rules of evidence for a criminal prosecution of contravening a current order.

[Section 35(4)](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U7&docFamilyGuid=I118b64d4045511e99495db3043f758b0&pubNum=1100190&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&docVersion=Law+in+Force&ppcid=59a834b721bb4325878399c1b55bbc34&contextData=(sc.Search)) provides that a failure by a police officer to explain a safety notice does not invalidate that notice. The apparent resulting conflict between [s 35(4)](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U7&docFamilyGuid=I118b64d4045511e99495db3043f758b0&pubNum=1100190&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&docVersion=Law+in+Force&ppcid=59a834b721bb4325878399c1b55bbc34&contextData=(sc.Search)) and [s 37(1)(b)](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U7&docFamilyGuid=I118b64f1045511e99495db3043f758b0&pubNum=1100190&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&docVersion=Law+in+Force&ppcid=59a834b721bb4325878399c1b55bbc34&contextData=(sc.Search)) is probably resolved by concluding that an unexplained notice would validly allow police to arrest and detain a person under [s 38](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U7&docFamilyGuid=I118b64b8045511e99495db3043f758b0&pubNum=1100190&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&docVersion=Law+in+Force&ppcid=59a834b721bb4325878399c1b55bbc34&contextData=(sc.Search)) for allegedly contravening the notice, but would be fatal to a criminal prosecution contrary to [s 37](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U7&docFamilyGuid=I118b64f1045511e99495db3043f758b0&pubNum=1100190&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&docVersion=Law+in+Force&ppcid=59a834b721bb4325878399c1b55bbc34&contextData=(sc.Search)) or [s 37A](https://anzlaw.thomsonreuters.com/Link/Document/FullText?refType=U7&docFamilyGuid=I118b6517045511e99495db3043f758b0&pubNum=1100190&originationContext=document&transitionType=DocumentItem&docVersion=Law+in+Force&ppcid=59a834b721bb4325878399c1b55bbc34&contextData=(sc.Search)) for that alleged contravention. That is, the notice would provide protection for the protected person, but not allow for a successful criminal prosecution.
