>[!WARNING] Vulnerable Persons
>Speaking to the accused and determining their vulnerabilities/including them on your application is often overlooked. These vulnerabilities affect their bail position. You **must** make note of them on the brief.
## Vulnerabilities
### Children
- If the person is under the age of 18, they are a child. 
- The matter should be heard in the Children's' Court (during court hours). BARC for after hours.
- There are further #surrounding-circumstances which the Magistrate must consider, see [[70 - 79 Research/30 - 39 Theory/33 Legislation/Bail Act 1977/Part 1 - Preliminary/Part 1 - Preliminary#^8730b3|Section 3B of the Bail Act 1977]].

### Aboriginal & Torres Strait Islander Persons
- The person's aboriginality is not for us to determine, if they say they are Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander, you must take it at face value.
- There are further #surrounding-circumstances which the Magistrate must consider see [[70 - 79 Research/30 - 39 Theory/33 Legislation/Bail Act 1977/Part 1 - Preliminary/Part 1 - Preliminary#^97219e|Section 3A of the Bail Act 1977]].

### Vulnerable Adults
>[!TIP] Often overlooked-
>Mental, cognitive and physical impairments are often overlooked and not noted on bail and remand applications.

- A person is considered a vulnerable adult ([Section 3AAAA of the Bail Act 1977](http://www6.austlii.edu.au/cgi-bin/viewdoc/au/legis/vic/consol_act/ba197741/s3aaaa.html))  if they have:
	- A #mental-impairment; or
	- A #cognitive-impairment; or
	- a #phyiscal-impairment; that
	- causes the person to have **difficulty** in **understanding** their rights; or making a **decision**; or **communicating** a decision. 
- Examples of #mental-impairment include; anxiety disorders, depression, schizophrenia, bipolar disorder, OCD, PTSD, etc.
- Examples of #cognitive-impairment include; Alzheimer's, ADHD, dementia, acquired brain injuries, learning disabilities, etc.
- Examples of #physical-impairment include; vision impairments, epilepsy, hearing impairments, amputation, etc.

## Services for the Accused
Based on the person's potential vulnerabilities, they may have access to services that will support them being granted bail. It is important for the prosecution to present a fair case, meaning to provide the person every opportunity to be granted bail. 
- DFFH / Youth Justice for #child
- VALS for #aboriginal-person 
- NDIS / Support Carers for #vulnerable-adult 

## Personal Circumstances

^553b13

>[!Tip]- A five minute conversation saves twenty+ minutes in court
>Take five minutes to ask the person about their history. If they don't tell you that's fine. But saving time in court makes everyone's day quicker.

I recommend asking the person about the following topics, which can be included in the personal circumstances box in the bail and remand application:
- **Employment** + source of **income**
	- What do they do for work?
	- Where do they get their income from & how much do they get?
- Residential **address** + **stability** of this address
	- Where do they live?
	- Who else lives with them?
	- Is it a rental property or owned by someone?
- **Relationships** + **Dependents** (children or caring for parents)
	- Do they care for any children?
	- Do they have sick or elderly people they look after?
	- Are they in a relationship or being actively supported by family?
- **Associates** (co-offenders?) + **lifestyle** (what do they do all day when they aren't working?)
	- What do they normally do each day? Who do they spend time with?
	- What are they doing when they aren't working?
- Drugs + Alcohol **dependency** (how much, how often, what kind)
	- How much alcohol do they drink per day?
	- What kind of drugs do they take, or have they taken in the past?
- Gambling habits (if applicable)
- Ties to interstate (family/friends) or **flight risks**?
